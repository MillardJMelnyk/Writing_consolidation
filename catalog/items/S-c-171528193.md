I’ve been studying theories—philosophical, scientific, religious, political, economic, psychological, sociological, you name it—all my literate life, part of my obsession with understanding *everything*.

I always bend over backwards to save the baby from getting dumped with the bathwater, even if it means hanging on to a bit of murk. Pitting theories against each other never made sense to me. Like Mom always said at dinner time: “Eat what you want and leave the rest.”

Mom was a great cook. Our plates only rarely had food left on them. And I only rarely fail to find some great stuff in amongst even the most misguided piles of potato peelings.

The more I look into those staples of “civilization”—the “state”, government, law, morality, and their religious and philosophical underpinnings—the more I realize it all rests on psychosis. Literally. Minds disconnected from reality conjuring paranoid hallucinations of enemies, dangers, and threats that in no wise exist—nor could they possibly exist, in most cases. Minds which then proceed as if these figments were more real than everything actually seen, acting accordingly—always in preemptive, aggressive defense.

Minds locked into *codependence*.

It’s often seemed to me that the minds of codependents are like scenes out of hell, spirits roving around, tormenting them with terrors that have nothing to do with what’s really going on.

Today, though, I realized what those “spirits” actually are.

*Opinions*.

Of all the shit that rolls around in our heads, daunting and demoralizing and defeating us before we ever lift a finger, the opinions of others and the images and narratives they form, by far, predominate.

But not the real opinions themselves—because we, in our paranoia, refuse to take the risk of finding out what they actually are—but opinions we hallucinate and yet feel sure are accurate and real from our positions of relative or even complete ignorance.

Those are the spooks that torment and, to some degree, possess us.
