### Claude · 2026-07-23 14:49

Direct motives, evidenced by his own statements and behavior pattern:

**1. Sovereignty assertion / political theater.** Ben-Gvir has explicitly framed visits in these terms: he says he feels like the "master" of Jerusalem's Temple Mount and vows to keep pushing PM Netanyahu to expand Jewish access there. In May 2026 he went further: "We have restored sovereignty at the Temple Mount through determination and deterrence," pointing to Ramadan as "the quietest," which he attributed to "deterrence." He capped that visit by repeating the 1967 IDF declaration "The Temple Mount is in our hands!" This is not religious observance — it's an explicit claim of political/territorial control, invoking a war-conquest slogan.

**2. Deliberate status-quo erosion.** Since 1967 the arrangement has barred Jewish prayer at the site (Muslim prayer only). Ben-Gvir's visits are designed to break that incrementally: he praised Jewish visitors who prayed at the site "in breach of arrangements that allow only Muslim prayer there". A rabbi accompanying him called this an "enormous historic change... for the entire people of Israel who have been waiting 2,000 years". That's a stated goal of permanent rule-change, not a one-off religious act.

**3. Coalition/base signaling.** These visits are choreographed with Otzma Yehudit party members and timed to nationalist dates (Jerusalem Day, not just holidays). On one such visit he was joined by an MK who calls to "get rid of all the mosques" and reconstruct the Temple, and the two waved an Israeli flag atop the Mount and danced next to the Dome of the Rock. This signals to his far-right base and coalition partners that he's delivering on maximalist territorial/religious claims — a domestic political payoff, independent of any religious calendar.

**4. Provocation with predictable diplomatic cost.** He and allies proceed despite reliable backlash — the visits repeatedly draw condemnation from Jordan and Palestinian officials (Jordan is legal custodian of the site under its 1994 treaty with Israel). Absorbing that cost repeatedly indicates the point is to demonstrate imperviousness to Jordanian/Palestinian objection — power projection, not incidental to worship.

Today's (July 23, 2026) visit fits the pattern: tied to Tisha B'Av, over 1,000 Jewish worshippers including Ben-Gvir at the compound, which he called "tremendous progress." "Progress" is the tell — progress toward a policy objective, not a description of prayer.

Counter-framing for completeness: Ben-Gvir's own public line is that this is about religious freedom for Jews at Judaism's holiest site, not sovereignty politics — that Muslim-only prayer restrictions are themselves the illegitimate discrimination. Whether you find that credible given the "master"/"in our hands" rhetoric is a separate empirical judgment call, but it's the stated counter-position.
