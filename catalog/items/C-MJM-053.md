who first coined the term "civil disobedience"?

### Claude · 2025-06-28 17:31

The term "civil disobedience" was first coined by Henry David Thoreau in his 1849 essay originally titled "Resistance to Civil Government." The essay was later republished in 1866 under the title "Civil Disobedience," which is how we know it today.

Thoreau wrote this essay after spending a night in jail in 1846 for refusing to pay his poll tax in protest of slavery and the Mexican-American War. In the essay, he argued that individuals have a moral responsibility to disobey unjust laws and that people should not permit government to override their conscience.

The concept and Thoreau's articulation of it would later influence major figures like Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr. in their nonviolent resistance movements.

### Millard · 2025-06-28 17:33

What actions of civil disobedience did Thoreau participate in?

### Claude · 2025-06-28 17:33

Thoreau's most famous act of civil disobedience was his refusal to pay his poll tax, which led to him spending a night in jail in July 1846. He refused to pay this tax as a protest against two government policies he considered unjust: slavery and the Mexican-American War (which he viewed as an attempt to extend slavery into new territories).

Thoreau had actually been refusing to pay his poll tax for several years before his arrest, but it was only in 1846 that he was finally jailed for it. According to accounts, he was prepared to stay in jail longer, but someone (likely his aunt) paid the tax for him against his wishes, securing his release.

This experience directly inspired his essay "Resistance to Civil Government" (later titled "Civil Disobedience"), where he wrote his famous line: "Under a government which imprisons any unjustly, the true place for a just man is also a prison."

Beyond tax resistance, Thoreau also participated in the Underground Railroad, helping enslaved people escape to freedom, though he was more discreet about this activity. He also publicly supported John Brown's raid on Harpers Ferry, delivering speeches defending Brown's actions.

Thoreau's approach to civil disobedience was both practical and philosophical - he believed individuals should withdraw their support from unjust government through non-cooperation, even if it meant facing legal consequences.

### Millard · 2025-06-28 17:39

what was the poll tax

### Claude · 2025-06-28 17:39

The poll tax was a fixed tax levied on individuals, typically as a requirement for voting or as a general head tax on all adult males. In Thoreau's case in Massachusetts in the 1840s, it was a tax that every adult male was required to pay to the state.

The poll tax served multiple purposes:

1. **Revenue generation**: It provided income for local and state governments
2. **Voting requirement**: In many places, paying the poll tax was a prerequisite for voting
3. **General civic obligation**: It was considered a basic duty of citizenship

Thoreau objected to paying his poll tax because he viewed it as direct financial support for a government that permitted slavery and was waging what he considered an unjust war against Mexico (the Mexican-American War of 1846-1848). He saw the war as an attempt to expand slavery into new territories.

By refusing to pay this tax, Thoreau was practicing what he called "non-cooperation" with an unjust government. He argued that citizens had a moral obligation to withdraw their support—including financial support—from governments that perpetuated injustice.

The poll tax would later become infamous in American history as a tool of voter suppression, particularly in the post-Reconstruction South, where it was used alongside literacy tests and other measures to prevent African Americans from voting. Poll taxes as a voting requirement were eventually outlawed by the 24th Amendment to the Constitution in 1964.
