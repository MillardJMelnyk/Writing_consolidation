### Claude · 2026-09-24 01:45

42 U.S.C. § 300aa-22(b)(1):

> "No vaccine manufacturer shall be liable in a civil action for damages arising from a vaccine-related injury or death associated with the administration of a vaccine after October 1, 1988, if the injury or death resulted from side effects that were unavoidable even though the vaccine was properly prepared and was accompanied by proper directions and warnings."

### Millard · 2026-09-24 01:46

Supposedly, in the report that accompanied the bill, it was explained that this language is based on comment k from a 1965 statute about unavoidably unsafe products.

### Claude · 2026-09-24 01:47

Correct: House Report 99-908 (1986) states that the committee wrote in comment k's "unavoidably unsafe" principle so that covered vaccines would not face tort liability for unavoidable side effects. Comment k comes from the Restatement (Second) of Torts § 402A (1965), which is a legal treatise published by the American Law Institute, not a statute. The Supreme Court leaned on that same report in *Bruesewitz v. Wyeth* (2011) to rule that the provision bars design-defect claims against vaccine makers.

### Claude · 2026-09-24 01:48

There is no separate clause; the statute never names comment k. The "write in" is the second half of § 300aa-22(b)(1) itself:
