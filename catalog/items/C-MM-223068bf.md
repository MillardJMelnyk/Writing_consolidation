### Claude · 2026-08-12 15:18

**A2 Flyers Speaking** — 7–9 minutes, one child alone with the examiner, scored up to 5 shields.

* **Part 1 – Find the differences:** the examiner greets the child, asks name, family name and age, then shows two similar pictures and asks the child to describe four differences.
* **Part 2 – Information exchange:** child and examiner each hold two similar pictures; the examiner has information about one, the child about the other, and they ask each other questions to fill the gaps.
* **Part 3 – Picture story:** the examiner gives the title and describes the first picture; the child continues the story across the remaining pictures.
* **Part 4 – Personal questions:** short answers about themselves, family and friends, home, school, free time, likes and dislikes — mostly present tense, but past, present perfect and "going to" can come up.

### Claude · 2026-08-12 15:19

**Part 1 – Find the differences**  
The examiner talks, the child answers. Typical lines:  
- "What's your name?" / "What's your family name?" / "How old are you?"  
- "These pictures look the same, but some things are different. I'll tell you about my picture, you tell me about yours."  
- "In my picture the boy is wearing a red hat." → child: "In my picture the boy is wearing a blue hat."  
- If the child freezes: "How many birds are there in your picture?" / "What colour is the door?" / "Where is the cat?"

**Part 2 – Information exchange**  
The child has to *ask* as well as answer. Example prompt cards (e.g. two swimming pools, two parties, two libraries):  
- "What's the name of the pool?" → *Blue Water.*  
- "How old is it?" / "When does it open?" / "How much is it?" / "Where is it?" / "How many people go there?"  
- Then the examiner: "Now you ask me about my pool." Child produces the same question forms.

**Part 3 – Picture story**  
- "These pictures show a story. It's called 'The Lost Kite'. Look at the pictures first. Jim and Sally are in the park. Jim has a new kite. Now you tell the story."  
- Support questions if the child stalls: "What's happening here?" / "Where's the kite now?" / "How does Sally feel?" / "What does Jim do next?"

**Part 4 – Personal questions**  
Family and friends:  
- "Tell me about your family." / "Who do you play with at school?" / "What's your best friend's name?"

Home:  
- "Tell me about your bedroom." / "Do you help at home? What do you do?"

School:  
- "What's your favourite subject? Why?" / "How do you get to school?" / "What do you do at break time?"

Free time and likes:  
- "What do you like doing at the weekend?" / "What sports do you play?" / "What food do you like? What don't you like?"

Past and future (they test these too):  
- "What did you do yesterday after school?" / "Where did you go on holiday last year?" / "What are you going to do this weekend?"

### Claude · 2026-08-12 18:26

Probably yes — earnings below the threshold roll over rather than expire. Classgap's own policy says that if you've earned less than the threshold in a month, they hold your earnings until you reach it. There's no published expiry date on a held balance.

Two things worth knowing:

The threshold may have dropped. Their current FAQ says you need a minimum of 25€ accumulated to receive your first payment, and if it's lower it gets postponed to the following month. If that's live, you might already be over it, or need less than you think.

The real risk isn't expiry, it's the account. After 3-4 years dormant, the concern is whether your profile and payment records are still intact. Log in and check the Payments section of your personal area — Classgap notes that if the total doesn't show in full, you filter by date and the older amounts are still there.

Since you're in Mexico, you'd be paid by PayPal, not SEPA bank transfer. Make sure the PayPal address on file is one you still control.

If the old balance doesn't show up, message support before you start teaching again — get confirmation in writing that the historical earnings are still credited to you.
