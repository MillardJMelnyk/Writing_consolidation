### Claude · 2025-12-14 14:10

I need to verify this claim before answering, as I have no knowledge of a "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" creating such a tax.

The 1% remittance tax (effective January 1, 2026, per the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed July 4, 2025) applies to:

**Transfer types covered:**

* Electronic transfers from individuals in the US to recipients in foreign countries
* For personal, family, or household purposes
* Funded by physical payment instruments: cash, money orders, cashier's checks, prepaid card reloads

**Exemptions:**

* Transfers from financial accounts at institutions subject to the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA)
* Direct withdrawals from bank accounts
* US-issued credit or debit card payments

**Note:** The image you provided is misleading. It says "funded by physical payment methods" but the tax applies to **electronic transfers** that are initiated/funded using physical instruments—not physical transfers themselves.

**Source verification timestamp:** December 14, 2025 (search results from IRS, Wikipedia, industry sources)

### Claude · 2025-12-14 14:12

Depends on how you send it.

**Taxed (1%):**

* Cash, money orders, or cashier's checks at money transfer services (Western Union, MoneyGram, etc.)
* Prepaid card reloads

**Not taxed:**

* Direct bank-to-bank transfer (wire transfer from your US bank account)
* US-issued debit or credit card payment
* Online transfers from your bank account

Sending to your son counts as "personal, family, or household purposes" to a foreign country, so it's within scope. The exemption is based on using regulated financial institution accounts (Bank Secrecy Act compliant), not the relationship or purpose.

### Claude · 2025-12-14 14:13

Venmo doesn't support international transfers. You cannot send money directly from Venmo to a recipient in Mexico.

If you're currently using Venmo, either:

1. Your son has a US Venmo account (meaning the money stays in the US until he transfers it elsewhere), or
2. You're using a different service

If your son is in Mexico with no US account, you'd need:

* **Not taxed:** Bank wire transfer, Wise, PayPal (bank-funded), or other services that pull directly from US bank accounts/cards
* **Taxed:** Western Union, MoneyGram, or similar services if funded with cash/money orders

Clarify: Does your son have a US-based Venmo/bank account, or are you using a different method to get money to him in Mexico?

**Not taxed.**

Wise transfers are exempt. According to Wise's own guidance (July 30, 2025), their transfers won't be subject to the 1% tax because they don't use the payment methods that trigger it.

The international transfer from Wise to your son is funded from your Wise account (a regulated financial institution account), not from cash or physical instruments. The Venmo→Wise step is domestic and irrelevant to the tax.
