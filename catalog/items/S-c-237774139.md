Sorry, Terrance: wrong.

We do not have to create our own culture.

In fact, we'd better fucking STOP creating "cultures".

Culture is the cultification of creativity: the freeze-framing, dehydrate-and-mount it, skin-and-stuff-it result of KILLING creativity and iconizing it as a testament to ego.

Which is why the "greatest artists" tend to be some of the worst, most egotistical mutherfuckers.

Because every single culture, everywhere and at every time, served as an inhibitor and opposer to real creativity -- which every single one of them saw as a threat, as "the enemy".

Of course they did. Because creativity -- real creativity -- ALWAYS INTERFERES, DISRUPTS, AND SPELLS THE DISSOLUTION OF THE KILL-AND-FREEZE DEATH MARCH THAT CULTURE IMPOSES.

Read some fucking history about what cultures ALWAYS do to their own up-and-coming, genuine and genius creators.

Always.

And read what the codependent mentality of cultism that insists on enculturation ALWAYS does to them after they've obliterated the culture they were locked in until they blew it open.

It subsumes their breakout and petrifies it into the next culture.

If your creativity does not threaten the end of the cult of culture, you're a replicator, not a creator. And if you smash the current culture only to make a new one out of your creation, you just become the new cult-maker, same as the old cult-maker.

It's significant that so many artists who detest cult-making and cult-leading end up committing suicide rather than get sucked back into the cesspool.

Yes, all creators begin as replicators -- that's how you master the method.

Method mastery doesn't make you a creator. Morphing the method into a new variant doesn't make you a creator.

Using your mastery to destroy the gaslit illusion that the current culture is the definition, superior, the "best ever" and likely the end-all, be-all...

THAT is creativity.

All the rest is circle-jerking.

> **Quoted note — , 2026-03-11:** [link](https://substack.com/@/note/c-226361311)  
> “We have to create culture. Don’t watch TV, don’t read magazines, don’t even listen to NPR. Create your own roadshow.”
> 
> In 1999, Terence McKenna delivered a lecture called Shamans Among the Machines, offering a sharp critique of modern, mass-consumer culture.
> 
> It’s here that he drops one of his most famous lines: “Culture is not your friend.”
> 
> McKenna argued that mass culture isn’t designed to make us free, creative, or fully conscious, but to condition, distract, and domesticate us.
> 
> It trains people to consume, conform, and identify with manufactured values — status, trends, celebrity, fear — keeping attention fixed outward rather than inward. This erodes imagination and personal autonomy.
> 
> His antidote was simple: withdraw psychic energy from cultural noise and create something yourself — art, ideas, lived experience.
> 
> Creation restores agency. Passive consumption gives it away.
> 
> As he put it:
> 
> “By putting the art pedal to the metal, we maximise our humanness and become much more necessary and incomprehensible to the machines.”
> 
> In another lecture, McKenna sharpened the point:
> 
> “If you’re worrying about Michael Jackson or Bill Clinton or somebody else, then you are disempowered… This is shit-brained thinking. That is all cultural diversion.”
> 
> His message endures because our predicament remains the same. Culture will happily think for you, unless you decide to think, and create, for yourself.
> 
> Source: Shamans Among the Machines (1999), Terence McKenna.
