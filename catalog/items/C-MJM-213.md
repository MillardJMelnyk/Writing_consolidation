### Claude · 2025-08-23 16:05

"Saturated" or "permeated" (past participle already indicates completed state).

No single common English word exists specifically meaning "having been completely permeated." Closest options:

* Saturated (implies fullness)
* Impregnated (suggests thorough infusion)
* Suffused (indicates spreading throughout)
* Pervaded (past state of permeation)

### Millard · 2025-08-23 16:05

OK, then what's wrong with "impermeated"?

### Claude · 2025-08-23 16:05

Nothing is definitively "wrong" with "impermeated."

Issues:

* Not in standard dictionaries
* Redundant prefix (im- + permeated both indicate completion)
* "Permeated" already conveys the completed state
* Won't be recognized by spell checkers
* May confuse readers unfamiliar with the construction

Technical validity: Follows English morphological patterns. Prefix im- (variant of in-) can intensify meaning.

Bottom line: Grammatically defensible but unnecessarily complex and non-standard.
