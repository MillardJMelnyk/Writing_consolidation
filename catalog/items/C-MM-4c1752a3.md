### Claude · 2026-07-04 21:50

Here it is — derived from your file, validated against your file, one rule, any level, any scale.

**The algorithm (final form)**

State = which side of the level you're committed to. A crossing becomes an EVENT only when the opposite-side excursion clears the **measured noise floor** OR the dwell outlasts the **measured noise duration**. Otherwise it's chatter — discarded, no state change. Nothing is smoothed; pivots, reach, cross times all come off the raw ticks. Only the *event decision* is gated.

The two scales, both computed live from the stream, zero constants picked from air:

* **σ₁** = rolling median |tick-to-tick delta| → $0.030 in your file
* **rate** = rolling ticks/second → 12.7 in your file
* **T** = noise duration ceiling. Your file gives it directly: I measured every re-cross interval across a $1 grid of levels — 3,666 crossing episodes. Two populations, no overlap worth mentioning: chatter re-crosses in median **1.0s**, 88% within 10s; real returns come back **minutes to hours** later. T ≈ 10s, and it can itself be rolling (p90 of recent re-cross gaps).
* **A** (amplitude floor) = Z · σ₁ · √(rate · T). That's the distance a pure random walk of *your measured tick noise* wanders past a level within the noise duration. Predicted $0.34–0.47; measured chatter-episode depths: median $0.18, p95 $0.64. Real episodes: median $1.58. Theory and data agree — the separation is real and derivable.

Z is the single declared number, and it is not arbitrary — it's the false-event rate you're willing to tolerate, stated as a probability. Z=2 rejects ~95% of what noise can do. That number *cannot* be derived from data because it's a decision about your tolerance, not a property of gold. Every method that hides this fact is lying about it.

Why OR and not AND: noise is small **and** brief. Big-and-fast = real spike, fires instantly on amplitude. Small-and-sustained = real grind, fires on dwell. Only small-and-brief dies.

Makes sense, and it's the right split — the current version wastefully recalibrates every tick when the numbers barely move minute to minute.

**Part 1 — Calibrator (periodic).** Runs on a timer (say every 1–5 minutes) over the recent window. Reads the raw ticks, produces three frozen numbers: noise-per-tick, tick rate, and the floor computed from them. Also owns the force-recalibrate trigger: it keeps a cheap running check (e.g., short-window noise vs the frozen value), and if current behavior departs from the frozen context by some clear margin — say short-window noise doubles or halves — it fires immediately instead of waiting for the timer. Also fires on session-gap reopen.

**Part 2 — Detector (per tick).** Dumb and fast. Consumes the frozen floor and dwell ceiling, maintains only the state machine: committed side, pending excursion, pending clock. No medians, no windows, no math beyond compare-and-max. This is the part that lives inside OnCalculate at tick rate, and it's a handful of operations per tick per level.

Clean contract between them: Calibrator hands Detector `{floor, dwell_ceiling}` and nothing else; Detector never looks at history. One open decision for you: what happens to a *pending* episode when a forced recalibration lands mid-episode — keep judging it under the old floor, or re-judge under the new. My position: re-judge under the new floor immediately; the new context is the truer one and the episode is at most seconds old.

### Claude · 2026-07-04 22:12

Good feedback. Three plan changes, two already covered:

**1. Adopted — the stall is real and the fix is principled.** Reset was too cheap: one tick back wiped everything, while confirmation cost a full floor. Make the reset cost the same evidence as the confirmation — symmetric dead band. New episode semantics:

* Episode ends (chatter, discard) only when price retreats to the committed side by ≥ the floor — not on a single touch-back.
* Dwell becomes *cumulative time spent on the opposite side* within the episode, not wall-clock since first cross — otherwise the dead-band change would let dwell fire while price mostly sits on the committed side.

The straddle-oscillation case now accumulates evidence instead of losing it, and a genuine rejection (retreat beyond noise) still clears it properly.

**2. Adopted — dual timestamps.** Detector reports both episode-start time (true cross) and confirmation time. Caller backdates to the first, measures latency from the pair.

**3. Adopted — two calibration corrections.** Rate counts only nonzero-delta ticks (consistent with the step-size measurement); window fill uses actual elapsed span, not the nominal 300s, so early-session rate isn't understated.

**4. No change needed** — Calibrator is already one shared instance per tick feed, detectors are per-level and cheap; three per line is the intended usage.

**5. Already handled by the split** — the .mqh Calibrator computes the median only inside `Recalibrate()` (copy-and-sort of the ring, on timer), never per tick. The per-tick path is append-only. No incremental median needed.

One consequence to note: with the symmetric dead band, raw-crossing counts will read lower (touch-backs no longer end/restart episodes), so rejection percentages against the old runs aren't directly comparable — event counts and timing are what should match.

### Claude · 2026-07-07 21:56

From the code:

**Getting blocked.** Three danger rules run every tick, each a "numbers look bad" test — ratio-2 slope too steep combined with the spread moving wrong. Each rule has a counter: bad tick, count up; clean tick, reset to zero. If any counter reaches its limit, that tick scores a "danger" mark. The marks feed a rolling scoreboard over recent ticks. Scoreboard hits its limit → trading shuts off, scoreboards wipe.

**Getting unblocked.** While shut off, a health check runs instead: spread big enough, both slopes pointing the same way as the spread, both slopes strong enough. Healthy ticks feed their own scoreboard. It hits its limit → trading turns back on, everything wipes.

**No crosses anywhere in it.** All size checks, same-direction checks, tick counters, and scoreboards. That's why the detector changes don't touch it.
