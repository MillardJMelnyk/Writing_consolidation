### Claude · 2026-10-03 15:46

Saturday, October 3, 2026, [Time] [Timezone]

Incorrect. Ad hominem is a classic informal fallacy, but only in its argumentative form: rejecting a claim *because of* who asserts it (e.g., "He's a liar, therefore his argument is wrong"). It's a relevance fallacy: the arguer's character is logically irrelevant to the claim's truth.

It is not a fallacy when:

* The insult isn't offered as a rebuttal (pure abuse, no inference drawn).
* The person's credibility is itself the issue (witness testimony, expert-authority claims, conflicts of interest). Attacking reliability there is relevant evidence, not fallacious.

define g in simple terms

### Claude · 2026-10-03 17:49

**g (Hedges' g)** is the gap between two group averages, measured in standard deviations, with a small correction for small sample sizes.

g = (mean of treatment group − mean of control group) ÷ pooled standard deviation

g = 0.5 means the average person in the treatment group scored half a standard deviation above the average person in the control group. For example, if test scores have a standard deviation of 10 points, g = 0.5 is a 5-point gap.

Conventional labels (Cohen's): about 0.2 is small, 0.5 is medium, and 0.8 is large.

Because it is unit-free, it lets studies with different tasks and scoring scales be compared. It is not a percentage of anything, which is why g = 0.476 for mental imagery can't be read as "47.6% of learning."

### Claude · 2026-10-03 17:50

g is a score for how much better one group did than another.

Say one group practices with visualization and another group does nothing. Test both groups. g tells you how far apart their average scores are, using the normal spread of scores as the ruler.

* g = 0.2: small gap. Most of the two groups overlap.
* g = 0.5: medium gap. The typical person in the visualization group beats about 7 in 10 of the other group.
* g = 0.8: big gap. The typical person beats about 8 in 10.

So the 0.476 for visualization means a medium gap versus doing nothing. It does not mean visualization accounts for 47.6% of anything.
