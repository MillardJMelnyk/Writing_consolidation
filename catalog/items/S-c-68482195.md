You are the gods of your child's world. You are invincible, irresistible, all-wise. You are their very standard of morality ***in what you do, not what you say****.*

* How do you *look* when you're “disciplining” them?
* What message is your face giving your child?
* How do you *act*, and by that I mean the *image* and *energy* you impart and imprint on your child's open, trusting heart?
* How do you *sound* when you're “disciplining” them?
* How does your child feel once you've "disciplined" them: safer? More confident? More closely connected to you? Better about themself? Happier? Stronger? More eager to try and do and learn?

I'm contemplating a post called "The Crime of Parental Egotism" because parents FUCKING SUCK at putting themselves in their children's place and seeing and *feeling* from their children's perspectives, and the effect on their children is devastating. Considering the damage done, the pills and booze they’ll consume throughout their lifetime, the trillions they’ll collectively spend on therapy…

And then, even after all that, limping through life pretending they’re running, totally empty when they cross the finish line, having done the same damage to their own children thanks to the example you set…

Explain (I dare you): ***how is that not criminal?***

You’re so wrapped up in your own games, as well-intentioned as they may be, that you’ve no room left to see own child, consumed as you are with your own fantasies/nightmares ***about the child in your pathetic fantasies****.*

Your fantasies and nightmares about your child are *imaginary*. In them, your child is not your child but an *object in your own imagination* which very, very often misses the truth by a mile—the truth of who your child actually is and what’s really going to happen.

Your child is right there, real, right in front of your nose but you cannot see them through the veils on which you project all kinds of nonsense in your heads.

***Fantasizing about your child is not only not the same as empathizing with your child.***

The two aren’t even remotely the same *kind* of thing. They revolve around completely different concerns. Your child is not your fantasy of them. Why should this even need to be stated?

Long before your child can understand that *you are not dealing with them but something else*, long before they can articulate it, *they feel it*.

You have *alienated* them and *they know it.*

They can tell that some other *thing* has possessed your attention, *but it's not them.*

While you’re so obsessed with "being a good parent", you've lost track and forgotten completely the person you're the parent of.
