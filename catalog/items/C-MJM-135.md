What kind of car was the Studebaker hawk

### Claude · 2025-08-06 00:32

The Studebaker Hawk was a two-door pillarless hardtop personal luxury car produced by Studebaker from 1956 to 1964. It was based on the acclaimed Raymond Loewy-designed coupe that first appeared in 1953 as a show car.

The four-model Hawk range launched in 1956, mirrored the engine and trim levels of the sedans, offering various configurations:

* **Flight Hawk** - The base model with a 185.6 cubic inch flathead straight-6 engine
* **Power Hawk** - Mid-level trim
* **Sky Hawk** - Higher trim level
* **Golden Hawk** - The flagship model

The Hawk series went through several iterations over its production run. For 1959, the Silver Hawk became the only Hawk model in production, largely because Studebaker dealers wanted a glamorous flagship model as a dealership draw. Later, the final version, the Gran Turismo Hawk, debuted in 1962.

The Hawk was essentially Studebaker's sporty personal luxury coupe, designed to compete in the growing market for stylish two-door cars. In late 1963, the South Bend, IN, Studebaker factory closed, ending production of the Hawk Series, although remaining examples were sold as 1964 models.

How did it perform and handle

### Claude · 2025-08-06 00:34

The Studebaker Hawk's performance and handling varied significantly depending on the specific model and engine configuration:

**Performance:**
The Golden Hawk with its powerful V8 offered the best performance. Acceleration in the Golden Hawk with its huge torque loaded V8 was astounding, with the best 0-60 time being 7.8 seconds and the worst being 10.0 seconds according to various magazine tests. The supercharged 289 V8 models were particularly quick.

In contrast, the base Flight Hawk was much more modest. Offered only with Studebaker's 101-horsepower 185.6-cubic-inch L-head six, it was fuel-efficient but underpowered. The later GT Hawks with the naturally aspirated 289 V8 fell somewhere in between - they're not fast but should be fine for a cruiser.

**Handling:**
The handling characteristics also varied by model. Interestingly, some critics maintain the Flight Hawk was the best handler in the flock because of its relatively light front end and lack of serious speed potential. The more powerful models suffered from weight distribution issues - handling was a bit less accomplished in the Golden Hawk due to the heavy V8 up front.

However, owners generally praised the Hawks' road manners. The car handled very well and you could get through corners reasonably fast, though the power steering setup had some quirks for city driving. Its brakes were great also according to owner reports.

Overall, the Hawks were considered competent handlers for their era, with the lighter six-cylinder models offering the best balance, while the V8 versions traded some handling finesse for straight-line performance.
