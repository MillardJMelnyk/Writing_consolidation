### Millard · 2025-12-11 13:14

If I said an argument was performative, what would you take that to mean?

### Claude · 2025-12-11 13:14

That the argument is made for effect or display rather than genuine inquiry or persuasion. It serves a social, rhetorical, or self-presentational function—signaling a position, demonstrating allegiance, playing to an audience—rather than an epistemic one aimed at truth or mutual understanding.

### Millard · 2025-12-11 13:16

So the arguer is trying to create the impression of what they're arguing for through the process of making the argument.

### Claude · 2025-12-11 13:16

Yes. The act of arguing performs what the content claims. The process instantiates the conclusion rather than supporting it through evidence or reasoning.

### Millard · 2025-12-11 13:17

That seems like an extremely close match to Harry Frankfurt's definition of bullshit.

### Claude · 2025-12-11 13:17

Correct. Frankfurt's bullshitter is indifferent to truth—concerned with the impression created about themselves, not the accuracy of what they're saying. The performative arguer operates identically: the argument's validity or soundness is beside the point; the point is what arguing it signals or performs.
