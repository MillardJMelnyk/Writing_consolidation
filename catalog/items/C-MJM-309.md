### Claude · 2025-11-14 14:33

I cannot provide a meaningful percentage because this question is unanswerable with empirical rigor.

**Why this fails as a quantifiable question:**

1. **Metric selection problem**: No single metric works across contexts:

   * Body count? Famines kill more than battles, but causation is diffuse
   * Battle deaths? Excludes structural violence, persecution, indirect deaths
   * Years of life lost? Data doesn't exist pre-modern era
   * Responsibility-weighted deaths? Requires impossible counterfactuals
2. **Attribution impossibility**: Most conflicts have multiple causes. Example: The Thirty Years' War (1618-1648, ~8 million deaths) was religious, but also dynastic, territorial, and economic. What percentage is "religion's fault"? 30%? 70%? This is arbitrary.
3. **Data absence**: We have no reliable death tolls for:

   * Most wars before 1500 CE
   * Persecution deaths in most regions/periods
   * Indirect deaths (economic disruption, disease, famine)
   * Non-state violence
4. **Denominations problem**: "Christian church" and "Islam" are not unified actors. The Catholic Church's Crusades differ categorically from Protestant settler colonialism or Ethiopian Orthodox conflicts.
5. **Scope inflation**: "Fault" would require determining:

   * Direct violence by religious authorities
   * Violence by secular rulers using religion as justification
   * Violence in religiously-mixed causation scenarios
   * Counterfactual: would violence have occurred anyway?

**What I can state empirically:**

* Both religions have been invoked to justify large-scale violence
* Scale estimates for major events exist (Crusades: 1-3M, Thirty Years' War: 8M, Inquisitions: tens of thousands, Islamic conquests 7th-8th c: unknown but substantial)
* But converting these to "percentage of all bloodshed" requires denominator data that doesn't exist

Any percentage given would be fabricated. The question assumes commensurability that historical data cannot support.
