I’ve been talking a lot lately about codependency and infantilism. Most people don’t understand what they refer to, mostly because of the perspective they (or the “experts” they listen to) take and what they’re trying to achieve: a behavioristic perspective to identify identifiable patterns of *expression.*

Why that? Because they’re interested for purposes of *profiling*.

That’s not a completely stupid perspective and purpose to have—but it is when it’s the only one you’ve got. It leads to dumb errors. If all you do is profile, you’ll likely come up with the equivalent of calling zebras “white horses with stripes”.

It’s what’s *inside* that matters more.

What’s inside codependency and infantilism?

Cognitive and motivational *principles*.

Defining them by their inner working principles, we get:

codependency : a pact of mutual extortion

infantilism : retention into adulthood of early-childhood-level emotional need for security and support hallmarked by abdicating one’s initiative and judgment to superiors
