### Claude · 2025-07-21 00:20

The saying is "A man's home is his castle" (or "An Englishman's home is his castle" in its original form). This principle means that a person has the right to privacy, security, and control within their own home - essentially that you're the sovereign ruler of your own domestic space.

The phrase originated in English common law, dating back to at least the 17th century, and reflects the legal concept that people should be free from unreasonable searches and intrusions in their homes. It emphasizes both the physical sanctuary that a home provides and the legal protections that surround private property.

In modern usage, it's often invoked when discussing privacy rights, property rights, or simply the idea that your home should be your personal refuge where you can live as you choose.
