In summer 2023, Gaza's roughly 2.2 million residents were 16 years into the Israeli-Egyptian blockade, with unemployment near 45%, about 80% dependent on aid, most tap water undrinkable, and electricity limited to roughly 10–12 hours a day during a brutal heat wave. ### Millard · 2026-10-06 12:43

### Claude · 2026-10-06 12:43

Leaving required permits that most people couldn't get: Israel's Erez crossing was open mainly to about 18,000–20,000 Gazans with work permits, plus approved medical, humanitarian, and business cases, while Egypt's Rafah crossing was open most days but involved registration lists, long waits, and often unofficial "coordination" fees to get through faster. There was no airport or seaport, so those two crossings were the only ways out for people, and goods came in mainly through Israel's Kerem Shalom crossing.
