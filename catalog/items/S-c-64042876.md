I was recently asked, “Given that the authorities keep lying to the people, why do the people keep believing the authorities?”

Well, because when you boil authoritarianism down to its essence and basic structure, it’s incontrovertible: we were all mind-fucked into thinking like cultists.

The foremost aspect of cultism is *infantilism*. Think about it. To believe authorities requires suspending our own better judgment and knowledge in order to adopt the position of a child before them.

Infantilism guarantees *codependence*.

Codependence guarantees *cultism*.

That’s what religious training and “education” are primarily about (and always have been), and that’s why they take so long. University, too. It takes a lotta-lotta work to break down our natural self-confidence and abuse us into relying interminably on others instead of maturing and becoming competent in our own right.

Sure, they taught us stuff, for good or ill—but before they taught us anything worth knowing, and far and away more than they informed us or made us smarter, they *conditioned* us by both the method of teaching itself (Freire’s “banking model of education” <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_model_of_education>, by systemic Pavlovian conditioning (seats all in a row that we might as well have been strapped to, bells to signal “passing periods” and mark “tardies”, regimentation throughout, etc.), and above all by authoritarian punishment and reward.

None of that aimed at bettering us in ways that *we* would agree are better, but rather to *better make us venerate and serve our higher-ups*.

That was the first 15–20 years of our lives, thanks to virtually every authority in our lives, and it gets reinforced incessantly ever afterwards, known as “socialization”. And anyone who resists the conditioning or rebels against it gets stigmatized as “antisocial”, having a “problem with authority”.

The problem isn’t with people who insist on being independent and free—it’s with everyone else who insists that we must all behave like children and submit/defer to and obey our “leaders”.

For my part: fuck that. They’re not my “leaders”. The only leaders I have are people I choose to follow *if and when they demonstrate they’re worth following for a while*, until I’ve learned what I needed from them. Anyone who’s worth following will be more than happy when a follower stops following, because they’re *competent, so they have no need for followers.*

Isn’t that what good parents hope: that their children will leave them, become independent, and eventually *surpass* them? False leaders (they used to be called “false prophets”) have no intention of being left or surpassed. They require interminable codependence, which means interminable infantilism.

How many authorities have you known who demonstrated their follow-worthy competence *before* they demanded that you follow them? I’m talking about parents, too. How many so-called “leaders” who presumed authority over us made sure that the choice to follow them was always squarely ours?

We’ve sunk so low that the response I usually get to that is, “But, how could that work? That would end up being a disaster!”

I just shake my head. Among fools, ignorance is taken as the limit of possibility.

Compare how many real leaders you’ve seen to how many would-be authorities demanded obedience from you—not only before demonstrating any competence that would support their demand, but far more often *despite grave, repeated, demonstrated incompetence*?

Elected leaders, those that they appoint to positions of power, and those that they hire into positions of authority *never* demonstrate their competence to us. At best, they give us promises, and their success rates in keeping those promises rarely rise above the single-digits.

No wonder, because *this is what authority is for in the first place:* it enables the incompetent to demand what only the competent should ever dare demand, completely bypassing every question of competence.

But the competent don’t do that, do they? Think of all the seriously competent people you’ve known. How many of them demanded that you obey them, even if just in their areas of competence? How many demanded that you obey them merely because they occupied a position that legitimized their demand *wholly apart from any and all question of competence?*

Competent people don’t need to demand obedience, because their power lies in *being very good at* ***doing*** *things*—not this fakery they call “power” which is nothing more than *an ability to get others to do for them what* ***they cannot do for themselves.***

Authority is what fools and idiots need in order to control the competence of others, because they’ve got no confidence whatsoever in their ability to do it themselves.
