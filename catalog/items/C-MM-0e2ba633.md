### Claude · 2026-08-10 15:39

The tail is almost always tucked under the outer layer right at one of the white ends — run a fingernail or a pin around the edge where the thread meets the cap and lift; it'll pop free.

If nothing's there, just catch any strand on the outer surface, pull it loose, and cut — you lose a few inches of thread, nothing more.

### Millard · 2026-08-10 15:40

Wrong. I cut a strand and like I said the way it's wrapped, no matter where you cut it, as you pull it free, it goes under another layer.

### Claude · 2026-08-10 15:40

That means you cut into the body, not the end — a cut strand is pinned by every crossing above it, so it'll never free.

Stop pulling and start rotating. Hold the strand loosely and spin the spool so thread comes off the way it went on; if you're pulling against the wrap direction, every turn drags it under a crossover. Try spinning both directions — one will unwind smoothly.

If that still binds, the real end is buried and you peel: pick up whatever loop is genuinely on top, rotate the spool to unwind it, and repeat. You'll shed a few yards of chopped thread before you reach clean continuous line.
