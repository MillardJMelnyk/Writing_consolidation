### Claude · 2025-08-19 16:49

"Self-abnegation of soul" suggests a profound renunciation or denial of one's inner self, spiritual essence, or core identity. The phrase combines two powerful concepts:

**Self-abnegation** typically means the denial or rejection of one's own interests, desires, or identity - often for a perceived greater good or higher purpose.

**Of soul** elevates this beyond mere behavioral change to something that touches the deepest part of a person's being - their spiritual core, essential nature, or what makes them fundamentally who they are.

Depending on context, this phrase could mean:

In a **religious or spiritual** sense, it might refer to the mystical practice of ego death or surrendering one's individual will to divine will - the kind of self-emptying found in certain contemplative traditions.

In a **psychological** context, it could describe a destructive process of self-erasure, where someone systematically denies or suppresses their authentic self, perhaps through trauma, oppression, or extreme self-criticism.

In **philosophical** terms, it might relate to existentialist concepts about the negation of authentic being, or the way social forces can lead people to abandon their true nature.

The phrase has a somewhat ominous quality - "abnegation" carries connotations of loss or sacrifice that go beyond healthy self-discipline or spiritual practice into potentially harmful territory. It suggests not just setting aside personal desires, but actually rejecting or destroying something fundamental about one's identity or spiritual nature.
