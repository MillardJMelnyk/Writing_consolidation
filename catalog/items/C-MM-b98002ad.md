The correct answer is **the Book of the Covenant** — specifically, the ***Mäshäfä Kidan*** (Book of the Testament/Covenant), and more precisely the text known as **"The First and Second Books of the Covenant"** which record dialogues between the risen Christ and the apostles.

The most specifically cited text for this is ***Lefafa Tsedek*** (Bandlet of Righteousness) or — most accurately — the ***Qalementos*** (Ethiopian Clement), which presents itself as teachings Jesus gave between resurrection and ascension.

The single clearest answer: **the *Qalementos*** (Ethiopic Clement), which is part of the Ethiopian Orthodox biblical canon and purports to record Jesus's post-resurrection instructions to the apostles before the Ascension.

### Claude · 2026-05-17 13:35

The **Book of the Covenant** (*Mäṣḥafä Kidan*), also known as the **Testament of Our Lord Jesus Christ**. It is a two-part work presenting itself as containing the teachings of Jesus to the apostles during the 40 days between his resurrection and ascension, and is recognized as two books in the broader New Testament canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church.

### Claude · 2026-05-17 13:36

Two options:

1. **Free online** — the First and Second Books of the Covenant (translated from the Syriac, with notes on the Ethiopian version) can be viewed free online. The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project lists it at **ethiopianorthodoxbible.wordpress.com** with a direct link to the free version.
2. **Purchase** — the best English edition, described as translating the two Ethiopic books of the Covenant (*Testamentum Domini* and *Epistula Apostolorum*), is available to purchase in print.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project page at **ethiopianorthodoxbible.wordpress.com/2025/09/17/a-complete-list-of-all-the-ethiopian-orthodox-bible-books-already-in-english** is your best starting point — it has direct links to both the free online version and the print purchase option.
