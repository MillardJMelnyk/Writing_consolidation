Fact: You can't listen well while you're making words.

Fact: If you don't hear, you can't understand.

Fact: If you don't understand, you've got no business making words.

I learned early in life not to trust talkers -- meaning those who take up a disproportionate amount of the time available for each person to speak.

First, they're inconsiderate.

Second, they're supremacist, elevating the value of their words over that of everyone else present.

Third, they're hypocrites, because while emitting sounds that seem meaningful, they're drowning out meaningful speech that they consider inferior to their own.

Fourth, and maybe most important: their interest really has nothing to do with the value of their speech, because it's designed to evoke reactions by which they can gauge their interests against their read on the other present. It's a predatory tactic.

No, they're not innocent. If they're unaware of what they're doing and don't intend it, at some point they would have wizened up and fixed their habit.

Cultures of extroverts are cultures of liars, because having something worth saying results from shitloads of listening, not talking.

As early as high school, having mostly listened (I've always been kinda shy), I got the firm impression that talkers are trying to keep others away from them, not connect to them.

If they'd truly wanted to connect, they'd first have listened to the people they wanted to connect to, not firehosed them.
