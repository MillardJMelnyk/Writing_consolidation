### Claude · 2026-06-09 21:42

The short answer: **two camps, no universal rule, but clear patterns by context.**

**Title case** (Capitalize All Major Words) — traditional, still dominant for:  
- Book titles (essentially universal)  
- Chapter titles in books  
- Academic writing (Chicago, APA, MLA all use it, with minor differences)  
- Journalism headlines (AP style, though AP uses sentence case for actual news headlines — see below)  
- Most Substack newsletters aiming for a polished, authoritative feel

**Sentence case** (Only capitalize first word + proper nouns) — rising, especially for:  
- Tech and SaaS blogs  
- Casual/conversational newsletters  
- Many Medium posts  
- Google's own product interfaces and documentation  
- Apple documentation

Many successful Substack newsletters use title case for the post title but sentence case for the subtitle/preview text — the title stands out while the preview reads as conversational.

Notably, the AP Stylebook prescribes title case for *composition* titles (books, movies, songs) but sentence case for actual news *headlines* — a distinction that often gets conflated.

**For chapter titles specifically:** Title case applies to subtitles, chapter titles, blog headings — the same rules as book titles — under Chicago style, which most book publishing follows.

**The honest bottom line:** For your own blog or platforms like Medium or Substack, you're free to choose either style — there are no universally accepted rules, and many style guides were developed for academia, scientific journals, or reporting, making them feel like overkill for blog titles. The main thing is internal consistency. Pick one and stick to it within a publication.

If you're asking what *looks* more authoritative/serious: title case. What looks more modern/relaxed: sentence case. Your book manuscript would be Chicago title case throughout; a Substack with an analytical voice could go either way.
