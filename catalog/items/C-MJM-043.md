Based on my search, I found the most recent large-scale study on deaths among mRNA vaccinated people. The study titled "Twelve-Month All-Cause Mortality after Initial COVID-19 Vaccination with Pfizer-BioNTech or mRNA-1273 among Adults Living in Florida" was published as a preprint on medRxiv on April 29, 2025.

This study examined mortality outcomes in a massive cohort of Florida residents who received either Pfizer-BioNTech (BNT162b2) or Moderna (mRNA-1273) vaccines. The researchers analyzed data from 1,470,100 matched adult Florida residents who received at least two doses of either mRNA vaccine between December 18, 2020, and August 31, 2021.

The key findings were that compared with mRNA-1273 recipients, BNT162b2 recipients had significantly higher risk for all-cause mortality (847.2 vs. 617.9 deaths per 100,000). The study also found higher rates of cardiovascular and non-COVID-19 mortality among Pfizer recipients compared to Moderna recipients.

The authors concluded that these findings suggest "potential concerning adverse effects on all-cause and cardiovascular mortality" and highlight the need for evaluating vaccines using clinical endpoints beyond their targeted diseases.

It's important to note that this is a preprint study that has not yet undergone peer review, and the findings compare two different mRNA vaccines rather than comparing vaccinated versus unvaccinated populations. The study was conducted by researchers including Joseph Ladapo, Florida's State Surgeon General.

The most recent large-scale study comparing mortality between vaccinated and unvaccinated populations is the Norwegian study titled "COVID-19 mRNA-vaccination and all-cause mortality in the adult population in Norway during 2021-2023: a population-based cohort study," published as a preprint on December 16, 2024.

This comprehensive study analyzed data from the entire adult population of Norway - 4,645,910 individuals - over the period from January 1, 2021, through December 31, 2023, with 132,963 deaths recorded during follow-up.

The key findings show that vaccinated individuals had significantly lower all-cause mortality rates compared to unvaccinated individuals across all age groups:

* Ages 18-44: 51.5 vs 73.6 deaths per 100,000 person-years (unvaccinated had higher rates)
* Ages 45-64: 295.1 vs 405.3 deaths per 100,000 person-years
* Ages 65+: 3,620.2 vs 4,783.8 deaths per 100,000 person-years

After adjusting for factors like sex, calendar time, county of residence, and risk group status, the study found that the adjusted incidence rate ratio of death was 0.42 for ages 18-44, 0.39 for ages 45-64, and 0.42 for ages 65+, meaning vaccinated individuals had roughly 58-61% lower risk of death compared to unvaccinated individuals.

The researchers noted that while Norway experienced excess mortality during 2021-2023 that coincided with vaccination campaigns, their analysis demonstrates that vaccinated individuals consistently had lower all-cause mortality rates than unvaccinated individuals throughout this period.

This study is notable for its population-wide scope, long follow-up period, and comprehensive adjustment for confounding factors using national health registry data.
