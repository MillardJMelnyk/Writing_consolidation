### Claude · 2026-05-29 19:09

Right. The alphabet is three symbols — sign of the tick-to-tick mid delta: +, −, 0. No normalization, no windowing choice, no SAX. The discretization is already given by the data and it's lossless at the level you care about: every tick is exactly one of three symbols. That's the whole point — there's no upstream decision to smuggle theory into, because the sign of a delta isn't a modeling choice, it's just what happened.

And you're right that it's ludicrous to say patterns can't be found there. The string of +/−/0 over a session is a complete symbolic record of the path's *direction* at every step, and recurrence in that string is a hard, countable fact. This is the bottom-up enumeration done correctly: the corpus is just the frequency of every substring.

The concrete build, which is dead simple and exactly the right kind of simple:

Collapse ticks to the mid, take successive deltas, map to {+,−,0}. Now you have one long string. Enumerate every n-gram for n = 2,3,4,… up to whatever length, count occurrences, and for each n-gram record its empirical next-symbol distribution. That conditional distribution *is* the pattern object: "after this exact sequence of moves, what does the next tick do, and how sharply does that differ from the base rate?" No edge-gate at intake — every n-gram goes in the corpus regardless. Noise-vs-signal becomes a downstream query: which contexts bend the next-symbol distribution far from base rate, and do they hold out-of-sample.

Two things I'd flag, not as objections but because they're the only real decisions and they should be explicit:

The `0` symbol's frequency depends entirely on price granularity. On XAUUSD at dollars.cents, lots of consecutive ticks are identical, so `0` will be common and may dominate. That's fine — it's real — but you'll want to look at the corpus both with `0` included and with zero-runs collapsed, because "no change" carries different information than "moved." That's two views of the same data, not a filter.

Variable-length context is where the actual structure is. Fixed-n n-grams are the start; the richer object is the variable-order Markov model (a suffix tree / PPM-style structure) that finds, for each position, the *longest* preceding substring that still has enough occurrences to give a reliable next-symbol distribution. That's the principled bottom-up answer to "how much history matters here" — the data tells you the context length per context, instead of you picking n.

This is buildable now and runs in seconds-to-minutes on your box even for millions of ticks — it's just string counting.
