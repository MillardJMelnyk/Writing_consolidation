### Millard · 2026-09-28 00:32

It's very common for people to listen lazily, interpret what they heard incorrectly, reading ideas into what was said when they weren't actually articulated or implied, and then attack or criticize the resulting straw man. I need a word for this that clearly connotes the hallucination and injection of ideas into what was said.

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:32

**Eisegesis**: reading your own ideas into what someone said rather than drawing out what they actually meant, which is what makes the straw man possible.

### Millard · 2026-09-28 00:32

That's definitely the right term, but 9,999 out of 10,000 people will not know what it means

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:32

"Putting words in my mouth" is the plain-English version everyone understands, and it carries the same sense of inventing what you supposedly said.

### Millard · 2026-09-28 00:34

This is something that lies in between. Putting words in my mouth is someone acting like they know the real meaning of what I saidOr meant to say . What I'm talking about is more subtle.

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:34

"Reading into it," as in "you're reading things into what I said," names exactly that half-unconscious injection of ideas that were never there, and nearly everyone knows the phrase.

### Millard · 2026-09-28 00:34

Yeah, that one is probably the right one. I just avoided it because I'm looking for a noun

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:34

"Overinterpretation" is the closest common noun: everyone understands it, and it carries the sense of adding meaning that wasn't in what was said.

### Millard · 2026-09-28 00:34

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:34

### Claude · 2026-09-28 00:35

"Your version," as in "that's your version of what I said," lands a bit better because everyone instantly gets that the content came from them, not you.
