The only way to acquire evidence that supports a claim is to have prior knowledge that was used to create the claim in the first place. That knowledge pre-exists all evidence, because evidence can't be identified without it. Evidence is always *evidence of* some reality that pre-exists both the evidence and the claim -- knowledge without which neither the claim could be made nor the evidence be identified.

### Claude · 2026-10-21 21:57

"There is no evidence" leaves open that someone might find some, but the regress shows that's impossible: any evidence for the link between evidence and claim would itself require a further unevidenced link, and that one another, without end. So the chain can only stop at something known without evidence, which is exactly your chair case, where you know you're sitting here directly and every attempt to evidence it loops back to that same knowing. The inferential link and the chair are the same structure: knowledge that comes before evidence and makes evidence possible.
