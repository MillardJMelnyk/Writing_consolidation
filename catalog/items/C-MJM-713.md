### Claude · 2026-07-22 13:35

**Verified:** SMA Consular Agency does perform notarial services — that part is fine.

**Problems:**

1. **Wrong affiant.** The document has the SON swearing to the FATHER's identity. The son has no personal knowledge of how the father's passport was issued or that "MILLARD J" and "MILLARD JOHN" refer to the same person beyond visual comparison of two documents — that's not proper subject matter for his sworn testimony. The standard instrument here is a **"one and the same person" affidavit executed by the father himself**: "I, MILLARD JOHN MELNYK, depose that I am one and the same individual as MILLARD J MELNYK named as father on Birth Certificate State File No. 146-1990-070153." This is the standard fix for name-variance issues and is what INM typically expects. Consider having both — father's identity affidavit is the load-bearing one; the son's can be supplementary, not primary.
2. **No exhibits attached/referenced.** The affidavit asserts facts about two documents (birth certificate, passport) but doesn't attach or reference certified copies as Anexos. INM will want the source documents physically accompanying this.
3. **No translation.** Document is entirely in English. INM requires Spanish or certified translation (perito traductor) for tramite submission. Confirm with INM whether they'll accept it as-is or require a certified translation attached.
4. **Passport number format anomaly.** P523212DP — 1 letter + 6 digits + 2 letters. Standard Canadian passport numbers are 2 letters + 6 digits (e.g., AB123456), not this pattern. Verify against the actual passport before filing — a transcription error here undermines the entire evidentiary chain.
5. **No INM file/tramite reference number** on the document tying it to the specific residency application.
6. Blank date fields and "DO NOT SIGN UNTIL IN FRONT OF CONSULAR OFFICER" — correctly handled, no issue.
