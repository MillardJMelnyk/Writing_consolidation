Rancourt’s “17 million” figure is not a peer-reviewed causal estimate — it’s an ecological time-series extrapolation that equates any excess deaths during vaccination rollouts to the vaccines themselves. He explicitly defines a “vaccine-dose fatality rate” by dividing that inferred excess by doses administered and then extrapolates from 17 non-representative countries to the whole world. That method commits the ecological fallacy, ignores major confounders (COVID waves, health-system strain, delayed care, overdoses, heat, etc.), and produces implausible numbers that contradict surveillance and peer-reviewed work (for example, a Lancet modelling study estimated ~19.8 million lives saved in the first year of vaccination). Independent fact-checks and national health agencies have repeatedly shown these excess-death → vaccine attributions are flawed.

The Lancet <https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laninf/article/PIIS1473-3099(22)00320-6/fulltext>

Rancourt literally defines a “vaccine-dose fatality rate” as “the ratio of inferred vaccine-induced deaths to vaccine doses delivered” and then extrapolates 17 countries to the entire globe. That’s just assuming excess deaths = vaccine deaths — classic ecological fallacy, no control for COVID waves, healthcare collapse, or other confounders. His math works out to ~1 death per 794 shots, which is wildly inconsistent with surveillance and registries worldwide. By contrast, The Lancet Infectious Diseases estimated vaccines saved ~19.8 million lives in year one (Watson et al. 2022). The “17 million killed” claim collapses under scrutiny.
