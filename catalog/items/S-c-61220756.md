Codependence is no mystery; nor is it vague or difficult to identify.

Codependence is our still-active *infantilism* demanding that others change their behavior so that we won’t feel badly—getting upset and *punishing them if they refuse.*

Codependence isn’t difficult to spot: it’s glaring to everyone except the infantile.

What glitch of logic or failure of reason gives the codependent the idea that *others* are responsible for *their* (the codependent’s) feelings?

None.

To glitch or fail, logic and reason would have had to be in the picture in the first place.

That’s precisely what infantilism is: good sense overruled or abolished by instinct, psychopathology, and habit.

Oodles of people have sworn to me up and down that this overruling and abolishing of good sense is inevitable and unavoidable.

Those people are fools.

And given how easy it is to see that belief for what it is—*bullshit* (if we even want to see)—they’re *liars*, too.
