### Claude · 2026-01-12 17:55

**Horizon** comes closest to a single word.

It captures both aspects: defines the scope of the visible/thinkable (enabling) while simultaneously marking the boundary of what *cannot* be seen/conceived (constraining). What's beyond the horizon is literally invisible from your standpoint.

Philosophical precedent: Gadamer's "horizon of understanding" and Husserl's use carry exactly this dual function—the limit that both discloses and forecloses.

Alternatives that emphasize both but less elegantly:

* **Frame** (but common/weakened by overuse)
* **Episteme** (Foucauldian—the historical a priori that determines what counts as knowledge/truth in an era, both generative and restrictive)
* **Matrix** (generative womb + confining structure, but less standard in this usage)

Does **horizon** capture what you're after, or does it miss something essential?

### Claude · 2026-01-12 18:33

**Mental architecture** actually works well—concrete, visual, immediately graspable. The architectural metaphor inherently carries both functions: walls that define spaces also block access to other spaces; doorways that enable movement also channel it.

If you want something even more accessible/colloquial:

* **Mental map** (navigational—shows routes, hides what's off the map)
* **Thought-world** (emphasizes the totality/enclosure)
* **Frame of mind** (common but weaker—less structural)

But **mental architecture** is strong. It's relatable without sacrificing precision, and the construction metaphor does heavy lifting:

* Architecture is *built* (not natural/given)
* It structures what's possible within it
* It walls off what's outside it

Are you settling on "mental architecture" or still testing alternatives?

### Claude · 2026-01-12 18:34

Understood. Here are candidates worth tracking:

**Lens** — Optical precision. What it focuses, it also excludes from frame. Weakness: overused, slightly passive.

**Operating system** — Tech metaphor. Determines what programs can run, what inputs are legible. Weakness: generational accessibility.

**Worldview** — Maximum accessibility but loses structural precision. Too vague.

**Reality tunnel** (Robert Anton Wilson) — Vivid, captures both aspects sharply. Weakness: requires introducing the source.

**Cognitive landscape** — Spatial metaphor. Terrain enables certain paths, topography blocks others. More poetic than architectural.

**Thought-structure** — Parallel to mental architecture, slightly more abstract.

**Conceptual cage** — Emphasizes constraint more than enabling. Accurate but asymmetric.

I'll note if something better surfaces in other contexts.
