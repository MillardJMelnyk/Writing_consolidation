Attributed to Keynes without substantiation, but the statement is representative of his thinking.

Keynes didn't consider the "astounding belief" as a mortal flaw for capitalism, but as reason for government regulation.

I see the belief as akin to trying to siphon drinking water from a cesspool, but I guess capitalists prefer their water stank and foul.

Keynes's thinking is a great example of the nonsense that people come up with when they go into working on an idea with preexisting commitments. If those commitments contradict what's really going on -- as capitalism and every other -ism certainly do -- then they end up coming round full circle to self-contradictions in the idea, like this "astounding" belief.

It's "astounding" because it's an obvious self-contradiction *but capitalists refuse to admit it*. So, in order to escape the problem, they (astoundingly) "just believe".

This is the same situation that cult beliefs eventually find themselves in if a person thinks them through honestly.

At that point of self-contradiction, we can admit the problem and realize that the idea is nonsense (as far as the self-contradiction goes), or we can become cultists and vindicate our preexisting commitments by embracing the nonsensical by means of an "astounding" leap of pseudo-faith.

Just like Keynes did.

Simply put, this “astounding belief” is a gigantic turd of bullshit: a con job.
