Blocking issues
---------------

**1. You are not in this contract.** Tenant is Dinora Monserrat Chavez Alvarado; guarantor is Miren Guillermina Chavez Alvarado; both declared as Mexican nationals. Your name appears nowhere. If you're the one living there, you are legally a visitor under Clause Fifteenth — no right to possession, no standing to enforce anything, no claim on the deposit, and the named tenant is personally liable for everything you do in the unit. If this is meant to be your lease, it's the wrong document.

**2. Clause Thirteenth (pena convencional) is the single worst clause in the contract.** It applies to breach of *any* clause, with *no cure period*, and stacks *per breach*. The wording — "two instalments of the annual rent" — is ambiguous between $46,000 MXN (two monthly instalments) and $552,000 MXN (twice the annual rent). Trivial breaches trigger it: forgetting to WhatsApp a payment receipt (Third), reporting a leak at hour 50 instead of 48 (Sixth Bis), not handing over an *original* paid CFE receipt (Ninth), a friend's dog visiting (Seventh). Mexican civil law caps a penalty at the value of the principal obligation ($276,000), so $552,000 wouldn't survive a challenge — but you'd be litigating in Querétaro to find out. This clause needs the amount fixed in numbers, limited to material breach, and given a 15-day written cure period before it's signable.

**3. Exhibits 1 and 2 are missing.** The contract binds you to internal condo regulations (Exhibit 1) you haven't seen, and to returning an inventory (Exhibit 2) you haven't seen. Clauses First and Seventh both state the property is in perfect condition and you accept it as such. Signing without those two annexes attached, initialled, and accompanied by dated photographs of every room, appliance, wall and fixture is how deposits disappear.

**4. The contract is dated 15 August 2026 and the term already started.** Today is 3 September. The first month's rent was due 15–19 August. If nobody has moved in or paid, late interest is already accruing under Clause Third and Clause Sixteenth(A) is already live. Get the dates redrawn to the real move-in date.

Money traps
-----------

**5. Payment window is five days, penalties are brutal.** Rent is due 15th–19th. Pay on the 20th–24th and you owe 5% ($1,150). Pay on the 25th or later and you owe 10% ($2,300) — on a $23,000 rent that's a 120%+ annualised rate.

**6. Unpaid utilities = unpaid rent.** Clause Ninth: if a service bill is unpaid, "the rent shall not be deemed to have been paid." A $400 water bill you missed converts into late-rent interest plus a rescission ground under Sixteenth(A) and (C).

**7. If the water heater dies you pay full rent while it's dead.

**8. Deposit: 45 days after handover, no interest, and it can be eaten by utility debts.** It explicitly cannot be applied to rent or to the early-exit penalty. Treat $23,000 as gone until it isn't.

**9. Clause Third says rent "shall be increased annually" by INPC** — inside a 12-month fixed term that's contradictory. It should be scoped to renewal only. Get it struck or reworded.

**10. Whole-month rent regardless of occupancy.** Leave on the 16th, pay the full $23,000.

**11. Early exit costs the penalty, not the deposit.** Clause Second routes early departure through Clause Thirteenth — so you can't just walk away eating one month.

**12. Holdover: $1,000/day plus rent.** Stay 30 days past 14 Aug 2027 and that's $30,000 on top of $23,000.

**13. Clause Fourteenth commits you to renew a legal-coverage policy with a named firm, DESPACHO ACCR ARRENDAMIENTO CONFIABLE S.C., at your cost, with no price stated.** Open-ended, and failure to sign it is a rescission ground. Get the cost in writing or delete the clause.

Liability exposure
------------------

**14. Clause Eighth is strict liability for any casualty.** If there's a fire or flood, you compensate the landlord *and the neighbours* — no requirement that it be your fault. Renter's civil-liability insurance is not optional here. Note the contract names the landlord as the beneficiary of that policy, which is backwards for protecting yourself; you want the policy to defend *you*, with the landlord as additional insured.

**15. Asset-forfeiture indemnity (Fourth, Fifteenth, Sixteenth).** If the property is forfeited over a crime committed by anyone you let in, you owe the full market sale value of the house. This boilerplate is standard in Mexican leases; the exposure is real and uncapped.

**16. The guarantor signed a blank cheque.** Clause Tenth binds her to any future extension, renewal, rent increase or modification without notice or consent, and waives orden, excusión and división. Her liability doesn't end at the lease term — only when the landlord formally accepts the keys with zero debts outstanding.

One-sided and vague
-------------------

**17. Rescission grounds are subjective and unappealable.** Sixteenth(E): a complaint signed by two neighbours. (F): a written complaint from the administrator. (L): "disturbances or parties." (M): "does not respect the common areas." No definition, no cure period, no process. In a condo where you're a foreigner who doesn't speak Spanish, (E) and (F) are the ones to worry about.

**18. Sixteenth(N): the lease terminates on the tenant's death** — while the guarantor remains liable for clearing debts and handing back the property.

**19. Fifth: late rent lets the administration cut off amenities and services.** "Services" is undefined.

**20. Seventh: absolute ban on animals** — not just pets, any animal, and no visiting ones. Rescission ground.

**21. The landlord has essentially no obligations.** No delivery-of-possession clause, no quiet-enjoyment clause, no penalty on her for anything, no remedy if she fails to hand over on time. Every penalty in the document runs one direction.

**25. Clause Seventeenth (confidentiality) with damages attached** is unusual in a residential lease and technically covers showing the contract to third parties. Practically unenforceable against getting legal advice, but it's there.

**26. Four originals are signed.** Make sure you physically hold one fully signed copy with both annexes initialled.

A. Deal-breakers
================

**1. Pena convencional — two instalments, per breach, any clause, no cure period (Thirteenth)**  
The clause says breach of *any* clause of the contract costs you "two instalments of the annual rent." Three separate problems stacked in one sentence. First, the amount is ambiguous: "instalments of the annual rent" reads either as two monthly instalments ($46,000) or two payments of the annual rent ($552,000). Second, it applies to every clause equally — the clause requiring you to WhatsApp a payment receipt carries the same penalty as subletting the house. Third, "for each breach" means it stacks; three technical breaches over a year is three penalties. Mexican civil law caps a contractual penalty at the value of the principal obligation, so $552,000 would fail in court, but that means litigating in Querétaro to find out. Fix: fixed peso amount written in numbers, restricted to material breach, 15 days written notice to cure first.

**2. No cure period anywhere in the document**  
Rescission grounds A through N in Sixteenth fire on the fact of the breach. There is no clause anywhere giving you notice and an opportunity to fix a problem before consequences attach. A late water bill, a noise complaint, an unauthorised shelf bracket — each is immediately actionable. Every functioning lease has a notice-and-cure mechanism. This one has none, by design. Fix: add a general clause requiring written notice and 15 days to remedy before any rescission or penalty.

**3. The early-exit penalty is referenced three times and defined zero times**  
Clause Second says leaving early requires paying "the penalty set out in the PENA CONVENCIONAL clause." Clause Twelfth refers to "the pena convencional for early vacating" as though it's a known figure. But Clause Thirteenth only defines a penalty for *breach of a clause* — and leaving early after satisfying every condition in Second is not a breach, it's compliance with a procedure the contract itself lays out. So the amount you'd owe to leave early is genuinely undefined, and the landlord's position in a dispute will be that it's whatever Thirteenth says, at the highest reading. Fix: a separate numbered clause stating the early-termination fee as a peso figure.

**4. Annexes 1 and 2 are missing**  
Clause Fifth binds you to the condo's internal regulations, labelled Exhibit 1. Clause Seventh binds you to return the property matching an inventory, labelled Exhibit 2. Neither is attached. You would be agreeing to rules you have never read and to the completeness of a list you have never seen. The reglamento is where pets, guests, parking, noise hours, amenity access and the fine schedule actually live — and Sixteenth(F) makes a written complaint from the administrator a rescission ground, so those rules have teeth. Fix: get both documents, translated, initialled by both parties, physically attached before signature. Photograph every room, appliance, wall, fixture and existing defect on handover day and attach a dated condition report.

**5. Rent is structured as one annual obligation, not twelve monthly ones**  
Clause Third says you owe "$276,000 as rent for 12 months," *paid in* twelve instalments. That's a single debt with a payment schedule, not twelve separate monthly debts. Thirteenth then says prepaid unaccrued rent isn't refundable. Together these support an argument that on default the whole $276,000 is due, not just the months you actually occupied. Fix: reword as "monthly rent of $23,000 payable for a term of twelve months."

B. Money
========

**6. Five-day payment window with punitive interest (Third)**  
Rent is due the 15th through the 19th. Pay on the 20th and you owe 5% — $1,150. Pay on the 25th and you owe 10% — $2,300. On a monthly obligation that's an annualised rate north of 120%. A five-day window is unusually tight; most leases give ten. Fix: window of the 15th to the 25th, flat 3% thereafter.

**7. Unpaid utilities are deemed unpaid rent (Ninth)**  
The exact words: if you fail to pay for services, "the rent shall not be deemed to have been paid." A $300 water bill you overlooked converts into a rent default — which triggers late interest under Third, rescission under Sixteenth(A) and (C), and a penalty under Thirteenth. One small bill, three consequences. Fix: strike that sentence. Unpaid utilities should be recoverable as a debt, nothing more.

**8. Rent can never be withheld, for any reason (Third)**  
"May not be withheld on any ground or determination, whether judicial or extrajudicial." Read alongside Sixth Bis, where the landlord has fifteen days merely to *begin* repairs with no deadline to finish and no consequence for never finishing, this means you pay $23,000 a month on a house with no hot water for as long as she likes. Fix: add a repair-and-deduct right — if a habitability defect isn't remedied within a stated period, you may arrange the repair and deduct the cost from rent against receipts.

**9. Deposit held 45 days, no interest, consumable by utility debts (Twelfth, Ninth)**  
$23,000 out of your pocket, returned 45 days after handover, conditional on no damage, complete inventory and no outstanding service debts. It explicitly cannot be applied to rent or to the early-exit penalty. Since the inventory doesn't exist yet (item 4), the condition on its completeness is currently unfalsifiable in your favour. Fix: 15 days, and tie release to the signed condition report from item 4.

**10. Annual INPC increase written into a twelve-month fixed term (Third)**  
Clause Third says the rent "shall be increased annually" by the INPC. The term is twelve months. Either the sentence is meaningless or it's an argument for an increase during the term. It belongs in the renewal clause, not here. Fix: strike it from Third; Second already covers INPC on renewal.

**11. Whole-month rent regardless of occupancy (Third)**  
Vacate on the 16th, owe the full $23,000 for that month. Standard enough in Mexico, but combined with item 3's undefined exit penalty it means an early departure costs a full month plus an unquantified penalty plus a forfeited deposit.

**12. Holdover at $1,000 per day plus rent (Second)**  
Stay past 14 August 2027 without a signed renewal and you owe $1,000 daily on top of the normal rent. Thirty days of overlap while you find a new place costs $30,000 extra. Not unreasonable in principle — it exists to stop squatting — but there's no grace period at all, and no obligation on the landlord to respond promptly to your renewal notice. If she sits on your 30-day notice, the clock runs against you.

**13. Open-ended legal-coverage commitment to a named firm (Fourteenth)**  
On renewal you must buy a *póliza jurídica* from DESPACHO ACCR ARRENDAMIENTO CONFIABLE S.C. at your cost. No price is stated. Failure to sign it before expiry is a rescission ground. You're committing to an unknown amount payable to a firm you didn't choose, and the firm that drafted this contract is presumably that firm. Fix: get the price in writing now, or delete the clause.

**14. No proof-of-payment obligation on the landlord (Third)**  
You transfer to a personal Scotiabank account and send her a receipt. She owes you nothing in return. If she later claims a month went unpaid you'd have a bank record showing money moved to her account, but no document from her establishing what it settled. Fix: require a written receipt or CFDI within five days of each payment.

**15. No title or arrears verification**  
Recital I(c) asserts she owns the property, with no escritura number, notary, or folio real. Recital I(e) asserts no maintenance arrears. Both are bare assertions. Unpaid condo fees attach to the unit, not the owner, so her arrears become your problem with the administration. Fix: before signing, ask for the deed, a current predial receipt, and an *estado de cuenta* from the condo administration.

C. Liability
============

**16. Strict liability for any casualty, to the landlord and the neighbours (Eighth)**  
If a fire or flood occurs in the unit, you compensate the landlord *and the other residents* for their losses. There is no requirement that it be your fault. A failure in wiring that predates your tenancy is on you as written. In a condominium, water damage travels — one burst supply line can hit three units. Fix: limit to damage caused by your negligence, and buy renter's civil-liability insurance before move-in regardless of whether they amend it.

**17. The insurance clause names the wrong beneficiary (Eighth)**  
It says if you take civil-liability cover, the landlord is the beneficiary. That's backwards. A civil-liability policy exists to defend and indemnify *you* against third-party claims. Naming her as beneficiary of it produces a policy that pays her while leaving you exposed to the neighbours. Fix: you are the insured, she is an additional insured.

**18. Uncapped asset-forfeiture indemnity (Fourth, Fifteenth, Sixteenth)**  
If the property is subjected to *extinción de dominio* because of a crime committed by you or by anyone you allowed inside, you owe the landlord the full market sale value of the house. This is standard Mexican boilerplate post-2019 and you will find it in every lease in Querétaro — see item 39. The exposure is nonetheless real and unlimited, and the trigger includes acts by guests, contractors and delivery people.

**19. The guarantor is bound to obligations she never agreed to (Tenth)**  
Her liability survives any extension, renewal, modification or rent increase the landlord grants, without notice to her and without her consent. She also waives *orden*, *excusión* and *división* — meaning the landlord can pursue her first, for the full amount, without touching the tenant's assets. She is guaranteeing a contract whose future terms she has no vote on.

**20. The guarantor can never be released (Tenth)**  
Her liability ends only when the landlord "formally receives" the property free of occupants and debts. The contract defines no handover procedure, no *acta de entrega*, and no deadline for the landlord to attend a walkthrough. She can decline to formally receive it indefinitely. Meanwhile Twelfth triggers deposit return on plain "handover" — two different definitions of the same event, in the same contract. Fix: a defined walkthrough with a signed acta, and deemed acceptance if the landlord doesn't appear within a stated number of days of notice.

**21. Attempted disclaimer of the landlord's own liability (Fifteenth)**  
"THE LANDLORD assumes no liability of any kind for the harm suffered by any person within the Property." As written that covers her own defective wiring, a collapsing balcony, a faulty gas installation. Mexican law does not permit contracting out of liability for your own negligence, so it wouldn't hold — but leaving it in the document encourages her to behave as though it does. Fix: strike it.

**22. One-way legal costs (Thirteenth)**  
You pay her legal costs and expenses arising from your breach. There is no reciprocal provision if she breaches and you win. Fix: make it mutual, or delete.

D. Contradictions and drafting failures
=======================================

**23. Fifth contradicts Sixteenth(F) on administrator complaints**  
Fifth says it takes more than three sanctions from the administration during the term to cause rescission. Sixteenth(F) says one written complaint from the administrator does it. The lenient clause is dead — a landlord invoking rescission will cite F. Fix: delete F, or align it with Fifth's three-strike standard.

**24. Sixth Bis, Seventh and Eighth allocate the same risk three different ways**  
Sixth Bis puts habitability defects on the landlord. Seventh puts all repair costs on you. Eighth makes you liable for any casualty with no fault requirement. Which clause governs a burst pipe? All three, differently. Ambiguity in practice favours whoever is holding the deposit and can afford the lawyer.

**25. Clause First refers to "said regulations" with no antecedent**  
The clause describes the property, states it's in perfect condition, then obliges you to "comply in full with said regulations" — no regulations having been mentioned anywhere in that clause. It's orphaned text from a template. Harmless in isolation, but you're signing an undertaking to comply with an unidentified document and acknowledging awareness of its sanctions.

**26. The 30-day renewal notice appears twice in Clause Second, differently worded**  
Once as a tenant obligation to notify the landlord, once as a mutual obligation for both parties to notify each other. Sloppy drafting; the practical risk is arguing later about which formulation applied.

**27. Recital II(i) reverses the parties**  
The tenant declares she wishes "to lease the Property to THE LANDLORD." Legally meaningless — the operative clauses are clear — but it tells you nobody read this document before sending it.

E. Obligations you cannot actually perform
==========================================

**28. Original paid utility receipts (Ninth)**  
You must deliver "duly paid original receipts" to the landlord. CFE bills and Querétaro water bills are digital. There is no original to hand over. This creates a permanent, unavoidable technical breach that sits available to the landlord — and via Thirteenth, each month it recurs is worth two instalments. Fix: change to "copies or digital proof of payment."

**29. Service suspension "for whatever cause" is a rescission ground (Ninth)**  
As written, a scheduled CFE maintenance outage on your street is grounds to terminate your lease. Obviously not the intent, but it's what the words say, and the words are what you'd be arguing about. Fix: limit to suspension caused by your non-payment.

**30. The 48-hour defect notice is itself a clause (Sixth Bis)**  
Notice a slow leak on a Friday evening, report it Monday morning, and you've missed the window. Sixth Bis already penalises that with damages. But because it's a clause of the contract, missing it is also a breach of a clause — which triggers Thirteenth on top. Fix: five business days, and remove the double penalty.

F. Missing protections
======================

**31. No successor clause**  
Nothing binds a future buyer if the landlord sells during the term. An unregistered lease is weak against a new owner in Mexico. Fix: add a clause binding the landlord's successors and assigns, or register the contract with the Registro Público de la Propiedad.

**32. No allocation of extraordinary condo assessments**  
The $23,000 includes the ordinary maintenance fee. A special assessment — roof replacement, façade work, new pump — is unallocated. Those can run tens of thousands. Fix: state explicitly that extraordinary assessments are the owner's.

**33. No delivery-of-possession obligation**
