### Claude · 2025-11-25 08:54

Based on my examination of both studies, the Substack article contains **significant misrepresentations and omissions**:

Study 1 (Faksova et al., 99 million): PARTIAL ACCURACY
------------------------------------------------------

**Article Claims:**

* "500% increased risk of Myocarditis"
* "300% increased risk of spinal cord inflammation"

**Actual Findings:**
The study did find elevated observed-versus-expected (OE) ratios for:

* Myocarditis and pericarditis (study confirms increased risk but doesn't report 500% in the abstract)
* Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM - brain/spinal inflammation): OE ratio 3.78 (278% increase, not 300%) after first dose of mRNA-1273

The study conclusion: "This multi-country analysis **confirmed pre-established safety signals** for myocarditis, pericarditis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, and cerebral venous sinus thrombosis."

Study 2 (Karimi et al., 85 million): **MAJOR MISREPRESENTATION**
----------------------------------------------------------------

**Article Claims:**

* "300% increased risks of Heart attack"
* "200% increased risk of arrhythmias"
* "250% increased risk of stroke"

**Actual Primary Findings (from abstract):**

> "**No increased risk of heart attack, arrhythmia, or stroke was observed post-COVID-19 vaccination.**"

> "a **protective effect** on stroke (OR, 0.19; 95% CrI: 0.10–0.39) and myocardial infarction (OR, 0.003; 95% CrI: 0.001–0.006) was observed after the **third dose** of the vaccine."

**What the study actually found:**

* Overall: NO increased risk for MI, stroke, or arrhythmia
* Increased CAD risk: OR 1.70 (70% increase) for second dose specifically
* Third dose showed **protective effects** against stroke and MI
* Subgroup analyses showed some elevated risks in specific vaccine/dose combinations, but these were not the primary findings

The article **cherry-picked subgroup data** while **completely omitting** the study's primary conclusion that no overall increased risk was observed for these outcomes.

Critical Context Missing from Article:
--------------------------------------

Neither study concluded vaccines are "NOT SAFE FOR HUMAN USE." Both studies:

1. Acknowledged rare adverse events
2. Noted these as known safety signals requiring monitoring
3. Did not recommend discontinuing vaccination
4. The Karimi study specifically found **protective effects** for some outcomes

The article's headline claim is not supported by either study's conclusions.
