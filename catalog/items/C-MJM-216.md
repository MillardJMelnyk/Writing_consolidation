Prior to the 20th Century, what was the worst, most destructive and heinous war in Europe, ever?

### Claude · 2025-08-23 20:15

**Thirty Years' War (1618-1648).**

Death toll: 4.5-8 million, most estimates 8 million.

German population impact: 40% loss (20 million to 12 million). Rural areas: 40% loss. Urban areas: 33% loss. Some regions 50% loss (Pomerania), others 10% (Lower Saxony).

**Assumption to attack:** Question assumes "worst" = single metric. "Destructive" could mean economic damage, territorial loss, cultural destruction, not just deaths. "Heinous" introduces moral judgment—religious wars had sectarian brutality, but other conflicts had systematic enslavement/genocide.

**Counter-steelman:** Mongol invasions (13th century) killed higher percentage of European population but weren't primarily "European wars." Black Death (1347-1351) killed 30-60% but wasn't war.

### Claude · 2025-08-23 20:20

Pre-siege population: Up to 500,000 (world's largest city)

Killed: 700,000-1 million (including several hundred thousand refugees)

**Kill rate: 140-200% of base population.**

**Problem exposed:** Numbers impossible. Persian historian Juvayni claimed 1.3+ million killed. Contemporary estimates suggest impossible figures for region's total population.

**Assumption attack:** Death toll sources are Persian/Arab chroniclers writing decades later, incentivized to exaggerate Mongol brutality. No census data. Refugee influx unknown but likely <100,000 max given regional population density.

**Actual estimate:** 500,000 base + ~50,000 refugees = ~550,000 total. Kill rate: ~90-95% realistic maximum.
