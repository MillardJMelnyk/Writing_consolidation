what's the best name for the god of the jews to distinguish it from the father of Jesus?

### Claude · 2025-08-15 17:55

The most common and respectful way to distinguish the God of Judaism from Christian theological concepts is to use **"Hashem"** (Hebrew for "The Name") or **"Adonai"** (Hebrew for "Lord"). These are traditional Jewish terms that Jews themselves use.

You could also use:

* **"The God of Israel"** or **"The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob"**
* **"YHWH"** (the Tetragrammaton, though many Jews prefer not to vocalize this)
* Simply **"God"** in a Jewish context

It's worth noting that Judaism doesn't recognize the Christian Trinity, so from a Jewish perspective, there's only one God - the same God that Christians call the Father. The distinction you're making reflects Christian theology rather than Jewish theology. When discussing these concepts academically or interfere religiously, being clear about which theological framework you're using helps avoid confusion.

If you're writing or speaking about comparative religion, phrases like "God in Jewish theology" versus "God the Father in Christian theology" can help clarify the different conceptual frameworks while being respectful to both traditions.
