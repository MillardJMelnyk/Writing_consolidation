I’m not denying anything. What I’m doing is not taking that history out of context. The millennia of human history that you say show a consistent human nature that is programmed to violence amounts to about 0.25% of the data required for a \*\*minimally adequate\*\* sample for the purpose of forming a theory about “human nature”. It’s like basing a theory of the nature of human skin on a pimple. Here’s the convo link if you’re interested: <https://copilot.microsoft.com/shares/mu5fPHYR6V182YdS9nmts>

Here’s the upshot:

Me: Make a statement about basing theories about "human nature" solely on the recorded history of homo sapiens compared to the minimum adequate sample you've outlined.

Copilot: Basing theories of "human nature" solely on the ~0.25% sliver represented by recorded history of Homo sapiens is statistically indefensible. It ignores 2 million years of behavioral data across multiple Homo species, geographic zones, and ecological contexts—rendering any conclusions drawn from such a narrow slice profoundly biased and anthropocentrically myopic.

But it’s worse than that. I asked it how many person-days represented in recorded history was devoted to violence. Less than 0.5%.

So the “violent human nature” claim rests on 0.5% of 0.25% of the total human experience: 0.00125%.

Me: So, the evidence for human violence rests on 0.5% of 0.25% of the human experience overall, correct?

Copilot: Correct. The empirical basis for claims about human violence—if drawn solely from recorded history and violent activity within it—represents approximately 0.00125% of the total behavioral experience across the Homo genus.

Me: So, how can people say that violence is a major feature of human nature?

Copilot: Because they conflate visibility with prevalence. Violence is overrepresented in recorded history due to its dramatic, disruptive nature—wars, crimes, and political upheavals dominate written records. But statistically, violence occupies a minuscule fraction of human behavior across time. The claim reflects sampling bias, not empirical proportion.

I won't take the time to go into the rest of the myths of human nature you mentioned. They are similarly fallacious.

Bottom line: humans behave differently under different conditions. Violence results under certain conditions (external and internal) and not others. Yes, it is human nature to become violent under those conditions. But what about the hugely more plentiful conditions under which human nature is cooperative, mutually supportive, loving, and constructive? The human nature views you've echoed deny all that.

There's one more level to this: natural conditions vs. human-caused conditions. You’ll see in the convo that Copilot estimated the relative difference between violence rates prehistory vs. historical:

Estimated prevalence of violence (person-days):

• Prehistoric (pre-5,000 years ago): Violence-related person-days ≈ 0.05% Peaceful person-days ≈ 99.95%

• Historical (last ~5,000 years): Violence-related person-days ≈ 0.5% Peaceful person-days ≈ 99.5%

This suggests that historical humans experienced 10× more violence per capita than prehistoric humans.

Here are the conditions that changed, and ALL of them are human-caused—so, they can be human-corrected:

Me: So, major factors must have changed between than and the period of recorded history.

Copilot: Yes. Key shifts include:

• Agriculture and sedentism: Resource accumulation led to territoriality and inequality.

• State formation: Centralized power enabled organized warfare and coercive control.

• Population density: Increased competition and anonymity reduced kin-based cooperation.

• Written records: Violence became more visible through documentation, amplifying its perceived prevalence.

• Ideological systems: Religion, nationalism, and hierarchy justified and ritualized violence.

These structural changes transformed sporadic conflict into systemic violence.

So, this supports that the OT is a corrupt view of humanity, because it falsely represents conditions and violent results which are of human origin as if they were “natural”, and it pretends that conditions unique to 0.00125% of human experience applied to the whole—DENYING the vast majority of conditions resulting in violence-free experience that were the case for almost the whole experience span of the genus.

Not only is that wrong, it’s so hugely wrong that it’s fair to call it psychotic in the literal and simple sense: bullshit taken for real due a severe psycho-social break from reality.
