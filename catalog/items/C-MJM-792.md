### Claude · 2026-09-28 16:08

No. Research doesn't support "most people find sans serif easier" or the reverse. Reviews of legibility studies (e.g., Poole's literature review) find no consistent serif vs. sans-serif advantage for print body text at normal sizes. Differences are small and mostly track familiarity and personal preference. Sans serif has a real advantage on low-resolution screens and for some readers with dyslexia. The serif convention in books is largely habit and tradition, not measured superiority.

So Arial is defensible, and your preference is a legitimate basis for choosing it. Size, line length, and leading affect readability more than the serif choice. That is why 9 pt is the weak point in your setup, not the typeface.

Some are designed for readability, but none is proven more readable than Arial for print body text.

* **Verdana:** wider letterforms, larger x-height, looser spacing. Designed for small sizes. It is the strongest candidate at 9 pt, but runs about 10-15% wider than Arial (my estimate), so page count rises.
* **Atkinson Hyperlegible:** free, designed for low-vision readers, with distinct I/l/1 and 0/O. Also wider than Arial.

* **Arial's weak point:** tighter default spacing and ambiguous I/l/1 at small sizes.

The evidence is thin. The differences are small and depend on size, leading, and reader.

Give me exact installation instructions on Windows 11

### Claude · 2026-09-28 16:24

1. Go to https://fonts.google.com/specimen/PT+Sans and click **Get font**, then **Download all**. This saves `PT_Sans.zip` to Downloads.
2. Right-click the zip, choose **Extract All...**, then click **Extract**.
3. Open the extracted folder. Select the .ttf files: `PT_Sans-Web-Regular.ttf`, `PT_Sans-Web-Bold.ttf`, `PT_Sans-Web-Italic.ttf`, `PT_Sans-Web-BoldItalic.ttf`. (File names may differ slightly; install all .ttf files in the folder if unsure. The Narrow and Caption files are optional.)
4. Right-click the selected files and choose **Show more options**, then **Install for all users** (requires admin). If you don't have admin rights, choose **Install** instead.
5. Close Word completely, including any background Word processes, then reopen it.
6. Select your text, open the font box, and type `PT Sans`.

If it doesn't appear in Word: go to Settings > Personalization > Fonts and check that PT Sans is listed. If it's listed there, restart the PC.

For PDF export: File > Export > Create PDF/XPS > Options, and make sure **ISO 19005-1 compliant (PDF/A)** is checked. That ensures fonts are embedded. Word embeds them by default, but this makes it explicit.

From prior knowledge; not verified against the current Google Fonts download file names. Data as of Sep 28, 2026.
