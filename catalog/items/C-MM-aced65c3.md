### Millard · 2026-07-26 04:55

Am I correct in my impression that I'm far more likely to get in trouble for criticizing the policies and morality of genocidal countries like Israel and the United States if I'm present in the US, Canada, or the UK as opposed to Mexico?

### Claude · 2026-07-26 04:55

Yes, largely correct — with a citizenship-status caveat.

**US:** If you're a US citizen, the First Amendment protects you from prosecution for this — no legal risk. If you're a visa holder, green card holder, or foreign visitor, the risk is real and current: State Department officials testified under oath in July 2025 that criticism of Israel or US foreign policy is treated as grounds for visa/green card revocation. Over 300 student visas were revoked in 2025 on this basis; new DHS guidance (April 2026) extends this to green card denials for social-media criticism of Israel. A federal judge ruled the policy unconstitutional in Sept 2025, but it's still being litigated and enforced. There's also a pending bill to strip US citizens' passports for this — not law yet.

**UK:** No First Amendment equivalent. Public order laws and terrorism-related statutes (glorification/support of proscribed groups) have been used against pro-Palestinian protesters and speech, including arrests over placards and chants. Broad political criticism of a government's policy is generally legal, but the line into arrestable territory is much easier to cross than in the US.

**Canada:** Political speech criticizing foreign states is generally protected, no comparable crackdown to the US. Main exposure is professional/social (job loss, platform action) rather than legal.

**Mexico:** No equivalent enforcement regime targeting this speech. Lowest legal risk of the four for this specific issue.

Bottom line, ranked by actual risk: US (if non-citizen) > UK > Canada > Mexico. If you're a US citizen, Mexico and the US are close to equal legally, but the UK still carries more risk than either.

### Claude · 2026-07-26 04:57

As a Canadian citizen, your exposure depends on where you're physically present, not your citizenship:

* **In the US:** Same category as any foreign national — no First Amendment claim protects a non-citizen from visa denial/entry refusal/deportation. Canadians usually enter under visa-waiver rules, but border/customs officers have discretion to deny entry, and CBP can search phones/social media at the border. Risk: high if you're vocal and crossing into the US.
* **In the UK:** Public-order and terrorism-glorification laws apply regardless of citizenship. Risk: moderate-high.
* **In Canada:** Full Charter protection as a citizen — no legal risk for political criticism. Risk: low.
* **In Mexico:** No enforcement regime targeting this speech, but as a foreign visitor you have no special protection either — just no active policy targeting it. Risk: low.

Ranked for you: US > UK > Mexico ≈ Canada.
