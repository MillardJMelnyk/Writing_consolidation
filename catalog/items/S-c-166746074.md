//I didn't assume that's what you meant, and I wasn't accusing you of SAYING it so much as asking IF you were IMPLYING it.//

Cool, gotcha. As a standing note, in discussions like this I \*\*do not imply\*\*. Nothing to read between the lines. I do my best to say exactly what I mean and stand behind it exactly as I said it. If you find something vague or equivocal, my bad -- just ask me and I'll make it explicit and clear. A lot of people in the past have taken convos in some really bizarre directions because they just can't accept that I intended and meant something I didn't actually say. That's been happening less and less though. Grateful for that.

//

Meanwhile... look at your narrative. When you deny that we can each believe whatever we want, using the REASON that it's not possible or allowed, BECAUSE we are constrained by reality... and then you say to deny reality is to be psychotic (to break from reality)... What else are you saying by that?

//

I think that's a misread. Which statement(s) exactly are you referring to?

I took pains to make a sharp distinction between the questions of whether we're "allowed" to believe what we want and whether we are going to allow people to believe what they want. Did you get that part?

It's not me that says a break from reality constitutes psychosis, it's the psychologists.

Like I already said, I don't do insinuation, implied "what else I'm saying". Plus, you're the one who suspects "what else" -- so please reveal your suspicion. "What else" do you suspect I might have meant. Just sitting here, I don't have a clue.

Ah! Hadn't quite gotten there. No, I'm not saying "if we disagree then one of us is psychotic?" That would be pretty stupid, wouldn't it? I won't deny that I can sometimes be stupid, even extremely stupid -- just not that kind of stupid.

It will help from now on if you'd quote verbatim the phrase, sentence, or passage you're referring to when objecting or asking questions. It will spare crosstalk resulting from me guessing wrong what you have in mind.

I take it you're referring to the following?

//

So, no, you don't have the freedom to believe what you want unconstrained by reality, because breaking from reality constitutes psychosis. You and I reside in the same universe, so that's our common ground. It isn't subject to what we want to believe, and it doesn't change simply because we want to believe X, Y, or Z about it. Strictly speaking, you even have the freedom to be psychotic. But why would you want to? In other words, neither of us has the freedom to escape from the real situation we find ourselves in: residing in the same shared universe. That's why we call breaking from it "psychosis".

//

I'm not sure how you got "if we disagree then one of us is psychotic" from that, though. It will take a little work to read that and understand it, but it says nothing of the sort, nor does it imply or insinuate it. But maybe I need to hear from you how you're reading it. It's not uncommon for me to miss a possible interpretation that I didn't intend.

The point of that paragraph and the point of bringing the topic up at all is that, "... he's given us the freedom to say that. Given you the freedom to believe what you want, and me the freedom to believe what I want." is disembodied. It's a belief that exists in a conceptual vacuum, ignoring fundamental aspects of real life in the real world that would be its context, i.e., that would embody it. All I'm doing is shining some light on the context.

Remember Ra's al Ghul: “Mind your surroundings!” 😁
