### Claude · 2025-09-22 15:45

Both are correct. Context determines usage:

* "result" (singular) - one outcome
* "results" (plural) - multiple outcomes

Your statement works with either:

* "Not all kinds of hysteria result in evildoing" (hysteria causes)
* "Not all kinds of hysteria results in evildoing" (each kind results)

Standard usage favors "result" (plural verb) since "kinds" is plural subject.

### Millard · 2025-09-22 15:55

is there much difference between saying "deliberately intent on harm" and "deliberately intent on causing harm" ?

### Claude · 2025-09-22 15:55

Minimal difference. "Deliberately intent on causing harm" is more precise and standard.

"Deliberately intent on harm" is slightly ambiguous - could mean seeking harm generally or targeting oneself for harm.

"Deliberately intent on causing harm" explicitly specifies the person as harm's source/agent.

Both convey malicious intent. The second is clearer.
