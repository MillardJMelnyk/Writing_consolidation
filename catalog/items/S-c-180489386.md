I didn't take you as quoting him to praise it.

What you distinguish as codependence in different "disciplines" I take as seminal codependence -- which is psychological, induced by traumatic abuse of which rhetoric and spectacle are relatively superficial, reinforcing elements, not originating elements -- being expressed in different disciplines (domains, spheres, sectors, or whatever preferred label.) They're different in expression but not in the basic psychology being expressed.

> **Quoted note — , 2025-11-23:** [link](https://substack.com/@/note/c-180465104)  
> I'm not citing de Tocqueville to praise American democracy.
> 
> I cite him as a diagnostic of soft despotism—a precursor to the administrative infantilization you argue is now achieved through rhetoric and spectacle.
> 
> Not that you're incorrect, but codependence is a private, interpersonal pathology.
> 
> Tocqueville was talking about a structural, administrative one. So it's just a matter of what discipline one cares to see these movements. For me, most times, Im attempting to create an arc from thinkers like Pascal, Nietzsche, Scheler, Cioran, and Tocqueville in an attempt to diagnose the decay of civic life.
