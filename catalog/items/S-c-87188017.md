It's NOT just "capitalism" -- it's *every* "system" built on the foundation of "ownership".

In other words, it's every socio-economic system ever implemented by what we'd call a "civilized society".

Oddly and ironically enough, you'll only find gift and sharing systems among the "uncivilized".

Maybe being uncivilized is not at all a bad thing.

Ownership is, at essence, the unilaterally declared right of preemptive, causeless deprivation of others. Only the recognition of ownership requires other to agree. The assertion of ownership is undeniably, unequivocally, inevitably, and irremediably (I'd add irredeemably) an act of aggression. So, it's inherently and unavoidably violent, harmful, and injurious.

Before you knee-jerk into a defense of ownership, think a moment. What does the evidence show? The vast majority of people *thriving* in societies built on the principle of deprivation-first-always? Or a minuscule minority riding on the backs of the vast suffering, oppressed majority? Think Sumerians, Babylonians, Greeks, Romans, Medieval Europe, India (caste system), China (burying live servants and soldiers with royals), Egypt, you name it.

What does the evidence say about the success (and for whom?) of ownership-based societies?
