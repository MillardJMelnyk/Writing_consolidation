### Claude · 2026-08-14 23:59

A few options, roughly ordered from lighter (must/might/could) to heavier (must have/might have + past deduction):

**1. Mystery Object Bag**  
Put 4-5 unusual objects in a bag. Students reach in, feel one without looking, and guess aloud: "It might be a key. It could be a coin. It must be metal." Reveal after guesses. Good for present-tense modals of deduction (must/might/could/can't be).

**2. Sound Guessing**  
Play short audio clips (door creak, phone ringing, dog barking, rain) — even just from your phone. Students speculate: "That must be a dog. It can't be a car." Works well as a warm-up.

**3. Photo Deduction**  
Show a photo (person's room, an empty street, a suitcase) and ask "What can you tell about this person?" Elicit: "She must be a student — there are books everywhere." "He might live alone." Good bridge to past deduction if you follow with "What do you think happened here?"

**4. Crime Scene / Detective Roleplay**  
Give pairs a simple scenario (broken window, missing cookies, wet umbrella by the door) and clue cards. They build theories using past modals: "Someone must have climbed through the window." "It might have been the cat." "It can't have been Tom — he was at work." This is the strongest one for past deduction specifically, if that's your target today.

**5. Odd One Out / What's Missing**  
Show a set of 4 pictures, one doesn't fit. Students discuss and justify: "This one must be different because..." Trains reasoning + modal use together, not just isolated drills.

**6. Discussion prompts** (open speculation, less structured):  
- "Why do you think [certain shops] are always empty at [certain time]?"  
- "A friend didn't answer their phone all day — what could have happened?"  
- "Look at this abandoned building — what might have happened here?"

Got it — dropping the past forms, keeping it to modal + bare infinitive (must be, might be, could be, can't be).

**1. Mystery Object Bag**  
Objects in a bag, students feel one, guess: "It must be metal." "It could be a key." "It can't be a ball." Reveal at the end.

**2. Sound Guessing**  
Play short sounds (dog barking, phone ringing, rain). Students speculate: "That must be a dog." "It might be raining."

**3. Photo Deduction**  
Show a photo of a person or place. Elicit: "She must be a teacher." "He might be tired." "They can't be rich — look at the car."

**4. Odd One Out**  
4 pictures, one doesn't fit. Students justify: "This must be the odd one — it's the only animal."

**5. Who Am I? (mystery person)**  
One student thinks of a person (celebrity, classmate, family member); others ask yes/no questions and speculate: "You must be thinking of a man." "It could be someone from this class."

**6. Discussion prompts**  
- "Look at this house — who do you think lives there?"  
- "This person is carrying a big bag and running — what could be happening?"  
- "It's midnight and the lights are still on in that office — who might be inside?"

### Claude · 2026-08-15 00:41

1. **Guess the Emoji Story** — Share a string of emojis (🎂🔥🚒) and students speculate what happened: "Someone might be having a birthday party." "The cake could have caught fire." *(keep it modal+infinitive: "The cake could be on fire.")*
2. **Screen-Share Google Street View** — Drop into a random street view somewhere in the world. Students guess: "This must be in Asia." "It could be winter there."
3. **Zoom Background Guess** — Everyone changes their virtual background to something random (beach, city, forest). Others guess: "You must be at home really." "You could be on vacation."
4. **20 Questions (Deduction Edition)** — One student thinks of an object/person/place; others can only ask deduction-style guesses instead of yes/no questions: "It must be something you eat." "It could be an animal."
5. **Mystery Sound Round** — Share your screen and play random YouTube sound clips (typewriter, ocean waves, crowd cheering). Students guess the source and situation.
6. **Freeze Frame Guessing** — Pause a muted video/movie clip on a dramatic frame. Students speculate what's happening and what might happen next.
7. **"Whose Screen Is It?"** — Everyone shares a close-up photo of one item on their desk (not their face). Others guess whose it is and why: "This must be Ana's — she loves plants."
8. **Celebrity Silhouette/Zoom-in Reveal** — Show a heavily zoomed-in or blurred photo of a famous person, slowly reveal it. Students guess between reveals.
