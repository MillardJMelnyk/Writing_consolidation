Is it possible that "we got it wrong" about violence for thousands of years?

Better question: How is it possible to maintain millennia-long institutional systems when they're dead wrong in stupidly basic ways?

That's not getting it wrong.

That's never intending to get it right.

Give me a summary of this new Yorker article at

<https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2025/06/09/unforgiving-places-jens-ludwig-book-review>

//

"Unforgiving Places," economist Jens Ludwig argues that Americans misunderstand violent crime by overemphasizing planned, instrumental acts while ignoring impulsive, expressive violence (driven by anger or frustration), which accounts for most shootings and homicides. This focus leads to ineffective policies like mass incarceration, which fail to deter emotion-fueled acts rooted in "System 1" thinking (fast, intuitive) rather than rational calculation.

Key points: Violence clusters in specific "unforgiving" places due to environmental cues, not individual pathology or poverty alone; solutions involve de-escalation training (e.g., Chicago's BAM program, cutting teen arrests 50%), community interventions (e.g., greening lots reduced gun violence 29% in Philadelphia), and informal social controls over punishment or gun control.

Evidence includes FBI data showing 77% of murders as expressive, Chicago stats linking 70-80% of homicides to arguments, and trials proving environmental tweaks (better lighting) slash crime without policing escalation. Ludwig critiques the justice system for assuming rational actors and calls for treating violence as mundane misunderstandings, not moral failings, to rebuild safer communities.
