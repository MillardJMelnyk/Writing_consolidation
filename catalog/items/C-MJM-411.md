### Millard · 2025-12-06 13:19

My son was married in Mexico to a Mexican citizen, has lived here 4 years, and just recently got his Mexican permanent resident status. He's been told that there is a special allowance for parents of people given permanent residence. What steps do I need to take to take advantage of this?

### Claude · 2025-12-06 13:20

Based on the search results, here's what applies to your situation:

**The provision exists.** Under Mexico's Family Unity (Unidad Familiar) rules, parents of foreign permanent residents can apply for **permanent residency directly** — not temporary. This is codified in INM regulations: "Visa for permanent residency for the following Mexican relatives and foreigners holders of the condition of stay of permanent resident: a) parents"

**Two pathways depending on your current location:**

**If you're already in Mexico** (on a visitor permit/FMM):
You can apply for change of status to permanent resident at your local INM office without leaving. The procedure is "Change of status to permanent resident by Family Relationship" for foreign persons holding visitor status who prove a family relationship with a foreign permanent resident.

**If you're outside Mexico:**
Your son initiates the process by filing with INM. Once INM issues authorization, the foreign person must contact a Mexican Consulate and schedule an appointment within 30 business days.

**Required documents:**

* Your son's original and valid permanent resident card
* Original birth certificate proving parent-child relationship (your son's birth certificate showing you as parent)
* Birth certificate must be apostilled and translated into Spanish if issued in another language
* Your valid passport
* Application forms from INM website
* Fees (~$1,780 MXN for processing, additional for resident card issuance)

**Critical recent change (as of late 2024):** Both the foreign applicant and the permanent resident must now appear in person to complete the process. Previously, only the applicant was required to appear.

**Clarifying question:** Are you currently in Mexico on a visitor permit, or outside Mexico? This determines which pathway applies.

### Claude · 2025-12-06 18:02

They're asking if you have a **guarantor (aval/fiador)**.

An aval is a co-signer, or "aval," needed to guarantee the tenant's compliance with the terms of the lease. The guarantor, a Mexican property owner, promises to pay the rent if the renter defaults.

**Requirements for an aval:**
Usually, they must be a property owner in the city or state where you plan to rent as well as a Mexican citizen.

**Why they're asking:** Having an aval may bypass or reduce their concern about your residency documentation. The aval assumes financial liability, which reduces their risk.

**Potential solution:** Your son's wife (the Mexican citizen). If she owns property in Querétaro, she could potentially serve as your aval. Or she may know a property-owning Mexican who would be willing.

**If you don't have an aval:**
Some landlords are open to negotiating and it doesn't hurt to ask. Alternatives include paying multiple months upfront or using a póliza jurídica service that acts as a guarantor (for a fee, typically 30-50% of one month's rent).

### Millard · 2025-12-06 18:12

why is owning property a requirement for a person to act as aval?

### Claude · 2025-12-06 18:12

The aval guarantees payment if you default. The landlord wants assurance that the guarantee is enforceable.

A person with property has an **attachable asset** — something fixed, locatable, and of sufficient value that can be pursued through legal action (embargo/lien) if the aval fails to pay. Someone without property could claim insolvency or simply disappear.

The property functions as implicit collateral backing the guarantee, even though it's not formally pledged.
