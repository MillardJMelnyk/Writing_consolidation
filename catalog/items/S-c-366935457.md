Cultism continues to be the most unacknowledged pandemic in human history. Cultism is as cultism thinks, whether or not what it does is considered cultic.

> **Quoted note — , 2026-10-20:** [link](https://substack.com/@/note/c-366010996)  
> Well, let me give a first tentative response by quoting a few statements I made in posts on X on October 14, 2026:
> 
> "So many bots and not-so-rigorous minds want to know if I "believe in viruses", if I have "chosen a side".
> 
> It's not a religion. I deal in evidence and logic, not cult membership.
> 
> One relevant principle is: Absence of evidence is not proof of absence. I take that seriously.
> 
> Your time would be better spent learning by study rather than recruiting members."
> 
> Followed up by:
> 
> "Just to be clear, my sole purpose in this post was to push back against the obnoxious repeated demands that I side with a cult and profess my adherence.
> 
> The actual debate of the issue is a separate matter. There is much evidence (valid or not) that [an entity they call] viruses play a role in respiratory conditions and the said evidence can be critically assessed. So far, I have not found a conclusive body of evidence, and my critical evaluation of the science continues. I have written about it for those who read. And I plan more written reports. The Koch test is a valid positivity test but it is not the only route to demonstrate viruses [if they exist]. A demonstration does not need to involve separation and purification. That is why invalidating the proposed evidence is more complex than the meme argument anchored in Koch."
> 
> Basically, I am slow because I am rigorous. Still working on it. When I conclude, I will make my arguments explicit.
> 
> The absence of spread and contagion we already proved, so the virus thing is important only as a propaganda and financial-manipulation device, but it is not fundamentally important if you think about it. Why obsess on their invisible agent? And why obsess over me expressing a belief?
