Someone wanted clarification between the deprivation that will occur under share-first humanness vs. deprive-first ownership.

Share-first doesn’t eliminate deprivation, by mere virtue of physics. You can’t sit at the head of the table if someone else is sitting there. You can’t eat the slice of pizza I just took a big bite out of.

This is *functional* deprivation. If there’s only one between the two of us, either we divide and share, or we deprive one of us.

People need to remember that the lessons in sharing and fairness that Mommy and Daddy taught us are just as true now as they were then.

---------------------------------

The whole point of share-first is to *maximize cooperative access* (sharing) while *minimizing* deprivation.

Deprive-first ownership does *exactly* the opposite.

***So, here are variations on an example scenario: pizza dinner.***

---------------------------------

**Functional deprivation**

The family orders pizza for dinner. Plenty of pizza. Dad puts the pizza box on the table and opens it. You and I spy the same piece, but you pick it up before I can reach. You deprived me of that slice. I'm not, however, deprived of all slices, so I choose a different slice.

We're both eating pizza.

---------------------------------

**Deprivation-first by ownership**

Dad declares the pizza belongs to him. He owns it. He can do whatever he wants with it, because he bought it. We're only allowed a piece if he says so, and we only get as many pieces as he allows us. Dad puts the pizza box on the table and opens it. All eyes are on Dad to see what he says.

No one is eating pizza.

---------------------------------

See the difference?
