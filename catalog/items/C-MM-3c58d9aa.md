### Claude · 2026-09-28 13:42

|  | **Arab (Islamic) trade** | **Atlantic (Euro-American) trade** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People taken | 11–17 million | 12.5 million shipped, 10.7 million landed |
| Time span | ~1,250 years (650–1900) | ~350 years (1500–1866) |
| Average per year | ~10,000 (surged in the 1800s) | ~30,000 (peak ~80,000 in the 1780s) |
| Death before sale | 20–50% on desert marches and boats | 10–15% at sea (~1.8 million), plus heavy losses on marches and in coastal pens |
| Death soon after arrival | Castration killed most boys cut, often put at 60–90% | ~1 in 3 died in the first 3 years in Caribbean and Brazil |
| Life as a slave | Mostly household, harem, and soldier work. The worst was plantation and mine labor (Iraq marshes in the 800s, Saharan salt mines, Zanzibar cloves in the 1800s), which was high-death and constantly restocked. | Caribbean and Brazil sugar: deaths outran births, typical survival 7–10 years after arrival. US: population grew from ~400,000 imported to ~4 million by 1860. |
| Children | Few. Men were castrated, and a concubine's child by her owner was born free. | Born slaves (US, Caribbean, Brazil) |
| Freedom | Common, rewarded by religion | Rare in the US, more common in Brazil |
| Descendants today | Small communities. Most lines died out or blended in. | ~200 million people of African descent in the Americas |

**Bottom line:** Both took similar numbers. The Atlantic trade moved people faster. The Arab trade killed more in transit and through castration. Caribbean and Brazilian sugar worked people to death. The US grew its slave population by births. The Arab system left few descendants.

Arab-trade figures are far less certain than Atlantic figures.
