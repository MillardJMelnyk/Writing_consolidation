### Millard · 2026-02-21 16:34

as a rule in trading currencies/CFDs, when USD is the 2nd of the pair, points=pennies, and pips=dimes, right?

### Claude · 2026-02-21 16:34

Saturday, February 21, 2026, approximately 12:00 PM CST.

The ratio analogy is correct: 10 points = 1 pip, just as 10 pennies = 1 dime. A point (pipette) is 1/10th of a pip.

But the dollar-value mapping only holds at a specific lot size. The actual cash value per pip/point shifts with position size:

| Lot Size | 1 Pip ($) | 1 Point ($) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard (100K) | $10 | $1 |
| Mini (10K) | $1 | $0.10 |
| Micro (1K) | $0.10 | $0.01 |

So "points = pennies, pips = dimes" is literally true only at **micro lot** (1,000 units) when USD is the quote currency.
