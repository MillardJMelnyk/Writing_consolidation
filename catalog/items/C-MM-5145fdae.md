does best buy ship to mexico?

### Claude · 2026-04-11 16:41

Based on my knowledge, Best Buy's international shipping policies may have changed, so let me check for the most current information.

No. Best Buy ships to the 50 US states, Guam, Virgin Islands, and military addresses, but does not ship to Mexico. Best Buy México also ceased operations in the country.

If you still want to buy from Best Buy, there are a couple of workarounds:

**Order for U.S. pickup or delivery:** International customers can place orders on BestBuy.com and have them shipped to a U.S. mailing address, or pick up at a U.S. store. If you're near the border, that could work.

**Use a package forwarding service:** You can use forwarding services that give you a U.S. physical address — you ship your Best Buy order there, and they forward it to Mexico. Some services popular in Mexico for this include Shopeando, Ship7, and MyUS. Keep in mind you'd pay extra for the forwarding fee plus customs/import duties.

### Claude · 2026-04-11 16:44

Here's what PayPal Credit currently looks like:

**Standard APR:** As of January 1, 2026, the purchase APR is 29.64%, which is variable (prime rate plus 22.89%) and will not exceed 35.99%.

**Penalty APR:** The penalty APR is 34.24% (as of the same date), also capped at 35.99%, and can be triggered by a late payment. Once applied, it may remain in effect indefinitely.

**Promotional financing:** PayPal Credit offers 6 months of special financing (0% interest) on all purchases of $149 or more. The catch is that interest gets charged retroactively from the purchase date if you don't pay off the promotional balance in full within that period.

So the short version: it's around 29.64% variable APR, with a 0% promo option on purchases over $149 if you pay it off within 6 months. Pretty standard for store/PayPal financing — not cheap if you carry a balance.

Penalty APR what's that?

### Claude · 2026-04-11 16:44

A penalty APR is a higher interest rate that kicks in when you violate your card agreement — most commonly by making a late payment. Think of it as the "you broke the rules" rate.

So with PayPal Credit, your normal rate is ~29.64%, but if you miss a payment, they can bump you up to 34.24%. And once it's applied, it may stay in effect indefinitely — meaning they're not obligated to drop you back down to the regular rate.

It's not unique to PayPal — most credit cards have a penalty APR. It's just one more reason to set up autopay or at least calendar reminders if you're carrying a balance.
