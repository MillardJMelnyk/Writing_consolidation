### Claude · 2026-09-14 15:01

No — the Wittgenstein part is wrong on two counts.

The term is "language-games" (*Sprachspiele*), not "word games," and it wasn't a put-down of philosophy. It was his account of how meaning actually works: words get their sense from their use inside a practice. What he said about philosophy specifically is that its problems come from language running idle — grammar misleading us — and that the job is to dissolve those confusions, not to build theory.

And the second half runs against him. Language-games are anchored in "forms of life" — shared practice, training, doing things with other people. That's the opposite of a free-floating narrative layer with no necessary connection to the world. He's also the one who argued a purely private, unanchored language can't function at all.

The "we paste a story over reality" idea is real but it's Nietzsche/constructivist territory, not his.

Separately: "the entire landscape" has no referent in this turn — I don't know what you're pointing at.
