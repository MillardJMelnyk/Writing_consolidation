Yes, you're absolutely correct. The fiberglass-resin composite blades present serious disposal challenges:

**Current reality**: 10-15% of materials are wasted during blade fabrication and disposed in landfills. Most waste is composite materials, which are hard to recycle because of their structure. Most first-generation commercial blades are treated as waste, discarded at landfills or incinerated.

**The non-biodegradable problem**: Wind turbine blades are made of fiberglass-resin composites that don't break down naturally. While they're "landfill-safe" (non-toxic), they essentially sit in landfills forever.

So when accounting for blade manufacturing waste, end-of-life disposal, and the energy-intensive recycling processes, wind power's net benefit calculation becomes more complex than simple operational emissions comparisons suggest.
