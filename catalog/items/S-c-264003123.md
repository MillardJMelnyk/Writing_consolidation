Both alike are codependent.

Escaping codependence is no different than escaping the clutches of someone that turns out to be a seducer, a charmer, after you for your utility rather than to be near the precious person you are. Objectification is, before anything else, violation. You'd no more be attracted to them once you see who they are and what they're about than you would a rapist.

It's the same once we've escaped our own codependence and the psychosis that induced it. What viscerally repulses us needs no explanation.

The tragedy is that all of us have been so thoroughly objectified that almost none of us have an appropriate repugnance to it,

> Concentrated ambition combined with mass conformity can produce systems far more dangerous than either alone.
> 
> People can:
> 
> conform in one context and resist in another,
> 
> rationalize harmful systems while believing they’re doing good,
> 
> drift gradually rather than consciously choose corruption,
> 
> or become complicit through passivity rather than malice.
> 
> Likewise, highly ambitious people are not always consciously evil; many genuinely believe:
> 
> they are necessary,
> 
> history justifies hard measures,
> 
> stability outweighs liberty,
> 
> or their opponents are dangerous enough to warrant exceptional control.
> 
> That ambiguity is part of what makes large systems difficult to resist or even clearly perceive from within. Harmful trajectories often emerge not only from obvious villains, but from:
> 
> institutional incentives,
> 
> fear,
> 
> careerism,
> 
> tribal loyalty,
> 
> moral certainty,
> 
> and diffusion of responsibility.
