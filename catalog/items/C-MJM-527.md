### Millard · 2025-12-25 18:41

Which epidemic plague creates boils?

### Claude · 2025-12-25 18:42

Bubonic plague. Caused by *Yersinia pestis*, it produces **buboes**—swollen, painful lymph nodes (groin, armpit, neck) that can enlarge to egg-sized masses and sometimes ulcerate, resembling large boils.

When was ICE created?

### Claude · 2025-12-25 18:44

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) was created March 1, 2003, under the Homeland Security Act of 2002, as part of the newly formed Department of Homeland Security.

### Millard · 2025-12-25 18:45

So, under Bush. What did Obama do either to reduce/limit ICE or to strengthen and expand it?

### Claude · 2025-12-25 18:45

Obama did both—expanded capacity and volume while attempting to narrow targeting.

**Expansion/Strengthening:**

* Fiscal year 2009-2016 saw more than 2.7 million deportations—more people than any other president in U.S. history.
* Expanded the Secure Communities initiative from 14 jurisdictions in 2008 to more than 660, on track for all jurisdictions nationwide by 2013.
* Criminal deportations increased by more than 80 percent over previous administration.
* Nonjudicial removals reached an all-time high of 313,000 in FY 2012 (compared to 1,400 in 1995).
* An all-time high number of deportations led advocates to refer to him as the "deporter in chief."

**Limiting/Prioritization Attempts:**

* Issued enforcement priority memos (Morton memos 2010-2011, DHS-wide memo 2014) directing focus toward criminals, national security threats, and recent border crossers.
* Created DACA (2012) providing deportation protection for certain childhood arrivals.
* Eliminated large worksite raids widely used during Bush; emphasized investigating and penalizing employers instead.
* Ended Secure Communities program in November 2014, replaced it with Priority Enforcement Program (PEP) intended to focus on serious criminal convictions.
* ICE was instructed to arrest only people targeted in advance, not collateral arrests.

Net: Obama inherited and significantly expanded ICE's enforcement infrastructure and deportation volume while rhetorically and administratively attempting to constrain whom that expanded apparatus targeted.

### Millard · 2025-12-25 18:47

What about Trump and Biden?

### Claude · 2025-12-25 18:48

**Trump (First Term: 2017-2021):**

* Immediately after Trump became president, he signed an executive order to triple the size of the agency's deportation staff by hiring 10,000 officers (this did not happen, and the number of officers as of 2025 was about 6,100).
* The first Trump administration ordered the termination of PEP and restoration of Secure Communities, and expanded federal enforcement priorities to effectively target for arrest and removal any noncitizen with an immigration violation.
* ICE arrests of individuals either known to be or suspected of being in the United States illegally greatly increased during Trump's first term. The number of arrests ultimately remained well under those made during the Obama administration.
* Ended Obama's prioritization system—virtually everyone became a target.
* Family separation policy at the border (rescinded by Biden).

**Biden (2021-2025):**

* On January 20, 2021, President Biden revoked President Trump's 2017 executive order on immigration enforcement priorities and directed DHS to implement new policies intended to balance border security, public safety, and humanitarian considerations.
* Under Biden, ICE would primarily apprehend and remove people who pose a threat to national security, committed crimes designated as "aggravated" felonies or recently crossed the border.
* The interim priorities reduced the number of ICE arrests to approximately 72,000 in FY 2021, less than one-half the number of arrests in FY 2018 and less than one-quarter their peak under Obama, in FY 2011.
* On February 7, 2021, Biden began the implementation of new guidelines forbidding or restricting ICE from seeking out deportations on the basis of "drug based crimes (less serious offenses), simple assault, DUI, money laundering, property crimes, fraud, tax crimes, solicitation, or charges without convictions."
* On December 19, 2024, ICE reported that deportations had surged to a 10-year high under the Biden administration with 271,000 unauthorized immigrants deported in fiscal year 2024, surpassing the Trump-era high of 267,000 deportations in 2019.
* Courts blocked or challenged Biden's prioritization guidelines; Texas lawsuit reached Supreme Court, which ultimately upheld executive authority to set priorities.

**Trump (Second Term: 2025-present):**

* The Trump administration reversed the policy of the previous administration and gave ICE permission to raid schools, hospitals and places of worship.
* The Republican-led Congress set aside roughly $170 billion for immigration enforcement and border security efforts through the "One Big Beautiful Bill," including $75 billion in extra funding for ICE, making it by far the highest-funded law enforcement agency in the federal government.
* The law gives ICE $45 billion to expand its detention system over the next four years. The money could allow ICE to hold more than 100,000 detainees at any given time, roughly doubling the current capacity.
* The Trump administration has expanded the use of fast-track deportation powers (expedited removal) in unprecedented ways to remove unauthorized immigrants in as little as a few hours, on the decision of individual DHS personnel, and without judicial review.
* The Trump administration is granting ICE access to a swath of government databases that were previously off limits for immigration enforcement, including sensitive tax and Social Security records.
