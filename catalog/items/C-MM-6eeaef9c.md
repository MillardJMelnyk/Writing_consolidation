### Millard · 2026-08-21 04:37

how much alchohol do countries drink?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 04:37

Measured in litres of pure alcohol per adult (15+) per year:

* World average: roughly 5–6 litres.
* Top drinkers: Eastern and Central Europe — Romania around 17 litres, Georgia around 14, Czechia next, with Latvia, Lithuania, Austria, Germany and France all in the 11–13 range.
* OECD average was 8.5 litres in 2023. US about 9.8, UK and Spain similar.
* Mexico: roughly 5 litres — below the global pattern for the Americas, because a large share of the adult population doesn't drink at all, while those who do drink heavily.
* Bottom: Muslim-majority countries — Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Libya, Afghanistan, Bangladesh, all near zero.

One litre of pure alcohol is about 40 standard beers, so Romania's 17 litres works out to roughly two drinks a day averaged across every adult.

17 liters a year?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 04:38

Yes — but that number is pure ethanol, not 17 litres of drinks. It's the alcohol content stripped out of everything consumed in the year.

17 litres works out to about 47 mL of pure alcohol a day, roughly 2.5 standard drinks — a few beers, or half a bottle of wine, every day, averaged across every adult in the country including non-drinkers.

Two corrections to what I said before: a litre of pure alcohol is about 56 standard beers, not 40. And the 17 figure comes from a ranking site; WHO's own numbers put Romania closer to 12–13 litres. Czechia, Latvia and Moldova are in the same band. The top-of-Europe picture holds either way, but the exact 17 is soft.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 04:40

where does the uk rank?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 04:40

Upper-middle of the pack, not top.

* WHO basis (includes home-brewed and untaxed): 10.7 litres in 2020, about 21 UK units a week. That puts the UK somewhere around 25th–30th globally.
* OECD basis (recorded sales only): 9.3 litres in 2023 — 19th of the OECD countries listed, just above the 8.5 average, behind Portugal, Latvia, Austria, Czechia, Spain, Germany, France and the US.

Two numbers because the two bodies measure differently — WHO adds unrecorded alcohol, OECD doesn't. Neither has the UK near the top; that's still Eastern Europe.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 04:41

where does mexico rank?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 04:42

Low — bottom half globally, and last in North America.

* WHO figure: 6.7 litres per adult in 2024. That's roughly 80th–100th of ~190 countries.
* Lowest in North America; the US is about 9.9.
* OECD puts it near 5 litres — near the bottom of the OECD, comparable to one bottle of wine or 1.9 litres of beer a week per adult.

The average hides the pattern, though. Men average 11.1 litres against 2.1 for women, and roughly half of men and most women don't drink at all — so the drinkers who do drink, drink hard. Also, 77% of it is beer, 20% spirits.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 04:43

where does vietnam rank?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 04:43

Higher than you'd expect — around the UK's level, and rising faster than almost anywhere.

* WHO: 10.72 litres per adult in 2022, peaking at 11.21 in 2017; up 2.65× since 2000. That's roughly 25th–30th globally, above the US, Canada and Italy.
* Highest in ASEAN, alongside Thailand. Well above China, Laos, Thailand, the Philippines and Cambodia.
* Growth of 90% between 2010 and 2017 — fifth-fastest in the world.

Two things behind the number: 91% of it is beer, and a large chunk is unrecorded homemade rice spirit — 5.3 of the 8.3 litres in 2016 was untaxed. Men average 14.5 litres to women's 2.5, so the male figure alone is Romania-level.
