### Millard · 2026-09-24 23:08

Is there any truth to this claim that Ethiopian black seed oil can rid you of parasites in a matter of days? "Thymoquinone"

### Claude · 2026-09-24 23:08

Highly unlikely: thymoquinone kills some parasites in lab dishes and animal studies, and one small old study in children with tapeworms showed reduced egg counts, but no solid human trials show black seed oil clearing parasites in days, and "Ethiopian" is just a sourcing label, not a stronger version.

### Millard · 2026-09-24 23:09

Why hasn't it been studied

### Claude · 2026-09-24 23:09

Likely because nobody can patent black seed oil, so no company will pay for large human trials, and cheap, effective anti-parasite drugs (albendazole, praziquantel, ivermectin) already exist, making it hard to justify testing an unproven oil against them.

### Millard · 2026-09-24 23:13

I've been struggling for months with lack of motivation, lack of energy, always tired, I sleep 9 to 10 hours a night and still no energy, brain fog, dread doing the smallest tasks, etc. what are the chances this is caused by a parasite?

### Claude · 2026-09-24 23:14

Unlikely, unless you also have gut symptoms (diarrhea, bloating, cramps, weight loss); your pattern of oversleeping, fog, and dreading small tasks fits depression, low thyroid, anemia, low B12 or vitamin D, or sleep apnea far better.

The check that settles it: ask a lab (Salud Digna or Chopo, both cheap) for a blood panel with CBC, TSH, ferritin, B12, vitamin D, and glucose/A1c, plus a *coproparasitoscópico en serie de 3* (three-sample stool test) to rule parasites in or out.

Have you considered depression? Months of this with no physical cause turning up would point there, and it's very treatable.

### Millard · 2026-09-24 23:15

I'm on 100 mg per day of sertraline already, have been for years, I'm used to going through periodic spells of these symptoms but they usually last only weeks to a couple of months, never this long.

### Claude · 2026-09-24 23:15

That makes this a longer-than-usual depressive episode breaking through your medication, which is the most likely explanation, and it's a reason to see whoever prescribes your sertraline now rather than wait it out.

Things they can do: raise the dose (100 mg is mid-range, there's room above it), add a second medication, or switch. Long-term SSRI use can also cause its own flatness and low drive (called SSRI-induced apathy), which looks a lot like what you describe, so tell them the symptoms feel different this time.

Still get the blood panel: low thyroid, low B12, low vitamin D, or anemia can drag down an otherwise working antidepressant, and fixing them is cheap.
