Joy Garner's Control Group Survey (2019–early 2020) recruited unvaccinated Americans through anti-mandate rallies, social media, podcasts, and radio, and compared their self-reported rates to national figures. It reported that only 5.97% of unvaccinated adults had any chronic condition, compared to 60% in the vaccinated population, and 0% autism. The main weakness is that respondents chose themselves and nothing was medically checked, so the gap is likely inflated (Likely), though it doesn't by itself show how large the real difference is.

### Claude · 2026-10-31 13:51

Three reasons push it up and nothing comparable pushes it down. First, her respondents came through anti-mandate rallies and anti-vaccine media, and the survey's stated purpose was to show the unvaccinated are healthier, so families whose unvaccinated kids were sick had little reason to answer and healthy ones had every reason. Third, self-report lets respondents leave out conditions they don't count, don't know about, or would rather not admit, while medical records list every diagnosis made. The one force that could push the other way is sick people answering health surveys more eagerly, but her framing made that weak, so Likely up.
