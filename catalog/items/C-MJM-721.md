### Claude · 2026-08-06 17:26

Wrong. "Burden of proof" isn't a legal artifact—it's an epistemic norm: the party asserting a claim owes justification for it, proportional to the claim's specificity and its deviation from priors. Facts don't cease needing evidential support just because they're facts rather than legal charges. Whether X is true is independent of anyone's obligations; whether you're justified in believing X is not.

The text's move—"why wouldn't they"—inverts this norm specifically. It shifts the justificatory burden from the claimant (who asserts weather modification, coordinated food adulteration, bloodline networks) onto the skeptic, who is now required to disprove a specific, unfalsifiable coordination thesis rather than the author needing to establish it. That's the error I flagged, not a claim about legal procedure.
