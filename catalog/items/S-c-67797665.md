The widespread insistence on vindicating this thing we call “authority” is baffling to me.

Let’s consider what many have called the “good kind” of authority (or “valid” or “legitimate” or “beneficial” and oodles of other exonerating adjectives).

First, what possesses us to call the “good kind” and the “bad kind” as if they were just two flavors of the same thing? Should we call both sugar and arsenic different flavors of the same thing and label the jar “SWEET STUFF”?

The difference between “good authority” and “bad authority” is no less organic, striking, and uber-opposite than sugar and arsenic.

But let’s give the “good authority” idea a chance. Check out what ChatGPT has to say about it.

So, let’s consider what authority would be if it were stripped of threat, coercion, punishment/reward, and violence. Consider the kinds of things that elicit/evoke the desire to comply with, follow, attach ourselves to, and emulate a “good authority”.

Isn’t it interesting (and when you start to think about it, baffling and galling) that we don’t have an English word, a counterpart to “authority” that would make the difference between “good” and “bad” authority crystal clear? Why the omission?

Or, look at it this way: Let’s say we had a jar labeled “SWEET STUFF” which contained a mixture of sugar and arsenic. (This will also point out the stupidity of the argument that “authority is mostly a good thing”, because even if the ratio of sugar to arsenic were 100,000:1, or even 100,000,000:1, would you still sprinkle it on your children’s cereal?)

So, let’s also say that we have a sure-fire method of extracting 100% of the arsenic from the sweet stuff, leaving 100% pure sugar. (And come on now, even if you think that sugar is also a poison, this is an allegory for explanatory purposes, and no way is sugar a more poisonous poison than arsenic.)

Is there any intelligent reason why you wouldn’t extract the arsenic, put it in its own jar and label it “POISON: ARSENIC”, and put the 100% pure sugar in its own jar and label it “SUGAR”??

Of course you would. And you’d probably get the arsenic the hell out of your home. Maybe you’d give it to the chemistry lab of a pest control company or a military research facility (or something) where they experiment with different ways to exterminate life.

So, what should we do with cannot-but-be-poisonous “authority”?

I think the answer is clear, since all you need to do is quantify the usage of the word and its application in real life to see that when people say “authority”, they’re talking about “bad” authority at least 98% of the time. (I’m being generously conservative: I think the real number would turn out to be 99.999% of the time or more, but feel free to do your own work on that one.)

So, statistically, “authority” almost always refers to “bad” authority. I see no reason to mess with that equivalence. I just think we should make it clear and unambiguous.

That would leave us short a word, because none of the words that ChatGPT offered is unique to a “good” kind of authority. In fact, as long as they’re threat-, coercion-, punishment/reward-, and violence-free, I’m pretty sure that none of those would connote or raise associations to authority much. Some, like mentorship and leadership, might—unless you’d experienced authority-free mentorship and leadership and realized that it doesn’t take authority to mentor or lead. And yeah, I’d agree that there is precious little mentorship or leadership in this world that’s free of bad authority.

But that’s not the important question, is it?

The important question isn’t isn’t, “Is there any such thing?” but rather, “*Could* there be any such thing?”

In my mind, anyone that would answer the important question with an emphatic, committed, “No! There could never be!” without even considering it, let alone investigating it, is a cultist; because *refusing to look is what makes people cultists*.

And that’s just another way of saying *fools* (according to the Biblical book of Proverbs, anyway.)
