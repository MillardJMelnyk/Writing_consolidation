### Millard · 2026-08-21 14:02

Who regulates the production quality and distribution of vaccines?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 14:02

National drug regulators — COFEPRIS in Mexico, FDA in the US, EMA in the EU — license the manufacturing plants, inspect them for GMP compliance, and release each batch, while WHO prequalification governs vaccines bought through UN agencies and distribution falls to the same regulators plus each country's health ministry.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 14:02

That's a completely misleading answer.

What does "and release each batch," mean?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 14:02

Vaccines are sold in lots, and for each lot a government lab retests samples and issues a certificate before that lot can leave the factory — the manufacturer's own quality sign-off isn't enough.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 14:05

and where is the proof that was actually done?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 14:05

The lot release certificate itself — signed by the national control lab, on file with the manufacturer and the regulator, and obtainable in the US through FDA lot release records and in the EU through the OMCL/EU Official Control Authority Batch Release certificate; whether the testing behind any given certificate was actually performed as claimed rests on inspection records, not on anything a buyer can independently verify.

### Millard · 2026-08-21 14:06

How difficult would it be for a pharma company to compromise this oversight/QA setup?

### Claude · 2026-08-21 14:06

Easier than the system implies, but not trivially so.

Weak points, in order of how often they've actually failed: the plant's own records — batch records, sterility results, stability data — are written by the company, and inspectors see them only during scheduled visits, so falsification there is the documented failure mode (Ranbaxy pled guilty in 2013 to fabricating data; Emergent BioSolutions' Baltimore plant destroyed millions of COVID doses in 2021 after cross-contamination FDA found only on inspection).

The harder point to compromise is lot release, because the state lab tests its own samples with its own methods. Defeating that requires either sending a non-representative sample or corrupting the lab itself, which means multiple people across two organizations rather than one QA manager.

Hardest of all is a systematic, multi-country conspiracy — EU, US, WHO-prequalification labs test independently and don't share staff or budgets.

So: a single company hiding contamination or bad stability at one plant is realistic and has happened repeatedly. A company faking safety or efficacy across the whole global release chain is a much bigger lift, and the historical failures are the first kind, not the second.
