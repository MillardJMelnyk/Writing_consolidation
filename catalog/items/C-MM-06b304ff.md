### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:14

Paul said, “I don't like taking the bus”

can you convert this to reported speech?

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:14

Paul said (that) he didn't like taking the bus.

### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:14

could i use doesn't?

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:14

No — in reported speech the tense normally shifts back (present → past), so "doesn't" becomes "didn't." "Doesn't" would only stay if the statement is still true/general/timeless and you want to keep it in present tense (common when reporting facts), e.g. "Paul said he doesn't like taking the bus" is also acceptable in everyday English, especially if his dislike is still current. Both are fine — "didn't" is the standard textbook backshift, "doesn't" keeps the immediacy.

### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:14

but what if it's a general staement as in he generally does not like to take the bus?

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:15

Yes — if it's a general/habitual truth (not a one-time complaint), you can keep the present tense: "Paul said he doesn't like taking the bus." Backshift is optional for general truths/facts; native speakers often skip it precisely to show the statement is still generally true, not just something he felt in that moment.

### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:15

"Paul said he doesn't like taking the bus." so this structure of reported speech is also ok then correct?

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:15

Yes, that's correct and natural.

### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:17

Bill and Jane said, “ We are going to Spain this summer.”

Is this ok for an example to practice converting it into reported speech?

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:17

Yes, that works well for practice — it has a pronoun (we), a modal-ish future form (are going to), and a time expression (this summer), so it tests several backshift/change rules at once (we→they, are going to→were going to, this summer→that summer).

### Millard · 2026-08-13 21:17

and the fact bothe Bill and Jane said this together isnt an issue/

### Claude · 2026-08-13 21:17

Correct, not an issue — plural subject just takes "they said" and "we"→"they" as normal.
