### Claude · 2025-09-20 15:49

You're correct, and I apologize for the confusion. There are indeed important distinctions:

* **"Mutually exclusive"** means two things cannot exist or occur at the same time—they're logically incompatible by definition
* **"Antithetical"** means two things are in direct opposition or contradiction to each other—they represent opposing principles or values

So something could be antithetical (opposing in nature/principle) without being strictly mutually exclusive (unable to coexist), and vice versa.

In your sentence, if you're using both terms intentionally, you're emphasizing both that the alternative and codependence represent opposing principles AND that they cannot coexist. That's a stronger, more nuanced statement than either term alone would provide.
