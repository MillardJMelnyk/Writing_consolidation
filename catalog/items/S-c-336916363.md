Most people, I would think, consider statements like these as proof that our current systems have severely malfunctioned.

I don't, because that would imply they were intended to do other than they do.

If they were otherwise intended, they would have been fixed already -- or else those responsible for them are bloody incompetent, idiotic, and useless.

So, you get your choice: either the systems and their creators and operators -- your "leaders" -- are evil, or they're completely out of control, being bumbling fucking morons.

Lunatics, actually, because only lunatics could want and intend the kind of harm these systems do with oodles of support and cooperation but practically zero resistance.

Either way, why do you insist on "following" such "leaders" and defending them?

I have never once heard a convincing, cogent, competent answer to that question. All answers ended up boiling down to codependence.

But that entire discussion is completely beside the point that really matters.

What matters is our psychosis.

The way we *think* about our systems is not just irrational. Irrationality implies randomness, lack of control and sense, so therefore, lack of consistent, reliable direction. No, this is *perverse* irrationality: consistently precise *opposition* to what's beneficial, functional, and sane.

The proof?

In every case of horrific evildoing, by the time our systems, whether they're moral or religious or legal or social, come around to finally admitting what's been happening -- long before anyone has even begun to deal with it, having only just admitted it -- the evil's been done.

No one is protected, no evils are prevented, no victims are saved -- nothing.

This isn't an accident or an unfortunate limitation or an unavoidable inevitability.

This is the design. We create systems that guarantee that EVERYONE will get away with it for at least a while. And then we apply a completely self-serving, nonsensical, and useless practice called "punishment", as if it makes things better when all it does is increase net detriment.

We're convinced this is the best we can do and better than doing nothing at all -- but that's not our psychosis.

Our psychosis is that we refuse to investigate completely different kinds of systems, being certain before any investigation that there's no point in investigating them, and never once wonder how we became so certain or whether the sources of our certainty are wise or crazy.
