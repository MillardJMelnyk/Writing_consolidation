### Claude · 2026-09-29 17:28

At the level of core epistemic premises, Pepper's *World Hypotheses* (1942) rests on six claims. First, no knowledge claim is self-evident or indubitable, so both the dogmatist and the utter skeptic are rejected. Second, knowledge is refined common sense, backed either by multiplicative corroboration (observers agreeing) or structural corroboration (evidence converging within a theory), and world hypotheses depend on the structural kind. Third, a world hypothesis must be unlimited in scope, able to interpret any fact whatsoever. Fourth, every such hypothesis grows from a root metaphor, a familiar area of common-sense fact used as the model for everything. Fifth, hypotheses are judged by precision and scope, which leaves four relatively adequate ones, each irreducible to the others: formism (similarity), mechanism (the machine), contextualism (the historic event), and organicism (organic wholeness). Sixth, mixing them (eclecticism) only produces confusion, so they must be kept separate.
