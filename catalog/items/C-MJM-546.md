### Claude · 2025-12-29 23:27

Critical issue: **Being outside the US for over a year does not automatically end your US tax residency as a green card holder.**

**For immigration purposes (USCIS):** Your green card may be considered abandoned after 1 year abroad without a re-entry permit.

**For tax purposes (IRS):** You remain a US tax resident until you **formally abandon** the green card by:

* Filing Form I-407 (Record of Abandonment of Lawful Permanent Resident Status)
* Or having it administratively/judicially revoked

Until you do this, the IRS considers you a US tax resident owing tax on worldwide income.

**Additional complication—Long-term resident rules:**
If you held your green card for 8 or more of the last 15 years, you're classified as a "long-term resident." Upon expatriation:

* You must file Form 8854
* You may be subject to an **exit tax** (mark-to-market on worldwide assets above ~$866,000 exclusion threshold as of 2024)

**Regarding Outschool specifically:**
Even after proper expatriation, income earned from a US company for services may still be US-source income subject to 30% withholding (potentially reduced under Canada-US tax treaty).

### Claude · 2025-12-29 23:46

Yes. Mexico has a progressive income tax (Impuesto Sobre la Renta / ISR).

**2025 rates for residents:**

* ISR rates range from 1.92% to 35% based on income brackets.

**Tax residency:**
The Mexican government will consider you to be a tax resident if you spend more than 183 days in Mexico within a year, maintain a residence in Mexico, or if the country is the center of your vital interests.

**Residents vs. non-residents:**

* Tax residents of Mexico pay income tax on worldwide income. Non-residents pay tax only on Mexico-sourced income.

**Relevant to your situation:**
Once you become a Mexican permanent resident and spend 183+ days there, you'll be taxed on worldwide income by Mexico. If you're teaching Outschool students (US-source income), both countries may have claims on that income. There's a Mexico-US tax treaty that may provide relief from double taxation, but since you're Canadian, you'd need to verify treaty applicability based on your residency and citizenship status.

### Claude · 2025-12-29 23:49

Correct. Under the US-Mexico tax treaty, social security benefits and other public pensions paid by a Contracting State to a resident of the other Contracting State shall be taxable only in the first-mentioned State.

This means US Social Security payments to a Mexican resident are taxable **only by the US**, not Mexico.

**Complication for you:** You're Canadian, not American. However, the treaty language protects "residents of the other Contracting State"—it doesn't require citizenship. As a Mexican resident receiving US Social Security, the treaty should shield that income from Mexican tax.

**Separate question you didn't ask:** Whether the US will actually tax your Social Security after you expatriate. As a non-resident alien (post-LPR abandonment), US taxation of your Social Security benefits depends on additional factors including treaty provisions and IRS rules for NRAs receiving SS. That's a distinct issue from Mexican taxation.
