how do I tell if my PC is a Copilot+ PC

### Claude · 2025-07-28 14:47

Here's how to check if your PC is a Copilot+ PC:

**Key Requirements for Copilot+ PCs:**
--------------------------------------

Copilot+ is the term for several different next-gen AI features that only work on a computer with an NPU (Neural Processing Unit) that's capable of 40 TOPS or higher.

To qualify as a Copilot+ PC, devices must have incredible performance and battery life, a Copilot key embedded into the keyboard, run Windows 11, and have a Pluton security processor enabled by default. Crucially, these devices need to meet the performance requirements of 40 TOPS (Trillion Operations Per Second).

**How to Check:**
-----------------

### **1. Use PC Health Check App**

Use the PC Health Check app to see if your PC can run Windows 11. While The PC Health Check tool does not directly show an NPU, but it informs you about general Windows 11 compatibility.

### **2. Look for Visual Indicators**

* **Copilot Key**: Check if your keyboard has a dedicated Copilot key
* **Taskbar/Start Menu**: If you do not find Copilot in the taskbar or Start menu, this can indicate your system is an AI PC or not up-to-date enough.

### **3. Check for Copilot+ Features**

Several Copilot+ PC features, such as Windows Studio Effects, can be accessed by selecting Quick Settings from the Taskbar. Other Copilot+ PC features, like Cocreator in Paint, are accessed directly in the application.

### **4. System Specifications**

Look for:

* NPU (Neural Processing Unit) with 40+ TOPS performance
* Windows 11 operating system
* Pluton security processor
* ARM-based processors (Snapdragon X series) or newer Intel/AMD chips with integrated NPUs

**Quick Reality Check:**
------------------------

Most existing PCs are **not** Copilot+ PCs unless they were specifically purchased as such in 2024 or later. Copilot+ PCs are a new category of devices that were announced in 2024, so if your PC is older than that, it's likely not a Copilot+ PC.

The easiest way is to check your PC manufacturer's specifications or look for the dedicated Copilot key on your keyboard - if you don't have one, your PC is not a Copilot+ PC.

can i convert my PC to an AI PC by adding an NPU?

### Claude · 2025-07-28 14:50

**Short answer: No, you cannot convert your existing PC to a true Copilot+ PC by adding an NPU.**

Here's why and what your alternatives are:

**Why You Can't Add an NPU to Existing PCs:**
---------------------------------------------

1. **NPUs are integrated into processors**: Many of the new Windows AI features require an NPU with the ability to run 40+ TOPS, including but not limited to: ... Copilot+ PCs with new AMD and Intel silicon, including AMD Ryzen AI 300 series and Intel Core Ultra 200V series. The NPU is built into the CPU itself, not a separate component.
2. **No PCIe NPU cards exist**: Unlike the old math co-processor days, there are no consumer NPU cards available for standard PCs. The technology is integrated at the processor level.
3. **Your GPU already does AI processing**: Modern GPUs are already virtually identical to the kind of co-processor you'd want to improve AI performance.

**What You Can Do Instead:**
----------------------------

### **1. Use Your GPU for AI**

If you have a strong GPU and power usage is not a concern, you may be better off sticking to your existing setup. Your GPU can handle AI tasks effectively.

### **2. Consider AI Accelerator Cards**

There are some options available:

* The Coral Mini PCIe Accelerator is a half-size Mini PCIe module that brings the Edge TPU coprocessor to existing systems and products with an available Mini PCIe slot. The on-board Edge TPU coprocessor is capable of performing 4 trillion operations (tera-operations) per second (TOPS)
* AI Accelerator PCIe Card is equipped with excellent thermal stability to achieve inference acceleration with multiple Edge TPUs. Low power consumption : 36/52 W ( 8/16 Edge TPUs). No external PSU needed : Power is drawn directly from the PCIe slot.

### **3. Upgrade When Ready**

Engineers already ready for a workstation upgrade have no reason to avoid the latest generation of AI PCs, coming as they do with all the standard generational improvements. However, at this point the NPU itself is not a reason for engineers to upgrade.

**Bottom Line:**
----------------

To get true Copilot+ PC features, you'll need to buy a new computer with an NPU-equipped processor. For semantic document search and most AI tasks, your existing GPU will work fine with the applications I mentioned earlier (like Reor).

Unfortunately, **there are currently no true AI desktop PCs (Copilot+ PCs) available for purchase**. Here's the current situation:

**Current Reality:**
--------------------

All existing Copilot+ PCs are **laptops only**. Copilot+ PCs are a class of Windows AI PCs, laptops, and 2-in-1 PCs but no desktop models exist yet.

**What's Coming for Desktop AI PCs:**
-------------------------------------

### **Intel Arrow Lake Refresh (Late 2025)**

* **Timeline**: Intel is prepping an Arrow Lake refresh for the second half of this year, with chips expected to ships with a slight bump in clock speeds and an entirely new NPU focused on delivering Copilot+ AI prowess.
* **Features**: This will allow them to use Copilot+ features for the first time on the desktop

**However**, there's conflicting information about whether the refresh will actually include the improved NPU: Intel is set to launch the Arrow Lake-S refresh in the latter part of this year and, clashing with previous rumors, it apparently doesn't even have a new NPU. The company might only be overhauling the clock speeds for its upcoming desktop lineup.

**Your Options Right Now:**
---------------------------

### **1. Wait for Intel Arrow Lake Refresh (Q3-Q4 2025)**

* May be the first desktop CPUs with Copilot+ capabilities
* Uncertain if NPU will actually be included

### **2. Build a Desktop with Current GPUs**

* Use existing powerful GPUs for AI tasks
* Won't have official "Copilot+" designation but can run AI applications
* More cost-effective for semantic search applications like Reor

### **3. Consider Mini PCs with Mobile Processors**

* Some compact desktop-style computers use laptop chips with NPUs
* Not traditional desktops but desktop-like form factor

**Bottom Line:**
----------------

If you specifically want a desktop AI PC with NPU, you'll need to wait until late 2025 when Intel potentially releases Arrow Lake Refresh processors. For semantic document search right now, a regular desktop with a good GPU will work perfectly fine with applications like Reor.
