I appreciate your intent and am down 100% with the fact that what is, is now, that's all there is, so that's all the truth could be about. It's kinda like the extremely honest person who couldn't tell you the time because by the time they'd looked up from their watch to open their mouth, the second hand had already moved.

The "just be" sentiment -- to the point it's a denial of sense-making -- is just not enough, though. Your post is the result of sense-making which involved ruminating both about the now, the "past", and potential "futures". It feels much better to experience just being in the now, but you'd have a hard time arguing that such experience is sufficient for us to proceed intelligently and successfully through life, or that our "obsession" with sense-making is pathological. But maybe I'm oversimplifying what you're saying here?
