### Claude · 2025-06-26 15:49

This meme is completely false. Here's what's wrong:

**Main claim**: "There's never been a virus taken directly from a sick human and shown to cause disease in another" - **FALSE**

**Reality**: Viruses were historically isolated by inoculating susceptible laboratory animals or embryonated eggs with small quantities of homogenized tissues or fluids obtained from biological specimens. This has been done countless times since the early 1900s.

**The meme misunderstands virus isolation**: Koch's postulates were presented in 1890, at a time when germ theory was still controversial and before the discovery of viruses, to which Koch's postulates, as they were originally written, do not apply. Scientists developed modified versions specifically for viruses.

**Examples of successful virus isolation**: According to Koch's postulates, as modified by Rivers for viral diseases, six criteria are required to establish a virus as the cause of a disease. The first three criteria — isolation of virus from diseased hosts, cultivation in host cells, and proof of filterability — have been met for many viruses including SARS.

**Bottom line**: Viruses have been isolated from sick humans and shown to cause disease in healthy subjects thousands of times. This includes influenza, polio, measles, HIV, SARS, COVID-19, and many others. The scientific method for proving viral causation is well-established and has been successfully applied for over a century.

The meme promotes scientifically inaccurate conspiracy theories about virology.
