Of course it is.

The more fundamental problem has nothing to do with individualism or interdependence, and everything to do with the premise for "freedom".

As we typically conceive it, "freedom" is a function of *absence of adversity*. In other words: if no one stops us from doing what we want to do, we’re “free”.

Bullshit. That’s not nearly what real freedom is.

It’s a very typical notion, though, one that conforms to the way both children and psychopaths see the world.

Compare that notion of freedom to this one:

We’re free when we can be honest, be genuinely ourselves, and be accepted as we are for who we are without anyone trying to stop us or deny us or diss us or defame us.

Which version involves a deeper, far more powerful kind of freedom?

This kind of freedom takes interdependence to a whole new level, doesn’t it?

Why don’t we have *that* in mind when we think and talk about freedom?
