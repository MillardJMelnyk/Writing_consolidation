In a sane society, we’d have no question about this. Those who seek to control life and living beings have, inescapably, chosen to *harm* them.

How does an obsession with harming life and the living *not* indicate mental illness? We already know that cruelty to animals in childhood is a strong predictor of interpersonal violence and abuse in adulthood.

Before any question of the value or outcomes of controlling life and the living is coherent, the will to control them is, itself, highly suspect. Two major irrationalities are involved.

Supremacism: the decision to control, the agenda to acquire a position that affords control, and effort invested in achieving control rest on the utterly batshit conviction that the controller *should* “be in control”.

Violation of peerness: the elevation to a superior position with the right to control, which is actually an *exaltation* that constitutes supremacism, violates the preciousness, individual sanctity, and integrity of everyone subjected to control, including their most basic human rights.

Those irrationalities are unavoidable and harmful no matter what arguments attempt to justify them. What’s more, all justifications of those harms are premised on the “greater good” delusion: that harm is necessary to produce benefit, that violation is necessary to produce security, that evil is necessary to create good, and that we’re all pathetic and powerless to do anything better than that.

Bullshit. You just made that shit up.
