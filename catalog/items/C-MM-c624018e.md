### Millard · 2026-10-13 19:27

I understand that Rice especially brown rice can be high in arsenic. What effect does cooking the rice have on the arsenic availability when you eat it?

### Claude · 2026-10-13 19:27

Tuesday, October 13, 2026, 1:27 PM CST

Cooking the usual absorption way (all water soaked up) leaves the arsenic in, but boiling in lots of water (6–10 parts water to 1 rice) and pouring the excess off cuts inorganic arsenic about 40–60%, and parboiling for 5 minutes, draining, then finishing in fresh water cuts it about 50% in brown rice and 70% in white (Highly likely, per University of Sheffield and FDA studies).

### Millard · 2026-10-13 19:33

Explain in detail how you parboil

### Claude · 2026-10-13 19:33

Bring 4 cups of water per cup of rice to a full boil, add the brown rice, and boil it for 5 minutes, then pour it into a strainer and dump that water (that's where the arsenic goes). Return the rice to the pot with 2 cups of fresh water per cup of rice, bring it back to a boil, cover, and simmer on low until the water is absorbed and the rice is tender (about 20–30 minutes for brown rice), then let it sit covered 5 minutes before serving.
