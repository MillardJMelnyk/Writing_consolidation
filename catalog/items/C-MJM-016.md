### Claude · 2025-05-29 17:34

I can sometimes identify locations from satellite or aerial images, but it depends on several factors:

**What helps with identification:**

* Distinctive landmarks (famous buildings, unique geological formations)
* Recognizable urban layouts or infrastructure patterns
* Characteristic geographical features combined with context clues
* Very well-known locations that have unique visual signatures

**What makes it difficult:**

* Generic landscapes that could exist in many places (like this archipelago)
* Images without scale references or distinctive features
* Coastal areas, forests, or rural regions that share similar characteristics globally
* Low resolution or limited field of view

In your image, while I can see it's a beautiful archipelago with turquoise water and rocky islands, these features exist in many places worldwide - Scandinavia, Croatia, parts of Canada, Australia, and elsewhere. Without distinctive markers, I can't reliably distinguish between them.

So yes, I can sometimes identify locations from satellite images, but I should be upfront about when the visual information isn't sufficient for a confident identification rather than making educated guesses and presenting them as knowledge.
