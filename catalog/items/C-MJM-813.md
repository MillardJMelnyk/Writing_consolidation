### Claude · 2026-10-24 22:45

Being bigger doesn't change it: readiness depends on motor development (sitting unsupported, head and trunk control), not size, and extra weight makes a tip-over or stair fall hit harder. The pediatrician's one-year line is also generous, since the AAP advises against walkers at any age.

### Claude · 2026-10-24 22:46

Stepping motions while held upright are normal at six months and don't indicate walking readiness (highly likely). The missing balance is the point: the walker supplies it artificially, and studies of walker use link it to later sitting, crawling, and walking milestones (Siegel & Burton 1999; Garrett 2002).

Tip-overs and stairs are only part of the injury record. Walkers also put a baby at counter, stove, and cord height at roughly 3 feet per second. Is every room she can roll into free of those?

Does the pediatrician know about the stepping, and does the walker's listing give a minimum age?
