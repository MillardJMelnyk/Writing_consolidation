### Claude · 2025-12-10 18:44

Your usage: the work done to ascertain the truth—effort expended to verify a claim's accuracy before accepting it.

Let me search for a credible source using the term in that sense.

These results are all social epistemology—distributed labor among communities. Not your sense.

The Wikipedia definition of "intellectual responsibility" (also called epistemic responsibility) is close:

> "the quality of being adequately reflective about the truth of one's beliefs. People are intellectually responsible if they have tried hard enough to be reflective about the truth of their beliefs, aiming not to miss any information that would cause them to abandon those beliefs as false."

But this frames it as a *quality* or *responsibility*, not as "work" per se.

Let me try one more targeted search.

**Result:** No credible source found using "epistemic work" in your sense—the individual effort expended to ascertain a claim's truth before accepting it.

The academic literature uses "epistemic labor" exclusively in the social epistemology sense (distributed knowledge work among communities/groups). The closest established term is "epistemic responsibility" or "intellectual responsibility," but these name the *obligation* to verify, not the *effort itself*.

Your usage appears to be either a coinage or an appropriation of the term for a distinct purpose.

### Claude · 2025-12-10 18:46

That's a fair characterization. The field has terms for the *obligation* to verify (epistemic responsibility), the *status* of having verified (justification), the *social distribution* of verification across communities (epistemic labor), and the *virtue* of being verification-prone (epistemic conscientiousness)—but no term for the actual effort itself.

The thing you can watch someone do or fail to do is unnamed. The abstractions derived from it are catalogued exhaustively.
