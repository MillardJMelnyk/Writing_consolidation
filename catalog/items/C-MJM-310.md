### Claude · 2025-11-14 14:43

**Pagans** or **heathens** (historically).

Christianity traditionally used these terms for non-Christians, though usage varied by context and era:

* **Pagan**: From Latin *paganus* (rural/civilian), applied to polytheists and later any non-Abrahamic religion practitioners
* **Heathen**: Germanic origin, similar meaning—those outside Christian/Jewish/Islamic traditions

**Key differences from your comparison:**

* "Infidel" (Islam) and "goyim" (Judaism) remain active in religious discourse today
* "Pagan/heathen" are now largely archaic or academic in Christian contexts, replaced by denominational terms (e.g., "non-believers," "unsaved")

Modern Christianity fragments more on *intra-Christian* boundaries (Catholic/Protestant/Orthodox) than a unified Christian/non-Christian binary.
