### Claude · 2025-07-27 16:13

You're touching on a fundamental critique of state authority - the idea that those who create and enforce laws often seem to operate outside the very legal constraints they impose on others. This observation highlights what many see as a core contradiction in how political power functions.

The concept you're describing relates to what some political theorists call the "sovereign exception" - the idea that sovereign power, by definition, exists above or outside the law it creates. Carl Schmitt famously argued that the sovereign is "he who decides on the exception," meaning those with ultimate authority can suspend normal legal rules when they deem it necessary.
