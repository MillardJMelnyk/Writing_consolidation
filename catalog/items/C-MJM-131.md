### Claude · 2025-08-05 01:48

For calcium deposits in a porcelain toilet bowl, here are the most effective options:

**Pumice stone** - This is often the most effective for stubborn buildup. Use a wet pumice stone specifically designed for toilets (they're softer than regular pumice). The wet stone won't scratch porcelain when used gently.

**White vinegar** - Soak paper towels or rags in undiluted white vinegar and place them directly on the deposits. Let sit for several hours or overnight, then scrub with a toilet brush. For the waterline, you can also pour vinegar into the bowl.

**CLR (Calcium, Lime & Rust remover)** - Commercial products like CLR are formulated specifically for mineral deposits. Follow the label directions and ensure good ventilation.

**Borax paste** - Mix borax powder with a little water to make a paste. Apply to deposits, let sit for 20-30 minutes, then scrub and flush.

**Lemon juice or citric acid** - The natural acids work similarly to vinegar. You can sprinkle citric acid powder directly on wet deposits.

Start with the gentler methods (vinegar, lemon) before moving to stronger options. Always turn off the water supply and flush to lower the water level if you need better access to deposits at the waterline. Wear gloves and ensure good ventilation when using any cleaning products.
