### Claude · 2025-08-20 15:15

Yes, the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and Chinese government officially recognize climate change as a scientific reality and have committed to significant green agenda measures. Based on recent information, China has established several key climate policies and targets:

**Official Climate Recognition and Commitments:**
China has committed to peaking greenhouse gas emissions before 2030 and achieving carbon neutrality before 2060. Currently, China – which alone accounts for a third of global emissions – has these goals as part of its national climate strategy.

**Green Energy Progress:**
Wind and solar power capacity is growing at a record pace, reaching over 1,400 GW in 2024—six years ahead of the 1,200 GW target. The country is also making progress on increasing its non-fossil energy share.

**Policy Framework:**
China has implemented the "Interim Regulations on the Administration of Carbon Emission Trading" (2024) and established a national carbon trading system. The government issued an Action Plan for Energy Saving and Carbon Reduction (2024-2025) that aims to reduce energy consumption by 2.5% and CO2 intensity by 3.9%.

**Environmental Actions:**
In November 2024, China's government reported finishing a 3,000 kilometers green belt around the Taklamakan Desert after 46 years of work, showing long-term commitment to environmental restoration.

However, there are implementation challenges. China's carbon intensity fell 3.4 percent in 2024, missing Beijing's official target of 3.9 percent, indicating that while the commitment exists, achieving targets remains difficult.

So yes, China officially subscribes to climate change science and has adopted extensive green policies, though critics argue their targets could be more ambitious given their capabilities.
