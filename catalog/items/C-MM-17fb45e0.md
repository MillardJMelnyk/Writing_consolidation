### Claude · 2026-06-16 13:18

The US line is drawn differently but has its own significant carve-out.

**The core protection:** The First Amendment broadly protects advocacy, criticism, and even praise of foreign terrorist organizations. You can call something a genocide, denounce US policy, advocate for a proscribed group's political goals, hold any sign you want. Pure speech is protected.

**The material support exception — *Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project* (2010):** The Supreme Court upheld 6-3 that even *nonviolent* speech can be criminalized if it constitutes "material support" to a designated Foreign Terrorist Organization — specifically when that speech is *coordinated with or directed by* the organization. The plaintiffs wanted to help the PKK use UN conflict resolution processes. The Court said that's illegal because it frees up the group's resources for violence, even if the specific assistance is peaceful.

The critical distinction the Court drew: Americans remain free to talk independently about, and even praise, foreign terrorist organizations — but the law can criminalize speech *coordinated with or controlled by* those groups.

**So the line is roughly:**

* Saying "Hamas/Hezbollah/Palestine Action is right, there is a genocide" → protected
* Organizing independently to oppose genocide → protected
* Acting *in coordination with* a designated group, even nonviolently → potentially criminal under 18 U.S.C. § 2339B

**The parallel to the UK situation:** The US couldn't do what the UK just did — mass-arrest people for holding a sign that names a proscribed group — because independent advocacy is constitutionally protected regardless of who you're praising. The UK's hook is the *expression of support*, which is the offense. The US hook requires actual coordination.

**The caveat:** The material support law casts a long shadow over expressive activity even in the US. The designation power sits with the executive branch (Secretary of State), meaning the government can effectively suppress coordination with any group it chooses to label terrorist. That's a significant structural vulnerability, just a different one than the UK's.

Bottom line: in the US you can't be arrested for the sign. You *can* be prosecuted for helping a designated group execute its strategy, even peacefully, if you're doing it in concert with them.
