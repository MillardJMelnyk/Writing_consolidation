Chatting with a new friend about anarchism and how it characterized indigenous societies, and I thought this is worth posting.

——————————-

It’s not that I “don’t like” anarchism—it’s that anarchists are trapped in certain preconceptions that guarantee that they’ll never come to a permanent solution. It’s way better than being ruled, I agree.

I like a lot about the ways of indigenous peoples, too.

I think in vectors, not rules. Where we’re at now represents a point. Where indigenous peoples were at represents (very generally) a quite different point. Those two points define a vector. Follow it back beyond where the indigenous were at and you get even more cooperation, communalism, trust, etc. And recent archaeology (Graeber brings a lot of it together) confirms that prehistoric societies were, indeed, far more cooperative, communalistic, trusting, etc., than most today think is possible.

And yet, domination arose, and with it tyrants, and with them “civilization”. We haven’t figured out a solution to stop the predominance of domination (lol) nor to prevent it from recurring.

So, I’ve basically been doing psycho-social reverse-engineering. What I’ve found is mind-blowing on first glance. I’ve been living it awhile now, so now it’s like riding a bicycle.

The simple (simplistic) version is: evildoing, including domineering/ruling, is impossible in transparent situations, because of the incredibly powerful and basic fairness/justice/max-benefit bias of human psychology and relationships. So, the always-overwhelming majority of people won’t tolerate naked evildoing when they’re aware of it and haven’t been buffaloed. Both psychologically and sociologically, when people are honest and see what’s really going on, their minds and hearts and care for the people dear to them won’t let them do nothing about it.

That’s it. No “movements” need to be organized, because honesty naturally creates its own organic unity, which organized movements lack and must create, precisely because they *do not* require uncompromising, belligerent honesty from their participants.

So, all we need, thanks to its evil-stopping power and the way it effortlessly yet overwhelmingly spreads, is honesty.
