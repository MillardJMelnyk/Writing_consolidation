### Claude · 2025-07-03 15:07

Siskiyou Pass is a mountain pass located on the border between Oregon and California along Interstate 5. It sits at an elevation of about 4,310 feet (1,314 meters) in the Siskiyou Mountains, part of the larger Klamath Mountains range.

The pass is historically significant as it was used by Native American tribes for centuries before becoming part of the Siskiyou Trail used by early settlers and gold miners in the 1800s. Today, it's a major transportation corridor connecting the Pacific Northwest to California, carrying heavy truck traffic and passenger vehicles.

The area is known for its challenging winter driving conditions due to snow and ice, and chain requirements are often in effect during winter months. The pass also marks an important ecological transition zone between different forest types and climate regions.

### Claude · 2025-07-03 15:10

The alternate route that takes you through the redwoods heading south is **US Highway 101**, also known as the Redwood Highway.

Instead of taking I-5 over Siskiyou Pass, you can take Highway 101 south along the coast. This route passes through:

* **Redwood National and State Parks** in Northern California
* **Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park** (with the scenic Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway alternate route)
* **The Avenue of the Giants** - a famous 31-mile scenic drive through Humboldt Redwoods State Park

You can split off and take US 199 when you reach Crescent City if coming from the north, or simply follow Highway 101 south from the Oregon border. The route travels through some of the most spectacular old-growth redwood forests in the world, including areas where the road traverses the spectacular redwood forests within state park boundaries.

This coastal route is longer than I-5 but offers the incredible experience of driving among the world's tallest trees, making it a popular alternative for those who want to see the redwoods rather than just get through the mountains quickly.
