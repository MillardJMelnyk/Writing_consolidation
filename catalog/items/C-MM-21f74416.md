Is it difficult to live permanently in Panama for a US citizen

### Claude · 2026-10-16 01:29

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 7:29 PM CST

No, Panama is one of the easiest countries for Americans to get permanent residency. The Friendly Nations Visa gives you two years of temporary residency, then permanent residency once you renew and show continued economic or professional activity, and the Pensionado route requires a lifetime pension of at least US$1,000 a month and grants permanent residency right away.

### Millard · 2026-10-16 01:32

What about natural disasters down there earthquakes hurricanes etc

### Claude · 2026-10-16 01:32

Hurricanes almost never hit Panama because it sits south of the hurricane belt, and Panama City has low earthquake risk, while the western Chiriquí/Costa Rica border region (Boquete, David) gets frequent moderate quakes. The real hazard is rainy-season flooding and landslides, May through November.

### Millard · 2026-10-16 01:32

What about Colon

### Claude · 2026-10-16 01:32

Colón is on the Caribbean side, so it gets more rain and flooding than Panama City but is still essentially outside the hurricane belt. Its earthquake risk is moderate: an offshore fault zone along the north coast produced the 1882 quake that damaged the city, but nothing comparable has happened there since.

### Millard · 2026-10-16 01:37

What taxes do you pay in Panama

### Claude · 2026-10-16 01:37

Your trading profits and your Duna teaching pay would be foreign-source income, which Panama does not tax, because only Panama-source income is taxed, for both residents and non-residents. In practice you'd pay the 7% ITBMS sales tax and property tax only above US$120,000 on a primary residence, at a progressive 0.5%–0.7%, while your US taxes stay the same as now.

### Millard · 2026-10-16 02:22

Why do dogs tilt their head as if they're curious

### Claude · 2026-10-16 02:22

Nobody knows for certain, but the best evidence links it to processing meaningful sounds: a 2022 study found dogs that had learned toy names tilted their heads far more often when hearing those names than ordinary dogs did. Other likely factors are adjusting their ears to pinpoint a sound, seeing past their muzzle, and owners rewarding it because it looks cute.
