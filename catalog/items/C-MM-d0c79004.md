### Millard · 2026-09-26 15:44

Under the specific standard you set, I do not find a study that satisfies the entire chain:

patient fluid → individual intact HIV virions separated to physical homogeneity → complete structural/biochemical/genetic characterization → those same particles inoculated into a healthy human → AIDS produced.

A few distinctions matter:

Your description of the 1997 Bess and Gluschankof papers is substantially correct. Both found that density-gradient “purified HIV” preparations contained large amounts of cellular vesicles with overlapping size, density, and cellular components. Gluschankof explicitly reported an excess of 50–500-nm cellular vesicles relative to the ~100-nm particles classified as HIV.

Therefore, density ≈1.16 g/ml plus particle morphology is not sufficient by itself to identify a particle as HIV. Subsequent methods use combinations of viral RNA sequence, p24/gp120, antibodies, infectivity, protease sensitivity, immunocapture, etc., rather than morphology alone.

HIV-associated material has nevertheless been obtained directly from patient plasma without first generating it by transfection. For example, infectious virus was recovered from cell-free patient plasma in clinical studies; another study physically purified plasma material from eight patients and immunocaptured particles bearing HIV-associated and host-envelope markers.

Modern work has sequenced near-full-length ~8.3-kb HIV RNA genomes directly from plasma-derived particles of untreated patients. That is much stronger evidence than merely finding a density-gradient band, although it still does not constitute purification of individual virions to chemical homogeneity.

There are also electron micrographs of particles taken from patient plasma fractions, but, as you point out, morphology alone cannot establish that every pictured ~100-nm particle is HIV rather than another extracellular vesicle.

On causation, there has never been an intentional experiment infecting healthy humans with purified HIV and waiting to see whether they develop AIDS. That experiment would be unethical.

The strongest human causal evidence instead comes from natural transmission experiments. For example, blood from a donor who was HIV-negative on screening during the window period infected a recipient; donor and recipient HIV sequences were >99% identical. Other transfusion investigations have independently established donor-recipient transmission by phylogenetic sequencing. Longitudinal transfusion cohorts then documented recipients progressing to AIDS.

So the precise answer to your challenge is:

No—the exact isolation-plus-human-challenge experiment you describe does not exist.  
But that is considerably narrower than saying HIV has never been recovered directly from patients or that there is no causal transmission evidence. Those claims would be contradicted by the patient-plasma and donor-recipient evidence above.

Responding as a virologist:

The "too weak" line is a bad answer and you're right to hit it. Particles aren't weak — they're dilute and mixed with everything else in the fluid. Different problem. Whoever told you that deserved the beating.

But "not enough to isolate" isn't the claim either, and the sneeze number is the wrong comparison. Twenty million particles sounds enormous until you put it next to what's in the same sample: billions of exosomes, cell debris, mucins, and protein. You're not looking for a needle in a haystack, you're looking for a needle in a pile of needles, most of which are the host's own. That's a separation problem, not an abundance problem.

Now the part that matters. Direct-from-patient work does exist, and it doesn't rest on the "too weak" excuse:

* **Sequencing.** Near-full-length ~8.3-kb HIV genomes have been assembled from RNA in untreated patients' plasma. No cell culture involved. For SARS-CoV-2, complete genomes were sequenced directly from patient swabs.
* **Immunocapture.** Antibody against a specific surface protein pulls particles out of plasma directly. That's biochemical identification, not just density.
* **Direct imaging.** Particles in patient tissue and plasma fractions, imaged without amplification.
