### Millard · 2026-09-14 12:51

is there a difference between free will and free choice to a NFW adherent?

### Claude · 2026-09-14 12:51

No. They use the terms interchangeably, and that's part of the problem.

In the literature the distinction exists — free will is the capacity, free choice is an exercise of it — but NFW arguments collapse them deliberately, because their target is neither. Their target is *ultimate authorship*: whether you originated the causal chain. Once that's the criterion, capacity and exercise fail together, so no reason to keep them apart.

Which is why your interlocutor can grant every phenomenon — deliberating, weighing, choosing, trying — and still say nothing free happened. Pereboom says this outright: deliberation only requires options be epistemically open, not causally open. The word "free" in their usage doesn't modify the choosing. It modifies the origin.

So if you want to pry them apart, don't ask whether choices are free. Ask what work "free" is doing, and whether any capacity whatsoever could satisfy it. Nothing can be its own uncaused origin — so the criterion rules out not just free will but the concept of a capacity at all.
