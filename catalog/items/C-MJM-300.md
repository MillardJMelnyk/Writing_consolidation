### Claude · 2025-10-14 19:43

**Issues:**

1. **"humanness"** - Vague. What specific attribute are you contrasting with codependence? Autonomy? Self-reliance? Maturity?
2. **"opposite approaches to enablement"** - Grammatically awkward. "Approaches to" implies methods, but you're describing sources/agents of enablement.
3. **"who do you depend on to enable you?"** - Redundant phrasing ("depend on" + "enable")
4. **"limited to circumstances, events, or other beings"** - Why list circumstances and events separately? Both are external conditions.
5. **"in those aspects of your life where that's true, to the degree it's true"** - Wordy qualifier that weakens the claim
6. **"regardless whether...cooperate or oppose you"** - This addition confuses the distinction. If you're self-enabling *despite* opposition, that still involves external conditions affecting you.
7. **"free, mature, adult person in full experience and expression of your humanness"** - Overloaded conclusion. Are these four separate claims or synonyms?

**Clarifications needed:**

* Are you defining codependence clinically or colloquially?
* Does "self-enablement" mean total independence from external resources, or just psychological self-determination?
* Is partial codependence possible, or is this binary?

Codependence and autonomy are easily differentiated. The distinction is simple: what enables you?

If you depend on circumstances, events, or other people to enable you, you are codependent in those areas. If you depend on yourself to enable yourself—regardless of whether external factors cooperate or oppose you—you are autonomous: a free, mature adult operating in full expression of your humanness.
