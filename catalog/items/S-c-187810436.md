I didn't find anything radical in your article here. The information from the Kabbalah was interesting, though.

I recommend Adams’ book on the topic: <https://a.co/d/2bmQllK>.

Her main point is that, to date, no one who has taken up this topic has done it in a realistically human way. Philosophical takes on the so-called problem of evil completely miss the point: evil is not a philosophical matter. It is a real, experienced, destructive, cataclysmic matter that philosophical thinking both belittles and demeans merely by taking its abstract point of view on the issue.

One of the most interesting aspects of work on the “problem” is the fact that few if any at all take it on from the perspective of its victims. If anyone at all has a valid perspective on the question of why God allows evil, the people who have first-hand experience with evil would be the ones to ask.

The notable absence of representation of the victim's perspective in all this proves that the thinkers had a different problem in mind than the problem that evil poses to its prey. They're not trying to resolve the real-world threat posed by evil to real-world people. They're trying to vindicate their conception of God against the damning condemnation of reality: no such God exists.

And then they're arrogant enough to pretend that their conception of God defines God.

There is no “problem of evil”, actually. There’s just a problem for people who want to cling to an idea of God that’s incoherent.

I've read all the arguments. The only person that I consider to have dealt with the subject honestly was Bart Ehrman in \*God's Problem\*. His conclusion: there is no answer or solution to the “problem of evil”. So, he became “agnostic”, which was his mistake. Rather than let go of a bogus \*\*conception of God\*\*, he rejected every conception of God along with the incoherent one, as well as the possibility that God exists and is active in the universe, but in an incomprehensible way. He and I agree, though, that regardless of God/no God, regardless of the “problem” posed by evil to people who want to cling to an incoherent conception of God, evil must be dealt with.

There’s a lot to say about this—like the egotism and narcissism often involved, because most people who have been interested in the “problem of evil” aren’t motivated by a committed desire to eradicate evil, but by a desire to vindicate their incoherent conception of God. No one can honestly, genuinely take on the problem of evil in the world when their true concern is to protect the conception of God they cling to for dear life \*\*even though real evil in the real world exposes it to be incoherent\*\*. Those are two radically different concerns, and the latter has nothing to do with what really matters.

This problem dogged me for decades. Evil didn’t just devastate me. My devastation didn’t make me rage at God, even hate God—if there was one. It devastated my children. \*\*That\*\* devastation was why I cursed at God, hated God, and even felt like cursing God. I didn’t give a flying fuck if God struck me dead—that would just prove what kind of God it was. But I had to live, because my children needed me. They’re all grown men now, and they’re still in different stages and degrees of PTSD as a result. Fuck the God that claims to be good and let that happen. My oldest was 8 at the time. The youngest was a newborn. They were fucking innocent. There was nothing sane or reasonable or right or the least bit justifiable about what was done to them in utter opposition to everything I did to try to protect them.

Two decades later, I started to realize something: the “problem of evil” doesn’t matter. Whether or not there is some bye-in-the-sky reason that we’ll finally be in a position to understand in the hereafter, one which resolves how a good God could allow evil—\*\*create\*\* evil according to Isaiah and \*\*know intimately, experientially\*\*, according to Genesis—it makes not a single fucking point of difference to what \*\*I\*\* need to do about evil right \*now\*. The “problem of evil” is a side-issue that’s irrelevant to the problem of real evil in the world and its real violation, betrayal, and damage to real people.

So, no matter which way a person “solves” the irrelevant problem, it means nothing to the real problem. A question that leaves me in exactly the same place with exactly the same things to do, no matter what its answer might turn out to be, is a meaningless question. And when it comes to something as horrendous and profoundly hateful and destructive as evil, bringing up a meaningless question as if it’s important is just offensive and proves the people who prioritized it over the real question just don’t know or care what the fuck is really going on.

But that’s the damnable thing about people that get all wrapped up in irrelevancies, isn’t it? \*\*They have NO intention of DOING anything about the real problem\*\*. If the real problem were solved, their fake problem would need no solution, because the conditions that made it a problem at all would have been eliminated.

The ”problem of evil” is how people who have no intention of eradicating real evil cosplay as caring, righteous people who want good.

No, they don’t.

For people determined to eradicate evil from Earth, there is no conundrum.
