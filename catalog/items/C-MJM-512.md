### Millard · 2025-12-23 04:12

Is cocaine in smaller doses physically addictive?

### Claude · 2025-12-23 04:12

Yes, but the physical component is relatively minor compared to psychological dependence.

Cocaine produces physical adaptations in the brain's dopamine system with repeated use, even at lower doses. Physical withdrawal symptoms include fatigue, increased appetite, sleep disruption, and psychomotor changes—but these are typically less severe than withdrawal from alcohol, opioids, or benzodiazepines.

The key factors:

**Dose-dependence**: Lower doses produce weaker physical dependence than higher doses, but dependence can still develop with regular use.

**Route matters**: Smoking or IV use produces faster, more intense effects and stronger dependence than intranasal use, which is stronger than oral use—regardless of absolute dose.

**Individual variation**: Genetic factors and usage patterns create significant variability in who develops physical dependence and how quickly.

**The distinction is artificial**: Separating "physical" from "psychological" addiction is increasingly seen as false dichotomy—both involve measurable neurobiological changes. Cocaine's psychological hold (craving, compulsive use) is simply more prominent than its physical withdrawal syndrome.

Bottom line: smaller doses reduce but don't eliminate physical addiction risk with repeated use.

### Millard · 2025-12-23 04:13

Do people microdose cocaine like they microdose LSD?

### Claude · 2025-12-23 04:14

No. Cocaine microdosing is not an established practice like LSD microdosing.

**Why the difference:**

**Pharmacological profile**: LSD has ~10-12 hour duration, allowing once-daily dosing with stable effects. Cocaine has ~30-90 minute duration (intranasal), requiring repeated redosing that promotes compulsive use patterns.

**Tolerance patterns**: LSD builds rapid tolerance requiring days between doses. Cocaine's tolerance is less predictable and stimulates binge patterns rather than stable spacing.

**Subjective effects**: LSD at microdose levels produces subtle cognitive/perceptual changes. Cocaine at any active dose produces noticeable stimulation and reward—there's no truly "sub-perceptual" range that retains effects people would seek.

**Addiction liability**: LSD has minimal addiction potential. Cocaine has high addiction potential even at lower doses due to dopamine system effects.

**Anecdotal reports**: Some individuals claim to use small amounts of cocaine functionally (e.g., professionals using small bumps during work), but this is distinct from the structured microdosing protocols used with psychedelics and typically represents either low-level addiction or recreational use rather than a deliberate microdosing regimen.

The term "microdosing" specifically implies a sub-perceptual dose taken on a schedule for cognitive/therapeutic benefits—cocaine doesn't fit this model.
