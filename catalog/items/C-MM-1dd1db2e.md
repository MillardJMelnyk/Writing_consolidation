**What you're asking for:** a C1 worksheet as a PDF that explains and drills modal + plain verb (*can go, must be, should leave*), with no form containing "have."

**One thing is unclear:** "without have" definitely excludes the perfect forms (*must have gone, should have said*). It's not clear whether it also excludes (a) *have to / have got to / had better*, and (b) the "be" forms, both continuous (*must be working*) and passive (*must be done*). Unless you say otherwise, I'll exclude both (a) and (b) and cover only modal + plain verb.

**Plan:**  
- **Modals covered:** can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, ought to, needn't.  
- **Part 1, form:** the rules for building the forms. No -s, no "to" except *ought to*, how negatives and questions work, and no two modals together.  
- **Part 2, meaning by function:** each function gets an example table and C1 nuance notes.  
- Ability  
- Permission  
- Deduction and possibility (must / can't / may / might / could, and should for expectation)  
- Obligation, prohibition and lack of necessity (must / mustn't / needn't)  
- Advice and mild criticism (should / ought to)  
- Prediction, willingness and refusal (will / won't / wouldn't)  
- Typical behaviour and past habit (will / would)  
- Requests, offers and suggestions  
- Hedging in formal and academic register  
- Fixed expressions (*might as well, may well, could do with, would rather, can't help*)  
- **Part 3:** common C1-level errors.  
- **Part 4, exercises:** about 80 items across nine sections.  
1. Identify the function  
2. Choose the best modal (nuance pairs)  
3. Mustn't vs needn't  
4. Deduce from clues  
5. Key-word sentence transformation, Cambridge C1 style  
6. Error correction  
7. Rewrite blunt sentences in polite or hedged form  
8. Gap-fill text  
9. Open writing task  
- **Part 5:** answer key on separate pages.  
- **Look:** Letter size, a clean two-colour design with tables, numbered items with answer lines, about 10–12 pages.

Say GO, or correct the plan.
