It's called the Overton window.

Its ONLY application is in codependent populations -- which, yeah, sad to say, means pretty much *every* population.

If they're getting paid for it, they're chasing the window, so everything they say should be suspected. Period.

This is keenly true in the religious sphere.

There is no such thing as a "prophet" that heads a mega church. None. Not one.

There is you. There is your life, your experience, what you've learned from it, what you know when you're alone with yourself and let your fear of others' reactions and judgments go for a minute (if you're even capable of it.)

Or we could say: there is your soul, comprised of all the above.

And, if you like, there is God. Or gods. Or the universe. Or the cosmos. In other words, there is the Greater, the Whole.

You get a choice. You make this same choice as a general, this-is-who-and-what-I-am kind of way, and you make it over and over in your life at different times, with different people, over different issues and concerns.

You choose whether to honestly be who you are or fill the role of an appendage -- a "member" ***as defined by a group***.

A codependent.

A cultist.

Most of you have no fucking clue what I'm talking about because being a codependent and a cultist is all you've ever known how to be.

You either are who you are, or you derive an "identity" from some group (racial/ethnic, political, ideological, religious, whatever) that defines you.

There's no middle ground, just like pregnancy. You're either a real person, or you're just a reflection of something else.

You can behave as your own person in some ways and situations with certain people, but without swearing off codependence, without rejecting and refusing to be a cultist -- without kicking your addiction to sucking life out of others.

That means you're sterile. You can copy, mimic, emulate, etc. -- but you cannot be a life-bearer.

A lot of people think that our behavior defines us, so they discipline themselves in hopes that it will change them or prove what they are.

That's just an idea held by codependents and cultists. It's just not how human psychology works.

Either you define yourself or you let yourself be defined. You decide who you are or others will decide for you -- and then you submit to it.

You can imagine the person you want to be and then become that person -- but not by discipline or an act of will or training, etc. Those and other efforts are, at best, means of manifesting, of working out, who you have already become in fact, in truth, within.

Almost no one seems to understand this.

"Manifesting" what you want to become true is not the same as manifesting what already is true.

The "kingdom of heaven is within you" means that the reality of which everything else is at best an expression, a reflection, lies inside, in your soul, where you know who you are and what's what.

But if you're trying to suck life out of the responses you get to what you do, what you produce for others to see, you're a slave.

A cultist.

A codependent.

There is no overlap.
