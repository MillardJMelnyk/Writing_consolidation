### Claude · 2025-12-22 15:01

**Partially correct, with three significant errors:**

1. **"Almost exclusively" Europeans** - Overstated. Ottoman Empire (Armenian genocide), Imperial Japan (Nanjing, Unit 731, comfort women), Arab slave trade, Mongol conquests, and others demonstrate systematic rights violations across cultures. Europeans were major perpetrators over recent centuries, but not "almost exclusively."
2. **Southeast Asia claim is backwards** - Europeans DID directly perpetrate crimes against humanity in Burma (British), Indonesia (Dutch), and former French Indochina. Direct perpetration included: forced labor systems, mass killings, deliberate famines, torture. Characterizing this as merely "inciting" understates documented colonial violence.
3. **Burma and Myanmar** - These are the same place. Myanmar is Burma's current official name (changed 1989). Listing them separately suggests two different locations.

**Accurate core claim:** That proclaimed "inalienable rights" have been systematically violated throughout history, including through racial dehumanization by colonial powers, is factually correct. The philosophical declaration of rights as inalienable did not prevent their routine alienation in practice.
