Which is more common lay persons or lay people

### Claude · 2025-08-15 13:53

"Lay people" is more common and generally preferred in modern usage.

"Lay persons" follows a more formal, traditional pattern (like "chairpersons"), but "lay people" sounds more natural and is what most people use in everyday speech and writing. You'll see "lay people" used more frequently in newspapers, magazines, and casual conversation.

Both are grammatically correct, but if you're looking for the more widely accepted and contemporary option, go with "lay people."

"Laypeople" (as one word) is actually the most common and preferred form in modern usage.

Here's the breakdown from most to least common:

1. **Laypeople** - Most widely used, especially in professional and academic writing
2. **Lay people** (two words) - Also common and acceptable
3. **Laypersons** - Less common but still correct

So if you want the most contemporary and widely accepted option, "laypeople" as one word is your best choice.

### Millard · 2025-08-15 14:14

what's the proper format for [sic]

### Claude · 2025-08-15 14:14

The proper format for [sic] is:

**[sic]** - in square brackets, lowercase, usually italicized

**Usage rules:**

* Place it immediately after the error you're noting
* No space between the error and [sic]
* Usually italicized: *[sic]*
* Always lowercase
* Always in square brackets

**Examples:**

* "The sign read 'No tresspassing\*[sic]\*'"
* "He wrote that he was 'definately\*[sic]\* coming to the party'"
* "The headline stated: 'Mayor Announces New Initative\*[sic]\* for Schools'"

**When to use it:**

* When quoting someone else's spelling, grammar, or factual error
* To show the error was in the original, not your mistake
* Only when the error might confuse readers or when accuracy is important

**When NOT to use it:**

* Don't overuse it—it can seem pedantic
* Skip it for obvious typos in informal contexts
* Avoid it if the meaning is still clear

### Millard · 2025-08-15 14:22

"not allowed" is an adjective?

### Claude · 2025-08-15 14:22

You're right to question that! "Not allowed" is actually a **predicate adjective phrase**, not a simple adjective.

"Allowed" by itself is a past participle that functions as an adjective (like "broken" or "tired"), and "not" modifies it.

If you want a single-word adjective synonym for "impermissible," try:

* **Forbidden**
* **Banned**
* **Prohibited**
* **Unacceptable**
* **Wrong**

But if you're okay with phrases that function adjectivally:

* **Off-limits**
* **Out of bounds**
* **Against the rules**

Good catch on the grammar!
