### Claude · 2026-08-19 19:02

Yes. US citizens owe US tax on worldwide income no matter where they live. Living in Mexico changes nothing about the filing obligation.

What actually matters for this contract:

**Self-employment tax is the real bite.** As an independent contractor you owe 15.3% SE tax on net earnings once they exceed $400 for the year. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion does *not* exempt you from it — FEIE only shelters income tax. And there's no US–Mexico totalization agreement in force, so you can't opt out by paying into IMSS instead.

**Income tax you'll probably owe nothing on.** At $10/hour, you're far under the FEIE threshold (roughly $130K), and the foreign tax credit covers whatever Mexico takes.

**So the practical answer at this income level:** ~15.3% to the IRS on your net, plus whatever Mexico assesses, plus a Schedule C and Schedule SE on your 1040 every year.

**Which fixes point 1 from before.** You're a US person, so W-9 is correct, not W-8BEN, and the 1099-MISC language fits after all. "Proof of eligibility to work in the USA" is still template sloppiness — you're not working in the US — but a passport or SSN satisfies it. Nothing to raise with them.

**Mexico side:** if you're a tax resident there (which you are if Querétaro is your permanent home), SAT wants to see this income too. Whether you need to be registered in RFC for it depends on how you're already set up there.

Not a tax advisor — worth an hour with someone who does expat returns, since the SE tax piece catches a lot of people the first year.

### Millard · 2026-08-19 20:23

what is a W-8BEN?

### Claude · 2026-08-19 20:23

It's the IRS form a **non-US person** gives to a US company to certify they're foreign — so the payer knows how to handle withholding.

Doesn't apply to you. You're a US citizen, so you file a **W-9** instead, regardless of living in Mexico. Citizenship decides it, not address.
