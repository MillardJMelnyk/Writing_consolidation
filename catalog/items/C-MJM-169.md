Poisoned tree
So, if the root of the rights tree turns out to be rotten: what should we say about its legal fruit?
If the concept of “rights” itself is problematic or even bogus, then, to the extent that laws are based on recognition of “rights” and presumed to implement “rights” protections, they are likewise problematic or even bogus.
Think of it like this… when a relative sexually abuses, rapes, and impregnates a 12-year-old girl, if we took the whole of the meaning of her violation and reduced it to the “violation of her rights”: we’ll have fallen far, far short of honestly-and-fully (and humanly) grasping what's really happened and what’s actually at stake.
What’s more important and real: her “rights”? Or rather, is it her violated, injured body; her traumatized, mutilated psyche; the destruction of one-or-many of her most important relationships; and her ongoing sense of danger, shame, and insecurity in her own family?
“Rights” make us take our eyes off the ball and pretend we caught it instead of admitting that it hit us square in the nose—so we can’t see shit for the flood of tears.
So-called human rights were invented to serve as nothing but a straw man, a distraction, an impersonator, a fiction onto which our attention could be derailed off and away from real harm done to real persons.
When “rights” become the filter through which we view real occurrences of deliberate action intended to harm, the issue of violation of persons takes the back seat while deliberation of whether rights were violated takes the wheel.
Actually, no, I’ll revise that: the issue of violation of rights grabs the issue of violation of persons by the scruff of the neck and the seat of the pants and throws it into the trunk, (along with the realities of the evil done to the violated person,) and then takes the wheel.
No violation of a stipulated “right”? Then, no violation at all—not in a rights paradigm!
This is ridiculous!
Naw, it’s worse than that… Just ask a victim: everyone arguing, fighting, battling in family furors, and neighborhood witch-hunts, and court over abstractions while the concrete reality—the violated person and the evils done to them—becomes increasingly invisible. That is actually what it’s like for victims in rights paradigms. This is what they experience despite everything being said by rights advocates seeking “justice” for them with their backs turned to them.
Yes, I’m saying that “human rights”, “animal rights”, or any other kind of “rights” are ridiculous, insane, and a person-erasing violation in their own right. I’m saying, therefore, that the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) is ridiculous, insane, and a person-erasing violation in its own right. The very concept of “rights” is fundamentally-and-functionally fatally flawed. As such: I must conclude that the United Nations conceived and established a declaration of absurdity for some reason other than to reduce the actual, concretely real evil and violation in the world. 
The myth of rights
The ostensible point of invoking rights—the myth of rights—is to protect us against evildoing, violation, and harm… but, in fact, rights don’t do this at all.
There are at least four steps in any rational (key word) formulation of a set of rights. Here I’m talking about what reasonable, intelligent persons must do at a minimum to arrive at anything resembling a cogent list of rights:
1.	Recognition: They must witness and/or experience real-life patterns of behavior that no one wants to be subjected to and which no one would condone others being subjected to.

2.	Abstraction: They must generalize those patterns of behavior and designate them as wrong in principle.

3.	Articulation: They must describe the goods whose enjoyment would be damaged or destroyed by these wrongs.

4.	Declaration: They must declare that each of these goods implies a universal “right” that entitles those who “have” the right to enjoy that good and be free from wrongs that would damage or destroy their enjoyment of that good.
In a rights paradigm, there is no basis for arguing or pressing rights at all until all four steps have been taken.
This all seems to make sense to people for some reason I’ve long scratched my head trying to figure out. As best as I can understand: it’s because they take evildoing, violation, and harm as unquestionable, foregone conclusions.
Consider what would happen if we did not assume that evildoing, violation, and harm were interminable givens. I’ve had a very hard time getting anyone to sincerely consider that prospect.
Forget about getting people to open their minds to the possibility of a real world free from evildoing, violation, and harm. People seem unable even to entertain the idea of a hypothetical world free from evildoing, violation, and harm, let alone a potentially real one. The very thought boggles their minds but, more importantly, triggers their cynicism reflexes like a reflex hammer does a knee-jerk.
“No use even imagining that, because it will never happen.”
Bullshit!
Question: In a world free from evildoing, violation, and harm, would it ever occur to a person to formulate rights?
Question: In a world free from evildoing, violation, and harm, would going through all that work (Steps #1- #4) seem reasonable to spend time and effort on? If so, for what purpose and to what end?
I’m sure you can guess my answers. 
Answer without a sane question
In case you can’t guess my answers: consider, a little thought experiment.
Take Kady, for example. Her family loves her. She’s never been harmed. Her parents lavish goodness and good things on all their children. In fact, they rarely say “No” to a request from her or her siblings. But one evening: Kady stomps her way to the dinner table like she’s on a crusade and slaps down her “Declaration of My Right to Freedom From Sexual Molestation and Rape”.
What’s everyone going to think? What sparked in her mind to make it seem sensible and necessary to declare a right to a freedom she’d had all her life; to the very people who most want her good, freedom from a travesty that had never occurred as a credible possibility to anyone but her—being unthinkable to everyone in a family like hers?
By declaring rights, we declare that we consider the likelihood of suffering evildoing, violation, and harm is credible, real, and substantial. Rights are all and only a response to routine violation serious enough and frequent enough to warrant formulating statements of rights denouncing them—routine, deliberate action intended to harm by the stronger on the weaker which the stronger intend and the weaker expect will continue.
By declaring rights, we do not reject evildoing, violation, and harm. Instead, we declare that we accept their reality as an inevitability which we nonetheless oppose.
You can’t oppose what does not exist. An ongoing program of opposition implies none other than that the opposed both exists and will continue… But it cannot exist and continue against opposition if it’s been eradicated.
By asserting our rights, we declare our silent commitment to never eradicate evildoing, violation, and harm—but merely to argue for mitigation of their ongoing, damaging results.
Deliberate action intended to harm (and violation) are the prerequisites for a rights response—without which, any such response would be nonsensical. In other words: the only reason rights make any sense at all is that we have already failed to prevent evil that, by rights, never should have happened in the first place. This means that we’ve already surrendered to the inevitability of evil operating indefinitely before we ever utter the words, “But I have a right!”
Except for evildoing, violation, and harm, there would be nothing for rights arguments to argue against. Except that evil arose first, we would never assume it will arise again... unless we first assumed that evil is a given, routine, and interminable—and what factual, reasonable grounds could that assumption possibly rest? 5000-plus years of supremacist, authoritarian enculturation and its heinous results are incontrovertibly factual but utterly insane—apart from the conviction that they’re patently necessary and unavoidable. But that conviction is completely void of fact and reason.
In other words, we’d rightly see rights as absurd!
It’s impossible for rights to prevent evildoing, violation, and harm, because rights are not in any way sensible and relevant until after we’ve already failed to prevent evildoing, violation, and harm.

Just guess what the typical responses have been to my stated goal of eradicating evildoing, violation, and harm from Earth. Why is everyone so thoroughly convinced cynically? What makes everyone so sure that evildoing, violation, and harm are inevitable and interminable? Go ahead: show me how these unexamined, preconceived commitments to cynicism aren’t every bit as irrational and incoherent as the thinking just described that has led us to believe that rights are a good idea.
So, if rights exist in any sense at all, they exist as a function of evil that would vaporize the instant evil were eradicated… just like no equal and opposite reaction can occur without an action that triggers it.
Newton’s third law of motion falls short as an analogy of the evil-rights relationship in a key aspect: rights are in no way an equal and opposite reaction to evildoing, violation, and harm. Therein lies the fraudulence of rights. Rights are not a preventative or a corrective—they are a Band-Aid over a gangrene so far advanced that, sorry, you’re going to lose those rotten legs, for sure.
In other words: rights seem sensible only when we’re in the throes of terminal cynicism—unable to think straight, having already accepted that evildoing, violation, and harm will remain inexorable “facts of life” until we die.

You shift your focus from curative treatment to palliative care. You let go thought of eliminating or rectifying the causes of their suffering. If you happen to profit from their suffering and pain: you might palliate them, “help” them enough to keep them going… stopping short of letting them go belly-up, so that they’ll keep on gruing and toiling—earning that living and making you a fortune in the process.
Rights are a consolation gimmick, a panacea that remedies nothing for people who already have capitulated and lost all hope of ending evildoing, violation, and harm.
Ameliorating is not terminating, but rather an insidious way of prolonging.

Fraud of the ages

How’s that as some mind-fucked doublespoken pigshit for ya?
