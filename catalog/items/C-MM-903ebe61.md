What 623 likely gets you:

* **APR:** 7.74%–35.99% fixed, and a 623 score lands in the upper part of that range. Credible's closed-loan data shows Upgrade's APRs for fair credit were the highest, on average, of almost all its partner lenders.
* **Fee:** a 1.85%–9.99% origination fee, deducted from the loan amount. At the high end, you'd have to borrow about $8,570 to net $7,713, which means roughly $855 gone upfront.

* **Credit hit:** the rate check is a soft pull. Actually applying is a hard pull.

### Claude · 2026-09-27 20:48

**My opinion: this offer probably isn't worth it at 623. The better move is a free call to a nonprofit credit counselor (NFCC member). Upgrade only makes sense if its soft-pull quote comes back clearly below your card rates.**

**Why Upgrade is likely a wash for you:**  
- At your score, Upgrade is one of the more expensive places to borrow. Credible's closed-loan data shows Upgrade APRs were among the highest, on average, for borrowers with fair credit.  
- One reviewer's estimate for your range: if your score is in the low 600s, budget for something closer to 20% or above. The origination fee sits on top of that interest rate, and fair credit usually draws the upper end of the 1.85%–9.99% fee.  
- Your cards are probably in the same neighborhood. The average rate on credit card accounts assessed interest was 22.15% in Q2 2026, and fair-credit cards usually run higher.

**Rough cost to clear $7,713 in 3 years** (my estimates, assuming your cards are at 25%):

| Route | Monthly | Total paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep paying cards at 25% | ~$307 | ~$11,040 |
| Upgrade at ~21% interest + 8% fee (~27% APR) | ~$316 | ~$11,370 |
| Nonprofit debt management plan at ~8% + ~$30/mo fees | ~$272 | ~$9,780 |

At a realistic Upgrade quote, you'd pay more than you do now. If your cards are at 29.99%, the Upgrade loan comes out a few hundred dollars ahead. That's the only case where it's worth taking.

**The debt management plan route:**  
- An NFCC agency negotiates with your card companies for you. You make one payment to the agency, and it pays the cards. The average interest rate on an InCharge debt management program is about 8%.  
- No loan, no hard credit pull. The initial counselling session is free with no obligation.  
- The cost: creditors require you to close your credit cards, and your credit score may take a hit the first 6-12 months before on-time payments rebuild it.  
- It fits your situation: a moderate balance, a fair score, and no need for new credit for the next few years.

**Another option:** a federal credit union personal loan. Federal credit unions cap loan APRs at 18%, and many charge no origination fee. That beats the likely Upgrade quote, assuming you can join one.

**Upgrade's real advantage:** your cards stay open with zero balances, so your credit utilization drops and your score likely climbs fast. That's worth something if you want your score up soon and you're confident the cards stay at zero. If balances creep back, you end up carrying the loan and the card debt together.

**What to do:** run Upgrade's soft-pull check (it's free), and book the free NFCC session. Then take the lower all-in APR. My bet is that it will be the debt management plan.

**Best option: a PenFed Credit Union personal loan to pay off all four cards, then keep the cards open with zero balances.** Use Upgrade only as the fallback, and only if its quote beats your card rates. My earlier debt-management-plan pick doesn't fit anymore, because these accounts show a spotless payment record, and a plan would close the cards and cost you your 7-year Capital One.

Quick flag: these four balances total **$5,807**, not the $7,713 you mentioned earlier. The numbers below use $5,807.

**What's actually holding your score down (Likely):** every account shows 100% on-time payments, but you're using $5,807 of $7,970 in total limits. That's about 73% utilization, with three cards at 85–93%. That high usage is most likely what's keeping you at 623. Utilization has no memory in FICO scoring, so once the cards report near zero, your score reflects it the following month. Paying them off with a loan is the fastest way to raise it.

**Why PenFed first:**  
- APRs run 6.74%–17.99% with no origination fee, and anyone can join PenFed for free.  
- Loans go as small as $500, with terms of 12–60 months.  
- Its rate is capped at 18%. Federal credit unions cap most loan APRs at 18%, while commercial banks can go up to 24%–36%.  
- Approval at 623 is the uncertain part. PenFed publishes no minimum score, and industry-wide, 670 is where cheaper rates tend to start.

**3-year cost to clear $5,807** (card rate assumed at 25%, check your statements):

| Route | Monthly | Total paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PenFed at its 17.99% worst case | ~$210 | ~$7,560 |
| Keep paying cards at 25% | ~$231 | ~$8,310 |
| Upgrade at ~27% APR (8% fee) | ~$238 | ~$8,560 |

Even PenFed's worst rate saves you about $750. Upgrade at a typical fair-credit quote costs more than just paying the cards.

**Order of moves:**  
1. Pull the purchase APR off each card's statement. Store cards like Amazon and Best Buy often run near 30%, and that changes how much any loan saves you.  
2. Check the Best Buy card for a promotional balance. A $4 minimum payment on $409 suggests deferred-interest financing (Likely). If it is, that balance is at 0% now, but if it isn't paid off by the promo end date, all the back interest gets charged at once. Pay it off before then no matter what else you do.  
3. Join PenFed and check your loan rate.  
4. Check Upgrade's rate too (soft pull), for comparison.  
5. Take the lowest APR, as long as it's clearly below your card rates. Apply once, for the full $5,807.  
6. Pay off all four cards and keep every one of them open, especially the 7-year Capital One, since it's your oldest account. Put one small recurring charge on it and pay it in full each month.

**If both lenders say no, or quote above your card rates:** skip the loan. Put every spare dollar on the highest-APR card while paying minimums on the rest. The Capital One ($849) and Amazon ($826) balances are small enough to clear within a few months, which cuts utilization quickly. Recheck loan rates once your score moves, since a higher score gets you a much better quote.
