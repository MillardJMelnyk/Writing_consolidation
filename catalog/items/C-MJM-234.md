### Claude · 2025-09-01 14:49

**Verifiable**: Can be confirmed true through observation, testing, or evidence checking.

**Falsifiable**: Can potentially be proven wrong through observation, testing, or counter-evidence.

Key distinction: Verifiable focuses on proving truth. Falsifiable focuses on potential for disproof.

Examples:

* "There are 50 US states" - verifiable (can confirm) and falsifiable (could theoretically be wrong)
* "God exists" - neither verifiable nor falsifiable through empirical means
* "All swans are white" - falsifiable (one black swan disproves it) but not fully verifiable (can't check every swan)

Falsifiability is Popper's criterion for scientific theories - they must be structured so they could potentially be proven wrong.

### Claude · 2025-09-01 14:50

**Verifiable**: You can prove it's true by checking.

* "The store closes at 9 PM" - call and ask
* "It rained yesterday" - check weather records

**Falsifiable**: You can potentially prove it's wrong.

* "All cats are black" - find one non-black cat, statement is false
* "This medicine cures headaches" - test it, might fail

**Neither verifiable nor falsifiable**:

* "This painting is beautiful" - can't prove true or false objectively

**Key difference**: Verifiable = can prove RIGHT. Falsifiable = can prove WRONG.

Many scientific claims are falsifiable but not fully verifiable (you can't test every possible case).

### Claude · 2025-09-01 14:51

Correct. You cannot prove anything is definitively right.

**Verifiable** (as I used it) was imprecise. Better terms:

**Confirmable/Supported**: Evidence supports it being true, but doesn't prove it.

* "Water boils at 100°C at sea level" - repeatedly confirmed, never definitively proven

**Falsifiable**: Could potentially be proven wrong.

* Same water boiling example - one valid counter-instance would falsify it

Popper's point: Science advances by attempting to falsify theories, not by proving them true. Theories survive only until falsified.

**Unfalsifiable statements** can't be tested either way:

* "There's an invisible, undetectable dragon in this room"

The distinction matters because only falsifiable statements qualify as scientific - they can be tested and potentially rejected.
