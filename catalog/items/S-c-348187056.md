When you accept a particular ideology as the absolute truth and become so devoted to it that you follow it unquestioningly, your capacity to think reasonably and act responsibly diminishes.

//

Can you explain how closing a matter to question, accepting an idea as true without question, is qualitatively different than:

a. dogmatic belief

b. strong belief

c. any old belief

There are 2 things involved that I find people chronically conflate:

1. The idea

2. The extra magic factor added to the idea to make it a belief

Obviously, a mere idea is not a belief. A mere thought is not a belief. "I think" and "I believe" are not synonymous aside from involving thought/ideas.

So, what is that extra magic factor that makes an idea or a thought into a belief?

I'm not saying that you cannot equate thought = idea = belief. Go ahead if you want to.

I'm saying that in normal usage, people do not mean the same things by those terms.

So, what are actually the real differences between them?

And then, once you've identified that extra magic factor, what does it actually do for you?

And should it do that?
