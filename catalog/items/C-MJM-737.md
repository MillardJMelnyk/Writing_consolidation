### Claude · 2026-08-10 21:09

It's this study: Andersson et al., "Aluminum-Adsorbed Vaccines and Chronic Diseases in Childhood: A Nationwide Cohort Study," Annals of Internal Medicine, published July 15, 2025 (SSI, Copenhagen).

Accuracy check against the image:

* "1,000,000 children for 21 years" — actual: 1,224,176 children born in Denmark 1997–2018, followed to age 2 for exposure, outcomes tracked through 2020 (~24 years cohort span, not a uniform 21-year individual follow-up). "21 years" is imprecise; commonly cited as 24.
* "aluminum-adjuvanted vaccines were linked to a shocking number... that number was zero" — correct in substance: cumulative aluminum exposure was not associated with increased rates of any of the 50 disorders assessed.
* "48 other chronic conditions" — study measured 50 total, so autism + asthma + 48 others = 50. Consistent.
* The graphic's framing ("STUDY: ... found ... linked to a SHOCKING number ... THAT NUMBER WAS ZERO") is a garden-path setup implying causation was found, then reversed. Rhetorically loaded but the underlying number/conclusion checks out.

Net: the meme accurately represents the study's finding (no association), with one soft number (21 vs. 24 years).
