is there a need for "supremacistic"?

### Claude · 2025-12-30 17:19

The question hinges on whether "supremacist" (already functioning as both noun and adjective) leaves a semantic gap.

**Case for no:**
"Supremacist" as adjective covers standard usage: "supremacist ideology," "supremacist rhetoric." English typically doesn't double-derive when the base form already adjectivizes.

**Case for yes:**
The "-istic" suffix can mark *tendency toward* or *resemblance to* rather than full instantiation. Compare "fascist" (the thing itself) versus "fascistic" (having qualities of, leaning toward). "Supremacistic" could distinguish between:

* A supremacist policy (explicitly supremacist)
* A supremacistic policy (exhibiting supremacist-like characteristics without explicit identification)

This mirrors: nationalist/nationalistic, ideal/idealistic.

**Verdict:**
Lexically defensible if you need to mark degree or resemblance rather than identity. Unnecessary if you're describing explicit supremacism directly. The word isn't established in standard dictionaries but follows productive English morphology.
