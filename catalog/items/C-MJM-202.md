### Claude · 2025-08-21 19:14

Actually, "nor" isn't quite right there. You'd want "or" instead.

The issue is that "nor" is typically used after "neither" or in a series where you've already established a negative. Here you have "no matter how" which isn't creating that negative parallel structure that "nor" requires.

So it should be:
"...no matter how indefensible they seem at the time **or** how vulnerable it makes me feel."

The "or" flows more naturally and is grammatically correct in this context.

### Claude · 2025-08-21 20:49

A dog kennel can refer to several things:

**Physical structures:**

* A small shelter or house for a dog, usually outdoors
* A cage or enclosure for containing a dog
* A crate used for training or transport

**Businesses:**

* A boarding facility where dogs stay when owners are away
* A breeding facility for dogs
* Sometimes called a "kennel club" (though that's more about dog shows/breeding organizations)

The word comes from Old French "chenil" and originally meant a shelter for dogs. In everyday usage, people might say "put the dog in his kennel" (referring to a crate), or "we're boarding our dog at a kennel" (referring to a facility).

### Claude · 2025-08-21 20:49

Yes, exactly! In many cases, a kennel is just a crate - a wire or plastic box barely big enough for the dog to stand up and turn around in.

When people say "kennel training" or "put the dog in the kennel," they're often talking about what's essentially a cage. Even commercial boarding kennels frequently use rows of small concrete and chain-link enclosures that aren't much more spacious than large crates.

### Millard · 2025-08-21 21:02

none have or none has?

### Claude · 2025-08-21 21:02

It depends on what "none" is referring to:

**"None has"** - when "none" means "not one" (singular)

* "None of the dogs has been fed yet."
* "None of the evidence has surfaced."

**"None have"** - when "none" refers to multiple things (plural)

* "None of the dogs have been fed yet."
* "None of my friends have called."

In modern English, both are acceptable, but there's a slight preference depending on context:

* If you're emphasizing "not a single one," use "has"
* If you're thinking of the group collectively, use "have"

For practical purposes, "none have" is more common in everyday speech and usually sounds more natural. Unless you're being very formal or specifically want to emphasize "not one," go with "have."

So: "None of the kennels have proper ventilation" would be the natural choice.
