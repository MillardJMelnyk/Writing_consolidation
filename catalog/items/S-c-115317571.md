We’ve been so fixated and obsessed on Law for so long, we can hardly think of any other way to run things, and not just governmentally. Policies and procedures, regulations, churches, businesses, fraternal organizations, academia, family rules… we’ve got the offspring of Law running all over us and coming out our ears.

So, you can guess what I think of “natural law”, “laws of nature”, “laws of science”, “laws of physics”, and the rest.

Show me a single person, anywhere, who recognized the possibility that “law” is nothing more than a metaphor erroneously affixed to real, observable patterns of behavior of physical phenomena and living beings, and show me any of those who did legitimate, intelligent work to rule that possibility out.

Dare ya.

I’m tempted myself to talk that way, sometimes. To me, though, it’s like saying that person didn’t choose to follow their own consistent routines, but that they’ve got no choice about it, because a “law governs them” to consistently, routinely behave that way.

Makes sense as a theoretical possibility. Make no sense to say it’s real and true when you’ve got shitall facts or evidence that show it’s the only explanation, or that it’s the one which best fits the facts.

But if pattern in life approaches the qualifications to be regarded as a “law”, this one surely does. I’ll call it:

**The “Law” of Freedom From Codependence”**

It is my observation throughout my 70 years that, when it comes to things that really, really matter to us—the things that religious folks sweat drops of blood praying for, the things we feel like we’ll die without—we don’t get them until we reach a point where we’re just fine whether we get them or not. *It’s so very weird.*

* You’ll be ready to do something when you’re just fine never doing it.
* You’ll be ready to get something you wanted when you’ll be just fine whether you get it or not.

In other words, “ask, and you shall receive” is absolutely true, but it won’t come true until we emerge from our *codependence on it.*

What differentiates codependence from simple kinds of dependence like interdependence?

If you’re codependent, when faced with the fact that you can’t rely on what or whom seems to be your only hope, to you it will look and feel like the end of the world. “I’ll *die* if… [*whatever*]!!!”

If you’re dependent in a normal, healthy way, then when faced with the fact that can’t rely on what or whom seems to be your only hope, you won’t feel like you’re gonna die because:

1) You know there might likely be other things you could rely on or other ways to meet the need which you can’t rely on others for anymore. It might not involve dependence at all.

2) You know that you can acquire the competence to deal with the need in completely different ways than you’re capable of now.

The sense I have, especially when it comes to the most important things, is that we’re not in a position to take care of what we want, navigate it and handle it properly, and be constructive instead of destructive, so long as we’re codependent.
