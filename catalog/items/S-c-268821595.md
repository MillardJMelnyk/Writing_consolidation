Bottom line, even anarchists are authority addicts. Codependents, in other words.

The secret/hidden premise underlying all philosophy -- anarchistic or otherwise -- is that the human psyche, when left to its own devices, will fail—so, we need some systematic rationalization that can be instituted as authoritative.

Many opt for "natural law" -- not because it makes a lick of sense, but because most of us simply cannot think outside the paradigm of rules that compel.

Since everyone demands rules, here are four that, were they adhered to, would realize every good thing any anarchist has aspired to:

> 1. Be honest. Uncomfortably honest. Daringly honest. Painfully honest.
>
> 2. Absolutely refuse to tolerate or accept anything less than peerness.
>
> 3. Prioritize – absolutely – people over "property", or even more to the point, persons over shit.
>
> 4. Logically and psychologically entailed by the other pillars, insist on a completely free flow of information.

Those rules are not philosophical. They're not religious. They're not even "moral".

They're just what we'd naturally do if we hadn't been mindfucked. They're what we naturally do with people we truly care about. They're natural and fundamental to humanness.

And they absolutely block inhumanness.

I've read philosophy and anarchist thought since college in the 70s. I've never understood why these issues were made so complicated.

They're not complicated.

Now I see the reason: codependence -- the addiction to authority.

Even anarchists haven’t kicked the habit.
