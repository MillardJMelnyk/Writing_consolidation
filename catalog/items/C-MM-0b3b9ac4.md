### Millard · 2026-10-19 13:35

Quickly and simply list the possible readings of the Greek of that statement

### Claude · 2026-10-19 13:35

1. **Literal end of the world:** the law stands until the physical universe ends, and "till all be fulfilled" means till all history is done.
2. **Idiom for permanence:** "till heaven and earth pass" just means "never," as in Luke 16:17, so the law stands unchanged indefinitely.
3. **Second clause controls:** the verb is *genētai* ("happen, come to pass," not *plēroō* as in v.17), so the law stands until everything it points to has happened, i.e., Jesus' work culminating at the cross.
4. **Covenantal heaven and earth:** "heaven and earth" is prophetic shorthand for the temple-covenant order (Isa 51:16, 65:17), which passed away in AD 70.
5. **Fulfilled, not ended:** the law's authority continues, but as Jesus interprets and completes it, not as a separate code.

### Millard · 2026-10-19 13:36

### Claude · 2026-10-19 13:37

Verse 18 has two "until" clauses, and this reading treats the first ("till heaven and earth pass") as an emphatic way of saying "certainly," while the second ("till all things come to pass") sets the actual expiry date. "All things" means everything the law and prophets anticipated (v.17 names both), so once that is accomplished the law has done its job and its term runs out. Supporters tie this to Jesus' "It is finished" (John 19:30) and Luke 24:44, where he says everything written about him in the law of Moses and the prophets had to be fulfilled, which lines up with Hebrews putting the change of covenant at his death.
