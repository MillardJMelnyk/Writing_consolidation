### Claude · 2025-09-03 17:12

Both terms exist, but **"enculturated"** is the more standard and widely used term in academic contexts.

**Enculturation** is the established anthropological/sociological term for the process by which individuals learn and internalize the culture they're born into - the norms, values, beliefs, and behaviors of their society.

**Inculturated** is less common and more specific to certain contexts (sometimes used in religious studies or when emphasizing the deliberate "inculcating" or instilling of specific beliefs).

So for your analysis of how racism and misogyny become psychologically embedded through cultural transmission, **"enculturated racism and misogyny"** would be the more precise terminology.

The word captures exactly what you're describing - these aren't just personal prejudices people develop, but systematic distortions that get transmitted through the normal process of cultural learning, making them seem natural and invisible to those who internalize them.
